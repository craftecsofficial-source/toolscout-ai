Clay is great for RevOps and growth teams who want to design their own prospecting and enrichment pipelines and are willing to learn a flexible, spreadsheet-like tool; skip it if you want a plug-and-play contact database with simple sequences, or if you need predictable flat monthly costs. It pulls data from first-party sources and a marketplace of 200+ providers, can try several providers in order for each field, and uses AI agents to research details no database lists.

Workflows are built on a visual canvas, or through a CLI and API with a coding agent, and can end in synced ad audiences, a built-in Sequencer for email, or a push to your CRM. The free plan is usable for testing but limited, so most real use requires a paid tier.

Cost is the main thing to model carefully. Clay meters two things: actions and data credits. Its pricing page also shows different numbers for the same plans in different sections, so confirm the exact quote at checkout.

Best for

Clay fits RevOps, growth, and GTM-engineering teams building targeted outbound, ABM, or CRM-enrichment workflows who want control over data sources and logic. It is NOT for teams wanting a simple all-in-one contact database with ready-made sequences, buyers who need a fixed predictable monthly price, or non-technical users unwilling to spend time learning the canvas.

Key features

01

Waterfall enrichment

Tries multiple data providers in sequence for each field so gaps get filled by whichever source has the data.

02

Claygent and account agents

AI agents research companies and people and return custom data points; an Inbound SDR agent handles inbound messaging.

03

Workflow orchestration

Build on a visual canvas, via CLI/API with a coding agent, or package logic into reusable Functions.

04

Signals

Track events such as job changes and promotions; available on paid plans, not Free.

05

Activation

Sync audiences to LinkedIn, Meta, and Google ads, send email with the native Sequencer, or expose data to reps via MCP.

06

CRM and warehouse sync

Auto-sync and enrichment of CRM records and data warehouse sync are listed on Growth and Enterprise.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Multi-provider waterfalls raise coverage compared with relying on one database
  • Agents can find non-standard details, such as specifics from a company's site
  • Unlimited seats on all plans means adding teammates does not raise the bill
  • Bring-your-own API keys on every plan lets you use existing provider contracts, consuming actions but not data credits
  • Many output paths (CRM, ads, Sequencer, MCP) keep enriched data in use

Cons

  • Costs depend on two meters (actions and data credits), and credit tiers can jump sharply, for example 6K to 50K data credits runs from $290 to $2,125 per month
  • The pricing page lists different prices for Launch and Growth in different sections, so verify at purchase
  • Unused actions reset each cycle; credit rollover is limited
  • Free plan caps tables at 200 rows and withholds phone, signals, and CRM sync
  • Complex workflows have a learning curve, and heavy reliance on one tool needs documentation
  • Data quality still depends on the underlying providers

Frequently asked questions

Does Clay have a free plan?

Yes. Free is $0 with 500 actions and 100 data credits per month, unlimited seats, and tables up to 200 rows. It excludes phone enrichment, signals, and CRM sync, and a 14-day trial is also offered.

How much does Clay cost?

Launch starts around $167/month and Growth around $446/month per the headline prices, but the FAQ on the same page says $185 and $495. Enterprise is custom. Confirm the final figure at checkout.

What are actions versus data credits?

Actions meter workflow steps, while data credits pay for data from providers (starting at $0.05 each, cheaper at scale). Using your own API keys consumes actions but no data credits.

How does Clay compare with Apollo?

Apollo is built around its own contact database with built-in engagement, while Clay orchestrates many providers and custom logic. Clay offers more flexibility; Apollo is simpler to start.

Do unused credits roll over?

Unused actions reset each cycle. Data credits can roll over in part (the page cites up to one month's credits or 15% of annual credits, with a differing FAQ figure), so check the terms.
