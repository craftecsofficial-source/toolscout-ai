Luma Dream Machine suits creative teams and solo professionals who want polished, camera-driven short video and images from several models in one workspace, and who are comfortable paying from $25 a month. Skip it if you want a lasting free tier or are making long, dialogue-heavy stories. Luma AI began with 3D capture and moved into generative video; Dream Machine is now part of Luma Agents, an infinite-canvas workspace where an agent helps plan, generate and organize images, video, audio and text. It runs Luma's own models (Ray3.14, Ray3.2 and Uni-1 are listed) and also routes to third-party models including Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, Nano Banana and GPT Image 2.

Best for

Who it is for: ad agencies, in-house brand teams and independent creators making product shots, social ads and trailers who want multiple models and reference assets in one place. Who it is NOT for: people who need a permanent free plan, anyone planning long narrative or dialogue-driven films, or buyers who need fixed per-clip costs.

Key features

01

Ray3 / Ray3.14 video generation

Luma's proprietary video diffusion models produce HDR-grade clips with coherent camera motion, lighting and physics, generated from text prompts or a source image.

02

Luma Agent creative canvas

An infinite, zoomable board where a multimodal agent plans, generates, and organizes image, video, audio and text assets in one continuous workflow instead of separate tools.

03

Master Reference Assets

Users can lock in characters, products, logos or brand elements as reference assets so the agent preserves visual consistency across variations, video generation and localization.

04

Multi-model orchestration

The platform routes requests across third-party and in-house models (Nano Banana, GPT Image 2, Seedream, Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, ElevenLabs audio) automatically based on the task.

05

Lip-sync and localization

Upload a character video and new audio track to sync lips to speech, including re-syncing for translated voiceovers in other languages.

06

Storyboard and multi-shot mode

Supports developing characters, story arcs and episode boards, useful for building short trailers or sequential branded content rather than single clips.

07

Bulk variation generation

Generates multiple output variations from a single prompt or asset for A/B testing ad creative, product angles, or campaign concepts.

08

Team workspaces and shared credits

Team and Enterprise tiers add shared credit pools, project organization, usage analytics and SSO for creative departments working across multiple accounts.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Strong camera control and photorealistic output are the tool's reputation, especially for product and lifestyle shots
  • One canvas covers image, video, audio and text so you switch tools less
  • Reference assets help keep a character, product or logo consistent across variations
  • Several third-party models are available alongside Luma's own
  • Prices are published clearly with a yearly discount of up to 20%

Cons

  • No free plan on the pricing page; the 'Try for free' option has unstated limits
  • Consistency across long multi-shot sequences still needs manual setup and iteration
  • Credit use varies by model, resolution and length, so per-clip cost is hard to predict
  • The agent-driven interface has a learning curve
  • Best for short polished clips, not long-form storytelling

Frequently asked questions

Is Luma Dream Machine free?

The pricing page lists no free plan. The site shows 'Try for free' links, but the amount and terms are not stated, so check on sign-up.

How much does it cost?

Plus is $30/mo ($25/mo yearly), Pro $90/mo ($75 yearly), Ultra $300/mo ($250 yearly). Team and Enterprise are contact sales.

Can I use the output commercially?

The pricing page lists commercial use as included on Plus, and the Pro and Ultra plans inherit it. Check Luma's terms for your specific use.

Which models does it use?

Luma's own Ray3.14, Ray3.2 and Uni-1, plus third-party models like Veo 3.1, Kling 3.0, Seedance 2.0, Nano Banana and GPT Image 2.

Does it support lip-sync?

Yes, the platform lists lip-sync and localization of video with new audio.
