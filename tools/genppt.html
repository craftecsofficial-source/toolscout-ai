GenPPT's core pitch is input flexibility: instead of starting every deck from a blank prompt, it can take a PDF, a Word doc, a spreadsheet, a set of images, an audio recording, a video file, or a YouTube link and turn it into a slide-by-slide outline for approval before generating the full deck. That outline-first step is a sensible guardrail against the common AI-deck problem of getting twenty confidently wrong slides back from one vague prompt, and the chat-based editing agent that follows lets you request redesigns, chart swaps, or format changes without leaving the tool. Output isn't limited to PowerPoint either — GenPPT also renders PDFs, PNGs, and social-ready formats like LinkedIn carousels and TikTok/Reels-style vertical video, which points the product at content repurposing as much as at boardroom decks.

Where GenPPT departs most from AiPPT and AutoPPT is in how it treats first contact with a new user. AiPPT gives newcomers a one-time allowance of 140 credits on its free plan, enough to actually generate and inspect a real deck before paying anything. AutoPPT similarly offers a free trial on top of its topic- and document-to-deck workflow (PDF, Word, Excel, PPT, and image uploads). GenPPT skips a free tier entirely: you hand over card details for a $1, 3-day trial, and if you don't cancel in that window you're billed the full plan price. That's a meaningfully higher-friction front door than either competitor uses, even though the trial does grant full studio access rather than a stripped-down demo.

In exchange, GenPPT's intake is broader than what AiPPT and AutoPPT publicly document. Neither of those two lists audio files, video files, or YouTube URLs as native inputs — their published pipelines center on documents, text, and images. If your actual use case is "I have a recorded webinar or a YouTube video and I want a deck out of it," GenPPT is doing something the other two aren't advertising. On raw editing generosity, GenPPT also compares well: both of its paid tiers include unlimited slide edits in the editor, whereas AiPPT's plans are structured around monthly credit pools that also fund the initial generation.

The honest verdict is that GenPPT is worth it for a fairly specific user: someone who already has source material beyond plain documents (recordings, videos, long YouTube explainers) and who generates decks often enough to make a $25/mo or $99/yr subscription pay for itself. If you mostly want to test an AI deck tool for free before spending anything, or you only ever start from a document or a topic prompt, AiPPT's free credit allowance or AutoPPT's free trial are the lower-commitment entry points, and GenPPT's paid-only trial becomes a real point against it rather than a rounding error.

Best for

GenPPT suits people converting recorded or video-based content — meeting recordings, webinars, YouTube videos, long-form audio — into a slide deck, a job AiPPT and AutoPPT don't clearly cover with their document-and-text-first intake. It's a weaker fit for someone who just wants a free way to try an AI deck generator, since both of those alternatives offer a genuine no-cost or free-credit entry point that GenPPT does not.

Key features

01

Broad input support

Beyond standard PDFs, Word docs, and spreadsheets, GenPPT accepts images, audio files, video files (including voice memos), YouTube video links, and web URLs, letting you generate a deck from recorded or spoken content rather than only from written material.

02

Outline-first workflow

Before slides are fully generated, GenPPT produces a slide-by-slide outline for you to review and approve, so you can correct structure or emphasis before the AI commits to full designs, layouts, and copy.

03

Conversational AI editing agent

After generation, a chat-based design agent handles follow-up requests — redesigning a slide, updating a chart, or adjusting formatting — without requiring manual rebuilds in a separate editor.

04

Multi-format export

Finished decks export as PowerPoint (.pptx) or PDF for traditional presenting, PNG for static sharing, or in social-native formats like LinkedIn carousels and TikTok/Reels vertical video for repurposing slide content as social posts.

05

Style presets

GenPPT offers a range of visual themes, including cinematic, dark mode, and editorial styles, to set the overall look of a deck before generation rather than requiring manual restyling afterward.

06

Unlimited in-editor revisions

Both paid plans include unlimited slide edits inside the editor, so refining wording, layout, or charts after generation doesn't draw down a separate usage allowance.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • GenPPT's willingness to ingest audio, video, and YouTube links is a genuine differentiator: turning a recorded meeting or a YouTube explainer into a slide outline isn't something AiPPT or AutoPPT's public feature lists claim to do, which makes GenPPT worth a look specifically for repurposing recorded content rather than starting from a blank prompt.
  • Because slide edits are unlimited rather than credit-metered, you can iterate on wording, charts, and layout inside the editor without worrying about burning through an allowance the way you might on a credit-based plan like AiPPT's.
  • The outline-approval step before full slide generation is a small but meaningful safeguard against the classic AI-deck failure mode of getting twenty polished slides back that miss the brief entirely.
  • Watermark-free export is included from the first paid tier, so there's no separate upsell wall blocking a usable, presentable file once you've paid for a plan.

Cons

  • The absence of a genuine free plan is the biggest practical difference from AiPPT, which gives new users a one-time 140-credit allowance to actually build and export a deck before paying anything; GenPPT instead asks for a card and $1 upfront for a 3-day window, which is a real barrier for someone who just wants to kick the tires.
  • At $25/mo for month-to-month billing, GenPPT costs more than budget-oriented alternatives unless you're willing to prepay for the year at $99, so the effective price depends heavily on how confident you are before committing.
  • The 20-decks-per-month ceiling is generous for individual use but could be tight for an agency or content team producing decks daily across multiple clients or campaigns.
  • Because the trial auto-converts to a paid subscription, anyone who forgets to cancel within three days is billed the full plan price, which is worth double-checking before signing up.

Frequently asked questions

Does GenPPT have a free plan?

No. GenPPT doesn't offer an ongoing free tier; it runs a $1, 3-day trial that requires a credit card and automatically converts into the monthly ($25/mo) or annual (~$8/mo, billed at $99/yr) plan if you don't cancel in time.

What file types can I feed into GenPPT?

GenPPT accepts PDFs, Word documents, PowerPoint files, Excel/CSV spreadsheets, images, audio and video files (including voice memos), YouTube video links, and web URLs, in addition to plain text notes.

How much does GenPPT cost?

$25 per month billed monthly, or $99 per year (about $8/month) billed annually, after the $1, 3-day trial. Both paid tiers include 20 decks per month and unlimited slide edits.

How is GenPPT different from AiPPT?

AiPPT gives new users a free, one-time credit allowance (140 credits) to build a deck before paying, while GenPPT requires a paid $1 trial upfront. In exchange, GenPPT accepts a wider range of source material, including video, audio, and YouTube links, that AiPPT's document-and-text intake doesn't advertise.

How does GenPPT compare to AutoPPT?

AutoPPT focuses on quick topic-to-deck and document-to-deck generation (PDF, Word, Excel, PPT, images) with a free trial and no stated video or audio ingestion, while GenPPT adds an outline-approval step and can turn recorded audio, video, or a YouTube link directly into a deck.

Can I export a GenPPT deck without a watermark?

Yes. Both the monthly and annual paid plans include watermark-free export to PowerPoint and PDF.

What happens if I don't cancel the GenPPT trial?

The $1, 3-day trial requires a card, and if you don't cancel within that window, it automatically bills you at the plan's regular price ($25/mo or $99/yr, depending on what you selected).
