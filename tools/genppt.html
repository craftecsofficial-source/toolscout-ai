GenPPT is a strong pick if you need to turn recordings, YouTube videos or long documents into decks and are happy to pay monthly; skip it if you want a free plan to experiment with. It takes a source (PDF, Word, PowerPoint, spreadsheet, images, audio or video, a YouTube link, a web URL or pasted notes), produces a slide-by-slide outline for you to edit and approve, then generates designed slides.

After generation, a chat-based design agent handles follow-up requests such as restyling a slide or updating a chart. Output goes to PowerPoint or PDF, PNG images, and aspect ratios for social use (16:9, 1:1, 9:16), including LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok/Reels formats.

Pricing is simple but front-loaded: there is no free plan, the trial costs $1 for 3 days with a card, and it then bills $25 monthly or $99 yearly. Both paid plans include 20 full-slide decks a month and unlimited slide edits. We did not compare competitors' pricing or input support in detail today, so check rivals if input flexibility isn't your priority.

Best for

People converting recorded or video-based content (meetings, webinars, YouTube explainers) or long documents into decks, and who generate decks often enough to justify a subscription. Not for anyone wanting a free tier to try first, people producing more than 20 decks a month, or those who only need a quick deck from a topic prompt.

Key features

01

Broad input support

Accepts PDFs (including scanned), Word, PowerPoint, Excel/CSV, images, MP3/MP4/WAV and voice memos, YouTube URLs, web URLs and pasted notes. Google Docs must be exported to PDF or .docx first.

02

Outline-first workflow

You review and approve a slide-by-slide outline before the full slides are generated.

03

Conversational editing agent

A chat agent handles edits such as restyling a slide or updating a chart.

04

Multi-format export

PowerPoint, PDF and PNG, in 16:9, 1:1 and 9:16, with social presets for LinkedIn, Instagram and TikTok/Reels. Google Slides works by opening the exported .pptx.

05

Unlimited slide edits

Editing inside the editor isn't metered on either paid plan.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Audio, video and YouTube inputs make it practical for repurposing webinars and meeting recordings, which many deck tools don't support
  • Unlimited slide edits mean you can iterate without watching a usage meter
  • The outline-approval step catches off-brief structure before you get a full deck back
  • Paid exports carry no watermark, so the file is usable immediately

Cons

  • There is no free plan, only a paid $1 trial that needs a card, which is a real barrier for casual testing
  • Month-to-month at $25 is expensive; the $99 annual price needs more up-front confidence
  • The 20-deck monthly cap can bind agencies or content teams
  • Forgetting to cancel within 3 days means being billed the full plan price
  • We could not independently assess output quality or review-site ratings

Frequently asked questions

Does GenPPT have a free plan?

No. The pricing page states there is no free plan. You can start a $1, 3-day trial with a card.

What can I upload?

PDFs, Word, PowerPoint, Excel/CSV, images, audio and video files, YouTube links, web URLs and pasted notes.

How much does it cost after the trial?

$25 per month, or $99 per year (about $8/month). Both include 20 decks per month and unlimited slide edits.

Are exports watermarked?

No. Both paid plans export PowerPoint and PDF without a watermark.

What happens if I don't cancel the trial?

It converts to the plan you picked and bills the regular price. Cancelling within the 3 days avoids the plan charge.
