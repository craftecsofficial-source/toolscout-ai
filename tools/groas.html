An autonomous AI system that runs your Google Ads account instead of a human PPC manager

groas positions itself as a replacement for the human PPC manager: instead of a person checking bids once a day, its model-driven system reads auction and search-intent signals continuously and adjusts bids, budgets, keywords, ad copy and even landing pages in near-real time. The pitch — built on a claim of being 'trained on $500B+ in historical ad spend' — is compelling on paper, but that figure is a vendor claim with no independent audit trail available, so it's worth treating as marketing shorthand for 'a lot of data' rather than a verifiable benchmark. What is verifiable is the pricing and scope: $999/month for Google Ads automation alone, or $2,000/month bundled with a newer 'Earned Search' product that optimizes content for AI answer engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity, all on month-to-month terms with a genuine 7-day trial run on a live account.

The honest question for a buyer is not whether groas works, but whether it's the right shape of tool. Compared with a generalist suite like Semrush, groas is a narrow specialist. Semrush gives you keyword research, competitive intelligence, technical SEO auditing, and a wide lens across organic and paid channels — but its paid-search functionality stops at data and research; it does not autonomously set bids or rewrite ad copy for you. groas does exactly one execution job (running the Google Ads account itself, hands-off) and does it for a real monthly dollar cost most very small businesses can't justify. If what you need is research, rank tracking, and a broad marketing toolkit across SEO and content, Semrush is the better fit; if you specifically want the account run for you autonomously, and you're already spending enough on Google Ads to make a $999-$2,000/month fee proportionate, groas is the more direct answer.

groas is also worth placing next to ADFIXER, which solves the same underlying problem — autonomous, AI-driven paid-ad management — but for a completely different channel: Amazon Ads and DSP rather than Google Ads. A brand running both Google and Amazon ad spend at meaningful volume would likely need both tools rather than either alone, since neither platform reaches into the other's ad ecosystem; groas has no Amazon integration and ADFIXER has no Google Ads integration. Think of them as parallel, single-channel specialists rather than competitors.

Who should skip groas: businesses spending only a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars a month on Google Ads, where the $999+/month fee would exceed or rival the ad spend itself; anyone who runs paid campaigns primarily outside Google (Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn); and buyers who want full manual control over bidding decisions rather than delegating them to an opaque model. For everyone else — an established advertiser with real Google Ads budget who wants to stop paying a human agency a percentage fee — the flat-fee, always-on model is a legitimate and reasonably differentiated option.

Best for

A good fit for businesses or agencies with an established, meaningful Google Ads budget who want autonomous, 24/7 account management instead of a human PPC manager or a percentage-fee agency. A poor fit for small advertisers with modest ad budgets, anyone needing multi-channel (non-Google) paid ads management, or buyers who want hands-on manual control over bidding decisions.

Key features

01

Autonomous campaign build & launch

groas sets up Google Ads campaign structure, ad groups and targeting itself rather than requiring a human to configure the account from scratch.

02

Continuous bid & budget optimization

Bids and budget allocation are adjusted on an ongoing basis using purpose-built models, rather than the once-a-day checks typical of human account management.

03

AI ad copy generation

Ad creative and copy variants are generated and tested by the system to match search intent for each keyword group.

04

Dynamic landing page adaptation

Landing pages can be adapted to better match the intent behind the specific search query that triggered the ad, aiming to lift conversion rate.

05

Waste and negative-keyword blocking

The system identifies and blocks keywords and queries that are burning budget without producing qualified traffic.

06

Plain-language reporting with logged reasoning

Performance is reported in accessible language, and groas states that every automated action is logged along with its stated rationale.

07

Named account oversight

Despite the automation, accounts get an assigned account manager or strategist and defined guardrails, plus onboarding and periodic strategy calls.

08

Earned Search (AI answer-engine optimization)

A separate, newer product that analyzes how AI systems like ChatGPT and Perplexity choose citations, then produces content and fixes technical SEO to earn visibility in AI-generated answers.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The flat monthly fee structure removes the perverse incentive built into percentage-of-spend agency billing, where an agency can get paid more simply by having you spend more, regardless of results.
  • A trial run on the advertiser's real, live account (rather than a sandboxed demo) gives a genuinely useful signal on whether the automation performs before committing money, which is more honest than most 'book a demo' SaaS trials.
  • Logging the reasoning behind each automated action provides at least partial auditability, letting a skeptical advertiser check whether decisions make sense rather than trusting a total black box.
  • The white-label agency angle is a real differentiator versus doing manual PPC in-house at an agency — it turns a labor-intensive service line into a resellable, largely automated one, provided the agency's margins survive groas's aggregate-spend pricing.

Cons

  • Single-channel scope is a real structural limitation, not just a marketing simplification: any brand that also runs Meta, Microsoft Ads, TikTok or Amazon paid campaigns will need separate tools for those, and groas won't unify reporting across them.
  • The $999-$2,000/month price floor means the tool only pencils out once Google Ads spend is meaningful; a business spending $1-2k/month on ads would be paying almost as much for the tool as for the ads themselves.
  • The '$500B trained' claim is unfalsifiable from the outside — there's no way for a prospective buyer to verify the dataset size, its recency, or how directly it maps to their specific vertical or geography.
  • Not publishing agency pricing forces a sales conversation before an agency can do basic cost-benefit math, which adds friction compared to competitors that post transparent tiers.
  • Ceding bid, budget and creative decisions to an autonomous system is a trust exercise — for high-stakes or heavily regulated advertisers, the loss of granular manual control may outweigh the efficiency gains.

Frequently asked questions

Is groas worth it for a small business?

Usually not — the $999-$2,000/month fee is hard to justify unless Google Ads spend is already in the five-figures-a-month range, since below that the tool's cost rivals or exceeds the ad budget itself.

Does groas manage ad platforms other than Google Ads?

Its core Paid Search product is Google Ads only. groas has added a separate 'Earned Search' product for AI-answer-engine visibility, but it does not manage Meta, Microsoft, LinkedIn or TikTok ads.

How is groas different from Semrush for paid search?

Semrush (see Semrush) provides paid-search research and competitive data as part of a broader SEO/marketing suite, but it doesn't execute or autonomously manage bids; groas is narrower but actually runs the Google Ads account end-to-end.

Can groas replace ADFIXER if I sell on Amazon?

No — ADFIXER automates Amazon Ads and DSP specifically, a channel groas doesn't touch. A seller running both Google and Amazon ads would likely need both tools rather than either alone.

What does the free trial actually include?

groas runs the engine live on the advertiser's actual Google Ads account for 7 days, logging every action with its reasoning, and delivers a full performance report at day 7 before any payment is required; canceling during the trial costs nothing.

Is the exact agency pricing public?

No. groas prices agency/white-label access individually based on the aggregate ad spend placed through the platform, and the company asks agencies to book a call rather than publishing a fixed price table, so any specific agency price range circulating online should be treated as unverified.
