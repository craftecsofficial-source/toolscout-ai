Neural Frames is a strong pick for musicians, labels and visual artists who want a finished, beat-synced music video from a song; skip it if you need general-purpose video generation or a cheap way to get 4K. You upload a track and Autopilot builds a full video, or you can work by hand in the frame-by-frame editor or the text-to-video editor that taps third-party models such as Kling, Seedance and Runway. Visuals respond to the audio, stems and lyrics are supported, and a Short-Form Studio makes vertical clips. All plans run on monthly credits, so the real cost depends on song length and the model you choose.

Best for

Good for independent musicians, DJs, labels and AI visual artists making music or lyric videos. Not for anyone needing general-purpose video generation unrelated to music, people who want 4K on the cheapest plan, or buyers who need a guaranteed refund.

Key features

01

Autopilot

Automatically generates a complete, character-consistent music video from an uploaded song in just two clicks, analyzing BPM and mood to drive visuals.

02

Frame-by-Frame Editor

Gives creators granular control over individual frames, aesthetics, and timing for hand-crafted animation sequences.

03

Text-to-Video Editor

A timeline-based editor that taps third-party video generation models including Kling, Seedance, and Runway for scene-level control.

04

Short-Form Studio

Purpose-built mode for generating vertical, social-optimized clips for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.

05

Stem Extraction & Audioreactive Effects

Splits uploaded tracks into stems and drives visual effects that pulse and shift with the music in real time.

06

Lyric & Lipsync Generation

Produces lyric videos and lipsync-matched vocal performance visuals directly from a song.

07

Upscaling on Export

Every video is intelligently upscaled at no extra cost, supporting 1080p on all plans and 4K on higher tiers.

08

AI Assistant

Offers smart prompt suggestions to speed up the creative process for less experienced users.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Visuals are driven by the music (tempo, stems, lyrics) rather than a silent clip with audio pasted on.
  • Autopilot gets from song to full video in a couple of clicks, with manual editors available when you want control.
  • Unused subscription credits roll over up to 300% of the monthly allocation while subscribed.
  • Failed generations never cost credits.
  • Extra credits can be bought any time, and every export is upscaled at no extra charge.

Cons

  • Knight ($39/mo) has no 4K and the vendor frames higher tiers as better for full Autopilot videos, nudging serious users up.
  • Cost per video varies with song length and chosen model, so you need their calculator to estimate it.
  • Unused credits expire when the subscription ends.
  • No blanket refund guarantee; only first-time users who made one project may be reviewed individually.
  • We could not verify what the free plan includes, and did not check third-party review scores today.

Frequently asked questions

What does it cost?

Knight is $39/mo ($26/mo billed annually), Ninja $99/mo ($66/mo annually), Nirvana $299/mo ($199/mo annually), per the pricing page today.

Is there a free plan?

The site invites you to start free, but the free plan's limits were not shown, so check the pricing page before relying on it.

Do credits roll over?

Yes while you stay subscribed, up to 300% of the monthly allocation. Unused credits expire when the subscription ends.

Can I get a refund?

There is no blanket refund guarantee. First-time subscribers who made only one project and are unhappy can contact support for an individual review.

Which plan gets 4K?

Ninja and Nirvana include 4K upscaling; Knight is limited to 1080p.
