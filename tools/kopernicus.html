Kopernicus is a good fit for procurement and category teams at larger companies who want data-backed vendor shortlisting, especially when suppliers are private firms. Skip it if you need a mature e-sourcing or spend-analytics suite with fully published pricing, because this is an early, beta-labelled product.

You ask questions in a chat interface and it returns benchmarked reports on vendors and market niches, drawing on a licensed S&P Global dataset that the company says covers about 3 million private companies and all public ones. Its central feature is the KOFO Resiliency Score, a proprietary 0-10,000 number built from growth, profitability, management efficiency and liquidity; the validation method is described only in marketing terms. Reports update automatically, and the site cites a design partnership with MassMutual and membership of the MassChallenge 2025 FinTech cohort.

Best for

Best for procurement leads, CPOs and category managers at mid-size to large companies who want to speed up vendor research and need financial visibility into private suppliers, and who are comfortable piloting an early-stage vendor. Not for small teams or solo buyers wanting a cheap, self-serve tool with a published price, nor for teams that need e-sourcing workflow, contract management or spend analytics rather than vendor financial benchmarking. Also skip it if your organization requires a long-established vendor with a large reference customer base.

Key features

01

AI chat vendor discovery

Query the dataset conversationally to surface and shortlist vendors. The company claims this saves 30+ hours per sourcing event; that figure is unverified.

02

KOFO Resiliency Score

A proprietary 0-10,000 score of a company's long-term competitive strength across growth, profitability, efficiency and liquidity. The site also calls it Koformance or K Score.

03

Public and private company data

Licensed S&P Global data on roughly 3 million private companies and all global public companies. Private-company financials are a paid module or Enterprise feature.

04

Peer groups and market niches

Creator users can build and edit peer groups across 100,000+ market niches to benchmark vendors against a chosen competitive set.

05

Auto-updating reports

Industry, segmented and niche reports refresh as data changes. Reader gets 5 industry reports, Creator 20 segmented and 120 niche reports.

06

Growth projections

The Niche Projections module adds forward-looking growth projections for 100,000+ niches, built on AI trained on analyst consensus.

07

Report sharing

Reports can be shared across the organization; the free Reader tier exists so colleagues can view reports made by paid users.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Dataset breadth (roughly 3 million private companies plus global public companies, per the vendor) gives visibility into private suppliers many procurement tools skip.
  • A numeric score alongside AI-written explanation gives you something concrete to show stakeholders when defending a vendor shortlist.
  • Custom peer groups across 100,000+ niches let you benchmark against a comparable set instead of a generic industry code.
  • The free Reader tier lets colleagues consume reports without buying seats, and a 30-day money-back guarantee is advertised on paid subscriptions.
  • Reports auto-update, which reduces the manual re-research that makes supplier monitoring go stale.
  • Discounts are offered on request to startups, students, non-profits and minority-owned businesses.

Cons

  • The company is early-stage and runs a labelled beta program, so plan structure, limits and prices may change.
  • The Creator plan, the core paid tier, has no published price; you must book a demo to learn the cost.
  • The most distinctive capabilities (private-company data, unlimited reports, growth projections) are $449/user/month modules or Enterprise-only, so real cost can far exceed the base plan once the free year ends.
  • Public proof of production customers is thin: the site highlights a MassMutual design partnership and an accelerator cohort rather than a broad customer base.
  • The KOFO score methodology is described only at marketing level, with no independent validation we could find, so treat it as one input rather than a verdict.
  • The pricing page showed placeholder (lorem ipsum) text in some FAQ answers when checked, and the 30-day guarantee's terms were not displayed, which suggests an unfinished buying experience.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Kopernicus cost?

Reader is free. Creator is billed per user annually but its price is not published; Enterprise is custom-quoted. Optional modules are listed at $449 per user per month, free for the first 12 months under the beta. Ask sales what you will pay once the free year ends.

What is the KOFO score and can I trust it?

It is Kopernicus's proprietary 0-10,000 score of a company's competitive strength based on growth, profitability, efficiency and liquidity. The methodology is only described at a high level and we found no independent validation, so use it to shortlist and then verify with your own diligence.

Is there a trial or refund?

The pricing page advertises a 30-day money-back guarantee on subscriptions, but the referenced terms were not visible. Confirm the conditions in writing before purchase. The free Reader tier only lets you view reports.

Where does the data come from?

The company says it licenses data from S&P Global covering about 3 million private companies and all global public companies. Private-company data is not in the base Creator plan; it is a paid module or Enterprise feature.

Is it ready for enterprise procurement?

It is labelled beta and the company is young. Ask for reference customers, data-continuity commitments and security documentation before rollout.
