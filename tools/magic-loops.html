Magic Loops is a good fit for individuals who want to prototype a small, single-purpose AI automation quickly, and a poor fit for teams needing production backends, persistent state or retrieval-based agents. A Loop pairs an LLM call with a little code or logic, and you can start by forking one of the community Loops in the public marketplace. The site says it has served over 3.5 million Loop runs from 65,000 users, which are vendor figures we could not independently check. Usage is metered in credits, with separate generation and run credits on the free plan and a flat $1 per 1,000 credits on paid plans. Compared with BuildShip, which targets backend workflows, or Dify, which targets conversational and agent apps with retrieval, this is a lighter sketchpad for narrow tasks. We found no shutdown notice and the site looked active on 2026-11-03, with ChatSeed, Inc. named in the footer.

Best for

Indie hackers, students, creators and non-technical marketers building narrow utilities such as summarizers, generators or small games, who are fine with a marketplace culture. Not for teams needing private data pipelines, multi-turn agents with knowledge bases, or production backends; free-plan Loops are also public, so anything proprietary needs a paid plan.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Going from an idea to a running AI automation takes minutes.
  • The marketplace works as a template library: forking a close Loop is often quicker than starting blank.
  • The free plan is enough to build and run a few Loops before paying.
  • Every paid plan uses the same $1 per 1,000 credits rate, so cost scaling is easy to reason about.

Cons

  • It is built for self-contained automations, not production backends with databases or complex API orchestration.
  • Generation and run credits are tracked separately on the free plan, which adds mental overhead.
  • No retrieval or knowledge-base features were described on the pages we checked, so document-grounded agents are out of scope.
  • Adoption numbers (3.5 million runs, 65,000 users) come from the vendor, and we did not verify third-party reviews today.
  • The jump from Developer ($50, 50,000 credits) to Startup ($300, 300,000 credits) leaves little middle ground.

Frequently asked questions

What is a Loop?

A small automation that combines an LLM call with code or logic, for example summarizing text and formatting the output. It is the platform's single building block.

Is Magic Loops free?

There is a free plan with 250 daily generation credits and 500 monthly run credits, limited to public Loops and community support.

How is it priced?

Paid tiers are Hobby $9, Professional $20, Developer $50 and Startup $300 per month, all at $1 per 1,000 credits. Enterprise is custom.

How does it compare to BuildShip?

BuildShip is geared to backend work like APIs and scheduled jobs. Magic Loops suits quick, small LLM-plus-code utilities.

How does it compare to Dify?

Dify targets full conversational and agent apps with retrieval and self-hosting. Magic Loops is lighter and narrower.

Is it suitable for enterprise use?

An Enterprise tier lists SOC 2 and on-premise hosting, but we could not verify its track record; test it before relying on it for critical work.
