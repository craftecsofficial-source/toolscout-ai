Geordy suits teams who want a cheap, no-rebuild way to give AI crawlers a cleaner copy of their site; skip it if you want to know what ChatGPT or Perplexity say about your brand, or need content written. It does not monitor citations or create content, so on its own you cannot tell whether it helped.

You give it a domain or sitemap and it generates plain machine-readable versions of each page (eight file types, sixteen on the top plan), hosted on a subdomain it manages. You add one block of link tags to your page head and a DNS record, and visitors see no change. A bot-analytics view logs which AI crawlers fetched which files and when, and a format editor lets you correct generated output by hand.

In practice it is infrastructure rather than a reporting tool. Pair it with a monitoring product such as Scrunch AI or AthenaHQ if you want evidence that crawlers reading these files translates into being cited. Pricing is based on file counts, so large sites climb tiers quickly. We could not find independent user reviews to back its claims.

Best for

Good for marketing and SEO teams and agencies who believe their content is fine but suspect AI crawlers struggle to parse the site, and who want a fix that needs little developer time. Not for teams that need to see how AI engines describe or cite their brand, teams that need new content written, or large sites with tight budgets, since file-based pricing grows with page count.

Key features

01

Format engine

Scans a URL or sitemap and writes eight machine-readable file types per page automatically (sixteen on Growth), then keeps them updated.

02

Bot analytics

Shows which AI engines open the generated files, which pages they read and when; the Pro and Business tiers add advanced and full reporting.

03

Schema graph

Maps how your brand, products, locations and main pages relate to each other.

04

Format editor

Lets you edit generated files by hand, or have Geordy regenerate them all from your sitemap.

05

Simple setup

Add the domain or sitemap, let Geordy write the files, then paste one block of link tags into the page head. Nothing is installed on the site.

06

Multiple domains

Every plan allows any number of domains, each with its own custom subdomain, sharing one monthly file allowance.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Setup is light: a DNS record and link tags, with no CMS rebuild and no script added to visitor-facing pages, so it rarely needs a dev ticket.
  • The free plan is genuinely usable for a trial: 40 files a month covers roughly 8 pages, and you can attach multiple domains.
  • Bot analytics exposes which AI crawlers fetch which files, a gap in most standard analytics setups.
  • When the file limit is reached, existing files stay live; only new builds and refreshes pause, so nothing breaks.

Cons

  • It only handles machine-readability. It does not measure whether you are cited or mentioned by AI engines, so you need another tool to judge impact.
  • Pricing counts files, not pages: every generated format and every refresh uses allowance, unused files do not roll over, and page estimates assume only 5 formats refreshed monthly. Refreshing more often or generating all 8 formats means fewer pages per plan.
  • The jump from Business ($319/mo annual) to Growth ($1,999/mo annual) is steep, and the 16-format set and API sit only on Growth.
  • We found no independent reviews to verify that crawlers actually use these files or that citations improve.

Frequently asked questions

Does Geordy change what my visitors see?

No. Files are served from a separate subdomain and referenced by link tags in your page head; the live pages look the same.

Can Geordy tell me if ChatGPT or Perplexity cite my site?

No. Bot analytics show which AI crawlers fetched which files, but it does not track citations or brand mentions. Use a visibility tracker for that.

How far does the free plan go?

40 files a month, about 8 pages at 5 formats refreshed monthly, with bot analytics, multiple domains and community support. No card is required.

What happens if I use up my monthly files?

New builds and refreshes pause until the period resets, but files already generated stay live. Unused files do not roll over.

Is there any proof AI engines use these files?

We could not verify that. llms.txt in particular is a proposed convention, not a confirmed ranking or citation factor, so treat gains as unproven and test on a few pages first.
