PageLens.ai suits small and mid-size marketing teams who want AI-visibility tracking and the content to fix gaps in one tool; it is a poor fit if you only want a cheap monitoring dashboard or need a long-established vendor. It runs buyer prompts daily against AI engines, shows how they describe your brand and competitors, traces which sources they cite, and its content engine writes pages and publishes them to your own domain after you approve them. Plans on the live pricing page run from $299/mo (Launch) to $699/mo (Growth) and from $1,499/mo (Enterprise); engine coverage grows with the tier. The product is young, and its only public praise is testimonials on its own site.

Best for

Small to mid-size marketing teams and agencies that want tracking plus content production, and are comfortable adopting a newer vendor. A free audit lets you test it first. NOT for: buyers who only need low-cost monitoring (the entry plan is $299/mo), teams that need Claude or Copilot tracking below enterprise pricing, or organisations that require a long public review record, security paperwork or multi-year track record up front.

Key features

01

AI visibility audit

A free check of how AI engines currently describe your brand (about 30 seconds per the site), plus other free tools such as an AI crawler checker and AI Overview checker.

02

Daily prompt tracking

Runs 100 visibility prompts a day on Launch and Growth across 3 engines (ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Perplexity) or 5 (adds Gemini and Grok); Enterprise covers all 7 including Copilot and Claude.

03

Share of voice and sentiment

Compares how often you versus up to 10 competitors per category are recommended, and shows the adjectives models use about your brand.

04

Citation and traffic analytics

Shows which sources engines cite, human sessions, and AI crawler activity such as GPTBot and ClaudeBot.

05

Content engine

Writes blogs, pillar and comparison pages, news and social content and publishes to your domain after approval. Launch is self-serve (25 pieces/month); Growth is done-for-you (50 pieces/month).

06

Outreach agent and integrations

On Growth and Enterprise: drafts researched pitches to sites AI engines already cite (with your approval), web 2.0 publishing, and MCP/API access.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The content engine drafts and publishes pages to your own domain after approval, so you get output and not only a dashboard.
  • Publishing under your own domain means the URLs and SEO value stay with you if you leave (per the vendor).
  • Plans and prices are public, and a free audit lets you see real results before paying.
  • Unlimited users on all plans, and Growth includes a done-for-you content service rather than only software.
  • Analytics cover AI crawler traffic and human sessions as well as citations, which ties visibility to traffic.

Cons

  • The cheapest paid plan is $299/mo, so it is a larger commitment than monitoring-only AEO tools.
  • Launch tracks only 3 engines; Growth adds Gemini and Grok, and Claude and Copilot are Enterprise (Launch lists them as add-ons with no price shown).
  • Extra countries cost from $25/mo per engine, which can add up for multi-market brands.
  • No G2 or Capterra rating was found; the testimonials are published on PageLens's own site and cannot be independently checked.
  • It is a young product from a small team, so longevity and roadmap depth are unproven versus larger funded competitors.

Frequently asked questions

How much does PageLens.ai cost?

Launch is $299/mo, Growth is $699/mo, and Enterprise starts at $1,499/mo. Annual billing gives 2 months free. Prices are USD and exclude GST.

Is there a free plan?

No free subscription is listed, but there is a free AI visibility audit and several free checker tools.

Which AI engines are tracked on each plan?

Launch: ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Perplexity. Growth adds Gemini and Grok. Enterprise covers all seven, including Copilot and Claude.

What is the difference between Launch and Growth?

Launch gives 25 content pieces a month that you run yourself; Growth gives 50 done-for-you pieces, a weekly action brief handled by PageLens, the outreach agent, MCP/API and web 2.0 publishing.

Where does the content get published?

To your own domain via a CMS connection, after you approve it. Check the exact setup with PageLens before relying on it.
