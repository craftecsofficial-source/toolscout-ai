Seeing AI is a free iOS app built by Microsoft as an ongoing research project designed with and for the blind and low-vision community. It uses AI-powered computer vision to narrate the visual world in real time: pointing a phone's camera at text, a scene, a product, or a person triggers spoken descriptions delivered through the phone's screen reader and audio channels. The app is organized around task-specific "channels" — Read, Describe, Products, People, Currency, Colors, Light, and a spatial-audio World mode for LiDAR-equipped devices — each optimized for a specific daily task like reading mail, identifying canned goods, or recognizing a friend's face.

Microsoft has continuously expanded Seeing AI since its original release, adding capabilities such as rich image descriptions with follow-up questions, document Q&A, multi-page document scanning, handwriting recognition in multiple languages, video description, PDF recognition, and "Find My Things" — a feature letting users teach the app to recognize personal objects using an audio-first training flow. The app is developed by Microsoft Corporation, listed in the Productivity category on the App Store, and localized in 19 languages. It differentiates itself from competitors like Envision AI or Supersense by being entirely free with no subscription tiers, reflecting Microsoft's positioning of it as an accessibility research and goodwill project rather than a commercial product.

Best for

Seeing AI is ideal for blind and low-vision users who want a free, all-in-one AI visual assistant for reading, scene description, product identification, and basic indoor navigation on iPhone or iPad. It is not designed for sighted productivity users or business workflow automation — its feature set and Microsoft's stated mission are specifically focused on accessibility for the blind and low-vision community.

Key features

01

Read

Combines short text, document, and handwriting recognition into one channel that reads printed or handwritten text aloud as soon as it's in view, preserving original formatting.

02

Describe

Takes a photo and returns a rich AI-generated description of the scene or people in it; users can ask follow-up questions about specific details.

03

Products

Scans barcodes and accessible QR codes with audio guidance to identify products and surface package information.

04

People

Recognizes saved contacts and estimates age, gender, and expression for people in view or in photos.

05

World

An audio augmented-reality mode using Spatial Audio and LiDAR to announce nearby objects and support indoor navigation between saved routes.

06

Find My Things

Lets users teach the app to recognize personal objects through an audio-first training flow, then locate them later via audio cues.

07

Currency & Colors

Identifies currency notes and colors on demand for everyday tasks like sorting cash or matching clothing.

08

Cross-App Recognition

Users can share photos or videos from Mail, Photos, WhatsApp, and other apps directly into Seeing AI for description.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Completely free with every feature unlocked — no premium tier, subscription, or ads, which is unusual for an app of this feature depth.
  • Covers an unusually wide range of daily tasks in one app: reading, scene description, product scanning, people recognition, currency, and indoor navigation.
  • Backed by Microsoft's ongoing investment; the app has shipped major updates yearly since launch, including rich image descriptions, video description, and object-teaching via Find My Things.
  • App Store reviewers frequently describe it as one of the most useful accessibility apps available, with long-time users citing years of daily reliance on it.
  • Supports 19 languages and multiple platforms (iOS natively, with a Microsoft Store version for Windows), extending reach beyond a single ecosystem.

Cons

  • Holds a 4.3 out of 5 rating from 627 App Store ratings, reflecting a solid but not top-tier consumer score with a meaningful minority of critical reviews.
  • Users report recurring bugs, such as settings and channel ordering resetting between app launches, forcing repeated reconfiguration.
  • The Short Text/Read channel is described by some long-term users as inconsistent when reading digital displays (e.g., kitchen scales), sometimes taking unusually long to return a result.
  • As a research-project app, feature requests (like expiration-date reading or wearable/glasses support) can take years to be addressed, if at all.

Frequently asked questions

Is Seeing AI free?

Yes, Seeing AI is completely free with no subscriptions, ads, or in-app purchases.

What platforms does Seeing AI support?

Seeing AI is available on iPhone and iPad via the App Store, with an app also listed on the Google Play Store for Android.

Who makes Seeing AI?

Seeing AI is developed by Microsoft Corporation as an ongoing accessibility research project.

What is the World channel?

World is an audio augmented-reality mode that uses Spatial Audio and, on LiDAR-equipped devices, announces nearby objects and supports saved indoor navigation routes.

Can Seeing AI read handwriting?

Yes, the Read channel includes handwriting recognition, supporting multiple languages including English, Japanese, and Korean.

What is Seeing AI's App Store rating?

As of the most recent listing, Seeing AI holds a 4.3 out of 5 rating from 627 ratings on the U.S. App Store.
