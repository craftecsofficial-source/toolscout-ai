Seeing AI is a strong first app for blind and low-vision people who want a free narrator for text, photos and products, and is not meant for sighted productivity or business automation. It is a Microsoft research project built with and for the blind community: you point your phone camera and it speaks what it sees, organized into channels such as Read, Describe, Products, People, Currency, Colors and a spatial-audio World mode. The official site lists it as free and available on the App Store and Google Play. Some capabilities, like World mode, depend on a LiDAR-capable iPhone.

Best for

Ideal for blind and low-vision users who want one free app for reading mail, identifying products and getting scene descriptions. Not for sighted users looking for productivity features, teams needing business workflow tools, or anyone needing human-assist or smart-glasses support, which the app does not offer.

Key features

01

Read

Combines short text, document, and handwriting recognition into one channel that reads printed or handwritten text aloud as soon as it's in view, preserving original formatting.

02

Describe

Takes a photo and returns a rich AI-generated description of the scene or people in it; users can ask follow-up questions about specific details.

03

Products

Scans barcodes and accessible QR codes with audio guidance to identify products and surface package information.

04

People

Recognizes saved contacts and estimates age, gender, and expression for people in view or in photos.

05

World

An audio augmented-reality mode using Spatial Audio and LiDAR to announce nearby objects and support indoor navigation between saved routes.

06

Find My Things

Lets users teach the app to recognize personal objects through an audio-first training flow, then locate them later via audio cues.

07

Currency & Colors

Identifies currency notes and colors on demand for everyday tasks like sorting cash or matching clothing.

08

Cross-App Recognition

Users can share photos or videos from Mail, Photos, WhatsApp, and other apps directly into Seeing AI for description.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Free, per the official site.
  • One app covers reading, scene description, product scanning, people and currency recognition.
  • Built by Microsoft with the blind and low-vision community.
  • Available on iOS and Android per the official site.

Cons

  • World mode and spatial navigation need LiDAR-equipped iPhones.
  • It is a research project, so feature requests may be slow to arrive.
  • Accuracy on tricky tasks such as digital displays varies; not verified by us today.
  • Handwriting and language coverage details could not be verified today.
