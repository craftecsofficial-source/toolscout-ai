Wordtune is great for people who write a lot of short text, such as emails and posts, and want quick rephrasing options; skip it if you need heavy grammar auditing or long-document editing. It is a rewriter first and a proofreader second.

You select a sentence and get several alternatives that change wording, tone or length. It also summarizes text and videos, can continue your writing, and checks grammar and spelling. It runs in a web editor, Chrome and Edge extensions, and an iOS app, and is made by AI21 Labs.

Best for

For: professionals, students and non-native English speakers polishing emails, posts and short documents. Not for: anyone needing rigorous grammar and style auditing, long-form structural editing, or a free tool with generous daily use.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Rewrite options are varied rather than one generic suggestion
  • Low price: Unlimited is $6.99/mo annually
  • Grammar and spelling checks are unlimited even on free
  • Works in browser and on iOS

Cons

  • Free tier rewrites cap at 10 per day, summaries at 3 per month
  • Advanced still limits rewrites to 30 per day, so frequent writers need Unlimited
  • Quality drops on long or structurally complex paragraphs
  • Grammar checking is lighter than dedicated checkers
  • A team plan was not found on the plans page checked today

Frequently asked questions

Is Wordtune free?

Yes: 10 rewrites/suggestions per day, 3 summaries per month and unlimited grammar and spelling checks.

How much is Wordtune Unlimited?

$9.99/mo monthly or $6.99/mo billed annually. Advanced is $6.99/mo or $4.89/mo annually.

Does it have a team plan?

The plans page checked on 2026-10-16 listed only Basic, Advanced and Unlimited; ask the vendor about business plans.

Wordtune or Grammarly?

Wordtune generates rewrite and tone options; Grammarly is stronger for error detection and style checking. Many people use both.

Who makes it?

AI21 Labs.
