Diffit is a good fit for K-12 teachers who regularly need one topic or text adapted for mixed reading levels, language needs or intervention tiers. Skip it if you want a full curriculum or lesson-planning system, or if you will not review AI output before it reaches students. You enter a topic, standard or pasted content, optionally set grade level, MTSS tier, challenge level, language and scaffolds, and Diffit produces activities, station rotations, sub plans, intervention materials and slides that you can then edit. Results print or export to Google Docs, Slides, Forms and Classroom, or Microsoft 365. The site says students do not need logins and that it collects no student data. The free Basic plan is genuinely usable, but exports to Google/Microsoft, standards alignment and anything older than 90 days sit behind a paid plan: $14.99/mo or $149.99/yr for an individual, or a school or district license.

Best for

K-12 teachers, instructional coaches and intervention staff who differentiate materials weekly, plus schools wanting a shared license. Not for those needing a full curriculum or lesson-planning suite, teachers unwilling to review and edit AI output, or anyone who needs published per-student school pricing without contacting Diffit.

Key features

01

Topic-to-resource generation

Enter a topic, standard, or paste in existing content, and Diffit produces a leveled reading passage, questions, and supporting materials without manual rewriting.

02

MTSS tier and challenge-level controls

Outputs can be tuned to specific support tiers or difficulty levels so the same underlying content serves students at different points in a classroom.

03

Multilingual output

Materials can be generated in multiple languages, supporting English learners and multilingual classrooms without a separate translation step.

04

Station rotation and sub plan builders

Beyond single worksheets, Diffit assembles multi-activity formats like station rotations and full substitute lesson plans from the same source content.

05

Intervention material generation

Creates targeted practice and support resources aligned to intervention needs, useful for RTI/MTSS workflows and small-group instruction.

06

Standards alignment

Generated resources can be tied to specific academic standards, helping teachers document how materials map to curriculum requirements.

07

Multi-platform export

Finished resources export to Google Docs, Slides, Forms, and Classroom, or to Microsoft 365, and remain editable after export rather than locking content into a fixed template.

08

Admin dashboard (school/district plans)

Paid school accounts include an administrative view for managing usage and licensing across a building or district.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Collapses a task that can take a teacher an hour or more of manual rewriting into a few minutes of setup, freeing planning time for instruction rather than material production.
  • Handles differentiation across several axes at once, tier, challenge level, and language, so one piece of content can serve a mixed-ability or multilingual classroom instead of requiring separate versions built by hand.
  • Purpose-built output formats like sub plans and station rotations map directly onto real classroom routines, rather than leaving teachers to adapt generic AI text into a usable format themselves.
  • Native export into Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 means resources arrive editable in the tools schools already standardize on, avoiding copy-paste reformatting.
  • A genuinely usable free tier lets an individual teacher validate the workflow before a school or district commits to a paid license.
  • Stated zero student-data-collection posture and third-party privacy endorsements matter to school IT and privacy officers evaluating classroom AI tools.

Cons

  • Free-plan resources older than 90 days become inaccessible, so export or upgrade before then.
  • Google/Microsoft export, standards alignment and full history require a paid plan.
  • School per-enrollment prices were not visible on the pages checked; budget planning needs a quote or the school pricing tool.
  • Individual plans are credit-card only and cannot be bought by schools on a purchase order.
  • AI output varies by topic and still needs teacher review for accuracy and grade level.
  • Narrowly focused on differentiated materials; not a curriculum or lesson-planning replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Is Diffit free?

The Basic plan is free for any subject, with PDF download, printing, sharing and editing, but only resources created in the last 90 days stay accessible.

How much does an individual teacher pay?

$14.99 per month or $149.99 per year (US price; may vary by country), by credit card from your own account. Schools cannot buy individual plans on a PO.

What does paying add over the free plan?

Export to Google Docs, Slides, Forms, Classroom and Microsoft; alignment to standards and MTSS tiers; full resource history; graphic organizers.

How is school pricing set?

A yearly flat rate by student enrollment tier; the exact figures were not shown on the pages checked. Districts request a quote.

Does it need student accounts?

The site says students do not need logins and no student data is collected.
