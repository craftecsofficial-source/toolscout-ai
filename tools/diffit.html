Diffit is a web-based AI tool built specifically for the mechanics of lesson differentiation: taking one topic, article, or standard and turning it into a set of leveled materials that fit a real classroom's spread of reading abilities, language needs, and support requirements. A teacher types in what they're teaching, or pastes in existing content, and Diffit returns text passages, comprehension questions, vocabulary support, and activity formats scaled to different tiers, challenge levels, or languages, which the teacher can then edit before sharing or printing.

What sets Diffit apart from a general-purpose AI writing assistant is its narrow focus on the workflows teachers actually repeat every week: building station rotations, assembling sub plans, generating intervention materials tied to MTSS tiers, and producing slides ready to present. Rather than requiring a teacher to prompt-engineer a chatbot into producing usable classroom output, Diffit wraps that process into structured inputs (grade level, tier, language, standard) and exports the result straight into Google or Microsoft formats teachers and students already use, positioning it as a time-saving productivity layer on top of everyday lesson planning rather than a general content generator.

Best for

Diffit is aimed at classroom teachers, special education and intervention staff, and instructional coaches in K-12 settings who regularly need to produce the same lesson at several difficulty levels or in multiple languages, as well as schools and districts looking to standardize how differentiation is done across a building. Compared to general AI writing tools, it trades flexibility for speed and structure, giving educators fewer decisions to make and outputs that are closer to classroom-ready on the first pass; compared to traditional resource marketplaces, it generates original, customized materials on demand instead of relying on a fixed library someone else wrote.

Key features

01

Topic-to-resource generation

Enter a topic, standard, or paste in existing content, and Diffit produces a leveled reading passage, questions, and supporting materials without manual rewriting.

02

MTSS tier and challenge-level controls

Outputs can be tuned to specific support tiers or difficulty levels so the same underlying content serves students at different points in a classroom.

03

Multilingual output

Materials can be generated in multiple languages, supporting English learners and multilingual classrooms without a separate translation step.

04

Station rotation and sub plan builders

Beyond single worksheets, Diffit assembles multi-activity formats like station rotations and full substitute lesson plans from the same source content.

05

Intervention material generation

Creates targeted practice and support resources aligned to intervention needs, useful for RTI/MTSS workflows and small-group instruction.

06

Standards alignment

Generated resources can be tied to specific academic standards, helping teachers document how materials map to curriculum requirements.

07

Multi-platform export

Finished resources export to Google Docs, Slides, Forms, and Classroom, or to Microsoft 365, and remain editable after export rather than locking content into a fixed template.

08

Admin dashboard (school/district plans)

Paid school accounts include an administrative view for managing usage and licensing across a building or district.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Collapses a task that can take a teacher an hour or more of manual rewriting into a few minutes of setup, freeing planning time for instruction rather than material production.
  • Handles differentiation across several axes at once, tier, challenge level, and language, so one piece of content can serve a mixed-ability or multilingual classroom instead of requiring separate versions built by hand.
  • Purpose-built output formats like sub plans and station rotations map directly onto real classroom routines, rather than leaving teachers to adapt generic AI text into a usable format themselves.
  • Native export into Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 means resources arrive editable in the tools schools already standardize on, avoiding copy-paste reformatting.
  • A genuinely usable free tier lets an individual teacher validate the workflow before a school or district commits to a paid license.
  • Stated zero student-data-collection posture and third-party privacy endorsements matter to school IT and privacy officers evaluating classroom AI tools.

Cons

  • The free plan's roughly 90-day resource window means teachers who don't organize exports promptly can lose access to materials they built earlier in the year.
  • Neither individual-teacher pricing nor the exact school subscription cost is published on the main site; both require visiting a separate pricing tool or submitting a contact form, which adds friction for quick budget decisions.
  • Like any generative AI system, output quality and grade-level accuracy will vary by topic and still needs a teacher's review before use with students.
  • Value depends heavily on a school or district committing to the paid tier for full resource history and export integrations, so a solo free-tier user gets a narrower slice of the product.
  • Being narrowly focused on differentiated classroom materials, it isn't a substitute for general lesson-planning or curriculum-design tools that cover broader instructional planning needs.
  • District-scale adoption requires a custom sales conversation rather than self-serve checkout, which can slow procurement in larger organizations.

Frequently asked questions

What does Diffit actually generate for teachers?

Diffit turns a topic, standard, or existing piece of content into differentiated, classroom-ready materials such as leveled reading passages, comprehension activities, station rotations, sub plans, and intervention resources.

Is Diffit free to use?

Yes, Diffit offers a free Basic plan that lets teachers generate and export classroom content, though access is limited to recently created resources; full resource history and integrations require a paid Diffit for Schools plan.

Can Diffit create materials for English language learners?

Yes, Diffit can generate content in multiple languages, which supports multilingual classrooms and English learners alongside its tiered difficulty options.

Does Diffit integrate with Google Classroom or Microsoft tools?

Diffit exports generated resources to Google Docs, Slides, Forms, and Classroom, as well as Microsoft 365, and the exported files remain editable rather than locked.

How much does Diffit for Schools cost?

Diffit for Schools uses a flat-rate annual subscription priced according to student enrollment; exact pricing is provided through Diffit's dedicated school pricing tool rather than listed publicly, and district-wide licensing is available on request.
