Cleanup.pictures is great for quick, no-fuss removal of distractions from photos, and not the right choice if you need full-resolution output for free or a broader editing suite. You paint over the unwanted object, person, text or watermark and the AI fills the gap; free use is unlimited but exports cap at 720p, while Pro lifts that and adds a refiner for better quality. The site links closely to Clipdrop, whose Pro plan bundles Cleanup with background removal, Uncrop and Generative Fill, and there is a usage-priced API for developers.

Best for

Good for bloggers, sellers and casual users cleaning up a handful of images. Not for print or high-resolution work on the free plan, teams needing shared seats (each subscription is single-user), or people who want layered editing.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Free use is unlimited in image count, so occasional users may never need to pay.
  • The brush-and-click flow takes seconds to learn.
  • Pro is cheap at about $3/month on the annual plan.
  • Refund policy: full within 14 days of purchase, prorated afterward.

Cons

  • 720p free exports are too small for print or large displays.
  • The pricing section and FAQ give different Pro figures, so the real price is unclear until checkout.
  • One-user-per-subscription rules out sharing an account across a team.
  • Complex backgrounds can still produce visible artifacts; this was not tested here.

Frequently asked questions

Is it really free?

Yes, unlimited images, but exports are limited to 720p. Pro removes the cap.

How much is Pro?

The site shows from $3/month, with the FAQ listing $5/month or $36/year. Confirm the figure at checkout.

What is the relationship with Clipdrop?

The site links to Clipdrop, whose Pro plan (from 11, currency not stated) includes Cleanup plus other tools. Corporate ownership is not stated on the pages checked.

Can I get a refund?

Refunds are described as rare; within 14 days of purchase it is total, after that prorated.
