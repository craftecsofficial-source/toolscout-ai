Cleanup.pictures solves a very common but historically fiddly photo editing problem: removing an unwanted element (a stranger in the background, a power line, a watermark, a piece of trash) without spending time in a full photo editor. Instead of manual cloning or healing tools, the user simply brushes over the object they want removed, and an AI inpainting model fills the area in with a plausible reconstruction of the background.

The tool became notable for being genuinely free and immediately usable in the browser without requiring a sign-up for basic edits, which set it apart from many freemium AI photo tools that gate core functionality behind a paywall. It was developed by the team behind Clipdrop, which was later acquired by Stability AI, and it shares underlying AI technology with other Clipdrop tools.

Because it focuses narrowly on object removal and inpainting rather than being a full editing suite, Cleanup.pictures is typically used as a quick fix-up step before or after other edits, rather than as a complete photo editing solution. Its simplicity and no-friction access have made it a go-to recommendation for casual users needing a one-off cleanup.

Best for

Cleanup.pictures is ideal for casual users, travelers, and social media creators who want to quickly remove a distracting person, object, or watermark from a photo without learning a full editing program. Small business owners cleaning up product photos and marketers doing light photo touch-ups also find it useful for its speed and zero-cost entry point. It's less suited to professional photographers or agencies needing high-resolution, pixel-perfect retouching, who would likely use Photoshop's more advanced tools instead.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • One of the few AI photo tools that offers genuinely useful functionality for free without requiring an account, making it very low-friction for one-off use.
  • The brush-based workflow is intuitive enough that non-designers can get good results on their first try.
  • Handles common real-world cleanup tasks well: removing photobombers, wires, small objects, and watermarks from otherwise good photos.
  • Fast, browser-based processing means there's no software to install and results appear within seconds.
  • Being part of the Clipdrop/Stability AI ecosystem means it benefits from ongoing improvements to the underlying inpainting models.

Cons

  • The free tier's resolution cap can be limiting for users who need high-resolution output for print or professional deliverables.
  • Highly detailed or repetitive-pattern backgrounds (fine textures, complex architecture) can trip up the inpainting and leave visible artifacts.
  • It only removes objects; it doesn't offer broader editing features like color correction, cropping, or filters, so it's usually one step in a larger workflow.
  • Very large objects or removals that cover a significant portion of the frame are harder for the AI to fill convincingly.
  • As adoption of the underlying technology has grown, similar object-removal features have become built into many other tools (phone camera apps, Photoshop's Generative Fill), reducing its former differentiation.

Frequently asked questions

Is Cleanup.pictures really free to use?

Yes, basic object removal edits can be done for free directly in the browser without creating an account.

Do I need to sign up to use it?

No signup is required for standard free edits; an account or subscription is only needed for higher-resolution exports and extra features.

What kinds of things can it remove from a photo?

It's commonly used to remove people, objects, wires, watermarks, timestamps, and text overlays from images.

Who makes Cleanup.pictures?

It was built by the team behind Clipdrop, which is now part of Stability AI.

Does it work well on complex backgrounds?

It handles simple to moderately complex backgrounds well, but very intricate textures or large removed areas can produce visible artifacts.

Can I get higher resolution output?

Yes, a paid Pro tier through the Clipdrop ecosystem unlocks higher-resolution exports and additional credits.
