PilotScribe is a good fit for a founder or small marketing team that wants a blog to run itself and is happy to approve AI-written posts in a review window. Skip it if you need a distinct editorial voice, heavy human editing, or more than about 100 articles a month. You give it your site URL, it picks buyer-intent keywords from live Google results, drafts articles against what already ranks, and publishes them on a schedule.

Its main difference from plain AI writers is the follow-up loop. After you connect Google Search Console, it watches clicks, impressions and position, then rewrites weak titles, refreshes fading posts and adds internal links to articles stuck on page two. Publishing goes to WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Ghost or HubSpot, through a headless API or webhook for coded sites, or to a hosted blog on your own subdomain. An MCP endpoint lets coding agents such as Claude Code wire up the integration.

Best for

PilotScribe suits solo founders, in-house marketers and small agencies who want keyword research, drafting, publishing and refreshes handled together, and who are comfortable with AI-drafted articles checked inside a review window. It is NOT for teams that need a strong human-written brand voice, very high volume (the Agency plan stops at 100 articles per month), non-blog content such as ads or social, or buyers who want a free tier or trial to test first, since none is listed.

Key features

01

Site & Keyword Analysis

Reads a submitted website URL to understand what the business sells and to whom, then targets real buyer-intent search terms rather than high-traffic vanity keywords.

02

Grounded Article Writing

Writes each article against the live Google results for its target keyword, matching the angle and depth of content that's already ranking, with fact-checked sourcing rather than generic AI filler.

03

Automated Publishing

Ships finished articles directly to a connected CMS (WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Ghost, HubSpot), a headless API for custom-coded sites, or an instant hosted blog — each with a review window before it goes live.

04

Google Search Console Integration

Connects to GSC to check how every article is actually ranking every day, tracking clicks, impressions, average position, and top queries in one dashboard.

05

Self-Improving Content Loop

Automatically rewrites weak titles, refreshes fading articles, and boosts page-2 posts with internal linking based on real performance data — without manual intervention.

06

Self-Filling Editorial Calendar

Plans and schedules a full month of content ahead of time on a chosen cadence, so users only need to glance and approve rather than build a content calendar manually.

07

MCP / AI-Agent Integration

Exposes an MCP server so a coding agent like Claude Code can find the site, grab an API key, write the framework-specific integration code, and verify the deploy automatically.

08

Multi-Site & Agency Support

Higher tiers support multiple connected sites and publishing to each client's own domain, with the Agency plan built specifically for reseller/agency use across many clients.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Covers the whole loop in one tool: keyword research, drafting, publishing and later rewrites, so you are not stitching together three products.
  • Rewrites are driven by your own Search Console data (clicks, impressions, position), not by a generic content score.
  • Many publishing routes: WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Ghost, HubSpot, headless API/webhook, or a hosted subdomain blog, so both no-code and developer sites are covered.
  • A review window before publishing lets you stop weak drafts, and the entry price of $39/month is low for an automated pipeline.

Cons

  • Hard monthly caps of 10, 30 and 100 articles; fast-scaling content operations can outgrow even Agency at $299.
  • Cheapest tier gets one site and a hosted blog; keyword research depth and rank tracking are listed only for Pro and above.
  • No free plan or trial is shown, so you pay before judging output quality.
  • No third-party review data (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) was found, so claims about ranking gains are unverified. The /pricing page did not load, so plan details come from the homepage.
  • Autopilot AI articles still need a human read for accuracy and brand fit.

Frequently asked questions

How much does PilotScribe cost?

Starter is $39/month for 10 articles and 1 site, Pro is $99/month for 30 articles and 3 sites, Agency is $299/month for 100 articles and 10 sites. All are billed monthly and can be cancelled anytime.

Is there a free plan or trial?

None is shown on the site as of 2026-10-30. Check the signup flow at app.pilotscribe.com for any current offer.

Does it publish without my approval?

Articles pass through a review window before going live, so you can approve or stop them.

Which sites can it publish to?

WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Ghost and HubSpot via connectors, a headless API or webhook for frameworks like Next.js and Astro, or a hosted blog on your subdomain.

Do I need Google Search Console?

It powers the daily rank tracking and the automatic rewrites, so connect it if you want the improvement loop.
