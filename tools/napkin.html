Napkin was built around a simple observation: most people think and communicate in writing long before they design a visual, yet turning that writing into a diagram usually means starting over in a separate design tool. Napkin closes that gap by treating text as the source of truth — you write or paste your content, and the tool analyzes the structure (steps, comparisons, hierarchies, timelines) to propose diagram types that match the underlying logic of what you wrote.

The product is positioned less as a presentation-deck generator and more as a 'visual layer' that sits on top of writing. Because of this, it's frequently used alongside tools like Google Slides, PowerPoint, or Notion rather than as a replacement for them — users generate a visual in Napkin, refine its style, and drop it into whatever document or deck they're building elsewhere.

Its appeal is speed and low friction: there's no template picker or blank canvas, just text in and visual options out. This makes it popular with people who write for a living (analysts, consultants, marketers) and need to communicate ideas visually without learning a design tool.

Best for

Napkin is best suited to people who write extensively as part of their job — consultants, analysts, content marketers, and product managers — and need a fast way to turn that writing into shareable visuals. It's especially useful for creating individual diagrams or infographics to drop into a larger deck, report, or blog post rather than for building an entire presentation from scratch. Teams that already have a strong visual brand system may find the customization options limiting for pixel-perfect brand compliance. It complements rather than replaces full slide-generation tools.

Key features

01

Text-to-Visual Engine

Analyzes the structure of pasted or typed text (sequences, comparisons, groupings) and proposes diagram types — flowcharts, funnels, timelines, matrices — that fit the content.

02

Multiple Visual Suggestions

For a single passage, Napkin generates several distinct visual interpretations so users can choose the layout that best communicates their point.

03

Icon & Illustration Library

A built-in library of icons and illustrations is automatically applied to generated visuals to add polish without manual asset hunting.

04

Style & Theme Customization

Users can switch color themes, layout orientation, and visual style after generation to match brand or presentation aesthetics.

05

Flexible Export

Finished visuals can be exported as PNG or SVG images, or copied directly into slide and document tools.

06

Highlight-to-Visualize

Users can select a specific sentence or paragraph within a longer document and generate a visual just for that section.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Removes the biggest barrier to visual communication — most people can write faster than they can design, and Napkin builds directly on writing rather than requiring a separate design step.
  • Because it offers multiple visual interpretations per prompt, users get quick creative options rather than being locked into one AI-generated layout.
  • The output integrates cleanly into existing workflows (decks, docs, wikis) rather than trying to replace the tools teams already use.
  • Low learning curve makes it accessible to non-designers on marketing, sales, and consulting teams.

Cons

  • It solves the 'visual for a paragraph' problem well but does not build a complete, multi-slide presentation narrative on its own.
  • Customization depth is shallower than a dedicated diagramming tool (e.g., Figma, Lucidchart), so complex or highly specific diagrams may need rework elsewhere.
  • Free tier generation limits can be restrictive for heavy users, pushing quickly toward a paid plan.
  • Visual style, while polished, can look similar across different users' outputs since it draws from a shared library of icons and templates.

Frequently asked questions

Does Napkin create full slide decks?

Napkin focuses on generating individual visuals and diagrams from text; those visuals are typically exported into a slide tool like PowerPoint or Google Slides rather than assembled into a full deck within Napkin itself.

What kind of text works best?

Structured or semi-structured writing — steps, comparisons, processes, lists — tends to produce the clearest diagrams, since Napkin looks for logical patterns in the text.

Can I edit the generated visuals?

Yes, generated visuals can be restyled, recolored, and have elements swapped after generation.

Is there a free version?

Yes, Napkin offers a free plan with a limited number of monthly visual generations.

What formats can I export to?

Visuals can be exported as PNG or SVG images and copied into presentation or document tools.

Is Napkin good for infographics as well as diagrams?

Yes, in addition to process diagrams and flowcharts, Napkin can generate infographic-style visuals summarizing statistics or comparisons from text.
