DocsBot is a strong choice for teams with a large body of documentation, help-center articles or ticket history who want a grounded support and sales agent that can also take actions. Very small sites or hobby projects will probably find it pricier than needed. It ingests content from websites, files, knowledge bases and helpdesks, retrieves and reranks relevant passages, then answers through a widget, Slack, Teams, API or voice. Actions via Zapier, webhooks, Stripe, booking and MCP let it do work beyond answering. Pricing is metered by monthly AI credits, bot count and source pages.

Best for

Good for SaaS and developer-tool companies, documentation owners, support teams on Help Scout, Zendesk or Freshdesk, and internal knowledge teams. Not for tiny sites needing a cheap widget, teams that need a ready-made live-chat inbox with human takeover, or buyers who want fixed predictable cost, since premium models burn credits faster.

Key features

01

Agentic RAG

Retrieves matching content, reranks it and uses it as context for answers.

02

Many source types

Websites, sitemaps, RSS, PDFs and Office files, audio/video transcription, Notion, Confluence, Google Drive, and ticketing systems. Several are beta; Gong and Gmail are 'coming soon'.

03

AI Actions

Skills, APIs, webhooks, Stripe billing, buttons, booking, search and MCP. Actions per bot: 3 Personal, 8 Standard, 12 Business.

04

Channels

Embeds, Slack, Microsoft Teams, ticketing systems, an API and voice agents.

05

Model choice and credits

Per-message credit cost depends on model, from 1x to 50x; bring-your-own OpenAI key counts as 1x.

06

Analytics and security

Topic, sentiment and question reports on higher tiers; Business adds PII redaction and SOC 2 Type II. HIPAA is described as available on Enterprise.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Connector breadth, especially helpdesk and knowledge-base sources, reduces manual content prep.
  • Actions turn the bot into a workflow tool: booking, billing lookups, webhooks.
  • Clear security tiering with SOC 2 Type II on Business and enterprise options such as SSO and self-hosting.
  • Choice of models, including bring-your-own OpenAI key.

Cons

  • Entry paid price of $49/mo is steep versus lighter builders.
  • Credit multipliers mean costs depend heavily on the model you choose.
  • Beta connectors and 'coming soon' items; source counts on the site are inconsistent (28+, 37+, 38+).
  • Key features such as analytics and sentiment sit on the $499 tier; I did not verify independent review scores today.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free plan?

Yes: 1 bot, 50 source pages and 100 credits per month, with no actions, integrations or analytics.

How are costs metered?

By monthly AI credits, bots and source pages. Premium models cost more credits per message (listed from 1x to 50x).

Is there an annual discount?

The page says annual plans include two months free but does not show annual prices.

Is it HIPAA or SOC 2 compliant?

The site lists SOC 2 Type II and GDPR on Business, and says HIPAA is available on Enterprise. Confirm with the vendor and request documentation.

Can the bot take actions?

Yes, on paid plans: skills, APIs, webhooks, Stripe, booking and MCP, with caps of 3, 8 or 12 per bot by tier.
