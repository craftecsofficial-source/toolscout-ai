Syntho is a strong choice for larger organizations that must keep data inside their own infrastructure and want realistic test data from one platform, and it is overkill for small teams that just need a few fake rows. It mixes masking, rule-based generation and AI-generated synthetic data, with a sensitivity scanner, subsetting, upsampling and foreign key handling to keep related tables consistent.

It is sold as a license for self-hosted deployment, with plans differing mainly by how many database connections and connector types they cover. Prices are quote-only, and the homepage mentions demos rather than a self-serve trial.

Best for

Mid-size and large organizations, especially those handling sensitive customer data, whose QA, data engineering or analytics teams need realistic non-production data. It is not for individuals or small teams needing quick sample data, nor for buyers wanting a public price or a self-serve free tier.

Key features

01

Three generation methods

Synthetic masking, rule-based data and AI-generated synthetic data, all listed in every plan.

02

Sensitivity scanner

Scans columns to find sensitive data; an open-text sensitive data scanner is Ultimate only.

03

Consistent mapping and foreign keys

Keeps values and relationships consistent across tables.

04

Subsetting and upsampling

Create smaller or larger datasets from the source structure.

05

Connectors, UI and API

Out-of-the-box database connectors, a UI and REST API for running and automating jobs.

06

Self-hosted engine

Syntho Engine is deployed on-premise or in your own environment; the vendor says it has no access to your data.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Self-hosting keeps source data inside your trusted environment, which helps with strict data residency rules.
  • Masking, rule-based and AI-generated data in one tool lets you pick the right method per dataset.
  • Foreign key management and consistent mapping matter for realistic multi-table test databases.
  • The pricing page states no usage-based fees or hidden costs, so volume growth should not change the license.

Cons

  • All pricing is quote-based, so budgeting needs a sales conversation.
  • The pricing page says unlimited deployments but its FAQ says extra deployments in other locations cost more; clarify before signing.
  • Plans are capped by database connections and connector types, so large estates may need Ultimate.
  • Implementation involves deployment and integration work with your databases.
  • The site names no specific compliance certifications, so verify any GDPR or HIPAA claims with your own compliance team.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Syntho cost?

Prices are not published. Basic, Standard and Ultimate each say 'Get a quote'. Licenses are typically a one-year agreement and cover feature usage plus one deployment.

Is there a free trial?

The homepage only offers demos. The pricing FAQ mentions an evaluation period within the license, but we found no free self-serve tier.

Where does my data go?

Syntho says it is deployed in your own environment and the vendor has no access to your data.

What separates the three plans?

Mainly the number of database connections (1-5, 6-10, 11-15), connector types, included features and implementation package size.

Does it claim compliance with GDPR or HIPAA?

The pages we saw call the output privacy-preserving but name no certifications. Verify regulatory fit with the vendor and your compliance team.
