Fraud.net suits payments companies, fintechs and large commerce businesses with a fraud or risk team and real transaction volume, and is a poor choice for small merchants who want self-serve tools with posted prices. It provides entity screening, ongoing monitoring and real-time transaction monitoring, using machine learning alongside a no-code rules engine and a shared anti-fraud network. Analysts get dashboards, case management and reporting to work flagged activity.

Best for

Fraud, risk and compliance teams at payments processors, banks, fintechs and larger e-commerce businesses that need real-time monitoring plus screening in one platform. Not for small merchants with low volume, businesses wanting plug-and-play pricing they can check online, or teams without anyone to own rules and model tuning.

Key features

01

Real-time transaction monitoring

Scores transactions as they occur using machine learning and rules.

02

Entity screening and monitoring

Screens entities at onboarding and keeps monitoring them over time for risk and compliance purposes.

03

Machine learning stack

Per the vendor, includes supervised models, anomaly detection, graph neural networks and generative AI with a learning loop.

04

No-code rules engine

Lets risk teams build deterministic rules and dashboards without engineering help.

05

Global Anti-Fraud Network

Shares fraud insights collaboratively across customers and industries.

06

Case management and APIs

Investigation workflows and reporting for analysts, plus API documentation for integration.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Machine learning plus a no-code rules engine gives both adaptive detection and deterministic control.
  • One vendor covers transaction monitoring and entity screening, reducing point-solution sprawl.
  • The shared network may help catch schemes seen at other customers before they reach you.
  • Case management and reporting let analysts act on alerts rather than only receive scores.
  • Non-engineers can adjust rules without code.

Cons

  • No published pricing or tiers, so budgeting requires a sales process.
  • Integrating into payment flows and tuning models takes engineering and data effort.
  • Detection quality and false positives depend on the quality of your historical data.
  • The homepage's outcome figures are vendor claims we could not verify.
  • Network benefits depend on how much relevant data exists for your industry.

Frequently asked questions

What is Fraud.net used for?

Detecting and preventing fraud and managing risk through transaction monitoring, entity screening and ongoing monitoring.

How much does Fraud.net cost?

Pricing is not public. The site offers only a demo request and contact form; we could not verify any plan prices.

Does it use only machine learning?

No. It pairs ML models with a no-code rules engine so teams can set explicit rules.

What is the Global Anti-Fraud Network?

A collaborative layer where insights are shared across industries to improve detection; the homepage does not detail data-sharing terms, so ask during evaluation.

Who is it built for?

Payments processors, financial services, fintechs and commerce businesses with substantial transaction volume.
