LANDR is a Montreal-based music technology company founded in 2012 (originally as MixGenius) by Pascal Pilon, Justin Evans, and Scott Murray, with the LANDR mastering platform launching publicly in 2014. It pioneered browser-based, AI-driven audio mastering: an artist uploads a mix, and machine-learning models analyze the track's frequency balance, dynamics, and loudness to produce a mastered version in minutes, with adjustable style presets and reference-track matching. The company has since expanded well beyond mastering into a broader creative platform, adding unlimited digital distribution to Spotify, Apple Music, and other streaming services, a marketplace of 70+ third-party plugins, a library of 3M+ royalty-free samples, artist collaboration and messaging tools, and online music production courses.

LANDR differentiates itself from single-purpose mastering tools (like eMastered or CloudBounce) and pure distributors (like DistroKid) by bundling mastering, release, and creative tools into one subscription, aimed at solo artists who want to avoid stitching together multiple services. In 2024 the company launched "Fair Trade AI," a program letting artists opt in to have their music used for AI model training in exchange for a share of resulting revenue and a transparency guarantee — a response to broader industry concerns about uncompensated AI training on musicians' work. LANDR also offers a mastering plugin for use inside a DAW and a mastering API for developers, extending its AI mastering technology beyond its own web app.

Best for

LANDR is best for solo and independent musicians who want fast, affordable, AI-driven mastering bundled with unlimited streaming distribution and don't need the granular manual control of a professional mastering engineer or standalone plugin suite. It's a weaker fit for audio engineers and producers who want deep manual control over the mastering chain, or for labels/artists who need highly customized metadata handling and fast, high-touch support during time-sensitive releases.

Lets artists share tracks, get feedback via messaging, and connect with collaborators or hire freelance professionals through LANDR's network.

Provides statistics on where and when a released track is streamed and tracks royalty payouts across distributed platforms.

Fair Trade AI Program

An opt-in program where artists can allow their music to be used for AI model training in exchange for a share of resulting revenue and disclosed usage terms.

Mastering API

Gives developers programmatic access to LANDR's mastering engine to integrate AI mastering into third-party apps and workflows.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Bundling AI mastering, unlimited distribution, a sample library, and a plugin marketplace into one subscription removes the need to pay separately for a distributor like DistroKid plus a mastering service.
  • Reviewers on G2 and Trustpilot describe the mastering quality as "surprisingly professional," delivering loudness and polish comparable to a studio engineer for typical genres.
  • The workflow is fast and requires no audio engineering knowledge: upload, pick a style, download — which several independent artists cite as saving them from hiring outside engineers.
  • The Fair Trade AI program is a differentiated, artist-friendly response to AI training controversy, giving contributing musicians a revenue share and consent controls other platforms don't offer.
  • A DAW-integrated mastering plugin and a public mastering API extend the same AI engine into professional workflows and third-party apps, not just LANDR's own web app.

Cons

  • Multiple reviewers (G2, Trustpilot) want more manual control over EQ and compression during mastering, since the AI-first workflow limits fine-tuned adjustments compared to a human engineer or standalone plugin.
  • Support responsiveness during high-traffic release periods has been flagged as slow, which is stressful for artists mastering and distributing against a release deadline.
  • At least one G2 reviewer reported LANDR's branding being embedded in exported file metadata without clear disclosure, requiring manual cleanup before submission to a distributor.
  • Reviewers note that album-level distribution and certain plans can feel costly relative to competitors, and that LANDR collects/retains royalties in ways some found less transparent than expected.
  • Metadata cannot always be easily updated after a release goes live, which has caused friction for artists needing to make corrections post-distribution.

What reviewers say

LANDR holds a solidly positive 4.1/5 on Trustpilot across roughly 3,000 reviews, with praise for ease of use and mastering quality offset by recurring complaints about pricing transparency and support speed during releases.

Frequently asked questions

Does LANDR offer a one-time mastering option, or only subscriptions?

Both: LANDR offers pay-per-track mastering starting at $9.99 for users who don't want a subscription, alongside monthly/annual plans (Basic, Advanced, Pro) with unlimited mastering and distribution.

Is music distribution included in LANDR's mastering plans?

Yes, unlimited digital distribution to Spotify, Apple Music, and other streaming platforms is bundled into LANDR's subscription tiers, not sold as a fully separate product.

What's the difference between LANDR's Basic, Advanced, and Pro plans?

Basic ($4/mo) offers unlimited LO-MP3 mastering, Advanced ($9/mo) adds HI-MP3 quality plus mastering styles and volume matching, and Pro ($25/mo) adds WAV and HD WAV export quality and advanced mastering preferences.

Can I use LANDR's AI mastering inside my own DAW?

Yes, the LANDR Mastering Plugin brings the same AI mastering engine into a DAW as a VST/AU plugin, so tracks can be mastered without leaving the session.

What is LANDR's Fair Trade AI program?

It's an opt-in program launched in 2024 that lets artists allow their music to be used for AI model training in exchange for a share of the resulting revenue and disclosed usage terms.

How good is LANDR's AI mastering compared to a human mastering engineer?

Reviewers describe the output as professional-sounding and fast, but note it offers less manual control over EQ and compression than working with a dedicated mastering engineer or standalone plugin.
