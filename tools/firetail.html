FireTail is a strong fit for security and GRC teams at mid-size or larger organizations that need to see and control AI use across both employees and their own applications; skip it if you are a small team, a solo developer, or want published pricing to compare. It is not a coding tool. It discovers AI usage in two places: workforce AI (employees using Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Claude, OpenAI, Gemini and Apple tools across browser, workspace and endpoint) and workload AI (AI inside your applications, agents and cloud accounts on Azure, AWS, Google Cloud, OpenAI and Anthropic, plus code on GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket). On top of that inventory it adds logging, threat detection and alerting, policy guardrails such as topic rules and sensitive-data protection, and compliance reporting mapped to OWASP, MITRE ATLAS and NIST AI frameworks. The company began in 2022 as an API security vendor, which explains the emphasis on log and traffic analysis. Its site cites SOC 2 Type 2 certification and says an AI inventory can be built in about 15 minutes, a vendor claim we could not test. A multi-tenant console exists for managed service providers.

Best for

Best for organizations with a security or GRC function that already know staff are using AI tools and need inventory, audit logs and policy, especially regulated companies and MSPs managing several client environments. Not for small teams or startups without formal compliance duties, solo developers looking for a coding assistant, or buyers who need transparent pricing before talking to sales.

Key features

01

Workforce AI discovery

Detects employee use of AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, Apple Intelligence, and similar) via browser, workspace, and endpoint signals, aiming to surface "shadow AI" that IT never approved.

02

Workload AI inventory

Scans cloud and application environments (AWS, Azure, GCP, GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket) to find AI embedded in the company's own products and infrastructure, not just consumer-facing chat tools.

03

Centralized AI logging and audit trails

Aggregates AI-related activity into a single log/audit trail intended to support incident investigation and compliance evidence requests.

04

Policy and guardrail enforcement

Lets teams set usage policies aimed at shaping safe AI adoption rather than issuing blanket bans on tools employees will likely use anyway.

05

AI-specific threat detection

Flags anomalous or risky AI usage patterns for security teams to investigate and respond to.

06

Compliance/GRC reporting

Maps discovered AI usage and controls to named frameworks — OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, and NIST's AI Risk Management Framework — to reduce manual audit-prep work for GRC teams.

07

FinOps-style AI cost/consumption tracking

Surfaces AI usage volume and spend patterns, useful for both cost control and as a proxy signal for shadow usage.

08

Multi-tenant management

Supports managing AI governance across multiple separate client environments, aimed at MSPs and managed security providers.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Covers both workforce AI (employee use of ChatGPT-style tools) and workload AI (AI embedded in your own apps and agents), where many tools only handle the first.
  • Compliance reporting maps to OWASP, MITRE ATLAS and NIST AI frameworks, which can cut manual audit prep for GRC teams.
  • API-security heritage suggests real log and traffic analysis rather than a surface-level list of detected apps, though we have not tested depth.
  • Multi-tenant console makes it practical for MSPs managing multiple clients.

Cons

  • No public pricing; the site only offers demos and onboarding calls, so even a rough budget needs a sales conversation.
  • Small, young vendor (founded 2022) in a category where larger security vendors are adding similar AI governance features; enterprise track record is limited.
  • Speed and ease claims such as a roughly 15-minute AI inventory come from the vendor and are unverified here.
  • Only valuable if you have a team to act on alerts and reports; it is a governance layer, not something that fixes code or models for you.

Frequently asked questions

What does FireTail actually do?

It inventories AI use by employees and by your own applications, then adds logging, threat detection, policy guardrails and compliance reporting so security teams can govern that usage.

Is FireTail a coding or AI development tool?

No. It does not write code or build models. It is closer to a CASB or SIEM for AI usage.

How much does FireTail cost?

Pricing is not published. The site routes to a demo request, so expect to get a quote from sales.

Which AI services and clouds does it connect to?

Per its site: Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, Claude, OpenAI, Gemini and Apple on the workforce side; Azure, AWS, Google Cloud, OpenAI, Anthropic, GitHub, GitLab and Bitbucket on the workload side. Check its docs for the current integration list.

Can an MSP use it for several clients?

Yes, it advertises a multi-tenant console for managed service providers.
