Zeedle AI is a software development platform (zeedleai.com) that markets itself as an end-to-end AI automation layer for the coding lifecycle: generating production-ready code from natural-language prompts or an uploaded image/screenshot, automatically writing and running tests, detecting and auto-fixing bugs in real time, optimizing code for performance, and assisting with code reviews and merge-conflict resolution. According to the company's own site, Zeedle AI was founded in 2022, launched its first AI code-generation platform in beta in 2023, added enterprise/team collaboration features in 2024, and describes 2025 as a global-scale-out year — though these milestones, along with headline stats like '1M+ lines generated,' '500+ companies,' and '50+ AI engineers,' come from the vendor's own marketing copy and were not independently verifiable through third-party sources during this research.

Zeedle AI positions itself against broader-scope AI coding agent platforms (like Factory or GitHub Copilot Workspace) by bundling generation, testing, bug detection, and optimization into a single lightweight product with markedly lower pricing — a free tier and a $19/mo Pro plan — rather than the $20-$200+/mo tiers common among agent-native competitors. Its differentiator, on paper, is affordability and breadth for individual developers and small teams, though the platform currently lacks the independent review footprint, enterprise case studies, and benchmark data that more established coding-agent tools (including Factory and Sofy, also covered on ToolScout) have accumulated.

Best for

Zeedle AI is best suited for individual developers, students, hobbyists, and very small teams who want a low-cost, all-in-one AI coding assistant for prototyping — generating components from prompts or screenshots, getting basic test/bug-detection help, and light code review — without paying $20+/mo for an agent-native platform. It is a weaker fit for teams evaluating a coding tool for production or enterprise use, since there is no independent review data, published security certification, or verifiable case study to validate its claims; those teams should look at more established, independently reviewed alternatives (such as Factory or GitHub Copilot) until Zeedle AI builds a public track record.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Pricing is significantly lower than most AI coding-agent competitors — a genuinely free tier plus a $19/mo Pro plan is well below the $20-$200+/mo range common among agent-native platforms like Factory.
  • Bundles generation, testing, bug detection, and optimization in one product, which could reduce tool-sprawl for a solo developer or very small team compared to stitching together separate point tools.
  • Supports both text-prompt and image-based code generation (e.g., turning a screenshot/mockup into a starting component), which is a convenient input method for UI-heavy work.
  • 14-day free trial with no credit card required lowers the friction to evaluate the paid Pro tier before committing.
  • Team analytics (code quality score, test coverage, deployment success rate) give at least a basic productivity dashboard out of the box rather than requiring separate tooling.

Cons

  • No independent review data exists on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot as of this research — there is no third-party verification of claimed accuracy, reliability, or customer satisfaction, unlike Factory, Sofy, RepliQ, and Shai, which all have at least a small G2 review base.
  • Headline performance claims on the marketing site (e.g., '99.9% code accuracy,' '10x faster development,' '500+ companies,' '1M+ lines generated') are vendor-supplied statistics without a visible methodology or independent benchmark backing them.
  • Public information about which underlying AI models power the platform, and what security/compliance certifications (if any) it holds, is limited compared to competitors that publish SOC 2 status or specific model partnerships.
  • As a smaller, less-established platform, longevity and roadmap continuity carry more uncertainty than for venture-scale competitors with larger public customer rosters and press coverage.
  • The Enterprise tier's promised capabilities (custom AI model training, on-premise deployment) are common in marketing copy for early-stage platforms but unverified here, since no case studies or named enterprise customers were found during research.

Frequently asked questions

Does Zeedle AI have a free plan?

Yes — the Starter plan is free and includes 100 AI code-generation requests per month, basic bug detection, code optimization, and 1 project, with no credit card required.

How much does the paid plan cost?

The Pro plan is $19/mo and includes 1,000 AI code-generation requests per month, automated testing, advanced bug detection and fixing, team analytics, API access, and support for 10 projects. All paid plans include a 14-day free trial.

Can Zeedle AI generate code from an image, not just text?

Yes — the platform's live demo supports both preset text prompts and uploading a photo/screenshot as an input for code generation.

Are there independent reviews of Zeedle AI available?

No verified reviews were found on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot for Zeedle AI (zeedleai.com) at the time of this research. Similarly named companies on Trustpilot (zeedle.com, zeedle.store) are unrelated businesses, so buyers should not confuse those ratings with this product.

Does Zeedle AI support enterprise deployment?

The vendor advertises an Enterprise tier with custom AI model training, on-premise deployment, SLA guarantees, and a dedicated support team, priced on a custom, contact-sales basis — but no public case studies were found verifying these claims in production enterprise use.

What does Zeedle AI actually automate in the dev lifecycle?

According to its own site, Zeedle AI automates code generation, automated test writing/execution, real-time bug detection and fixing, code optimization/refactoring, and AI-assisted code review and merge-conflict resolution, with a team analytics dashboard layered on top.
