Great for teams already running Zendesk that want AI resolution without adding another vendor; skip it if you are not on Zendesk or want a cheap, standalone chatbot. The cost model is seat licences plus a per-resolution fee, which suits steady, high-volume queues better than tiny teams.

Zendesk AI Agents handle customer requests over messaging, email, voice and social. They read your help center and other connected sources, follow workflows you describe in plain language, can take actions in connected systems, and pass tougher cases to a human team with the conversation context attached. Zendesk also says interactions can be quality-checked automatically and that the agents learn from past resolutions; these are vendor claims we could not test. Zendesk also announced standalone agents (based on its Forethought acquisition) that can run outside Zendesk, but the page gave no pricing or details for that.

Best for

Zendesk AI Agents suit support teams already on Zendesk, or planning to move, with enough repetitive volume that per-resolution fees are clearly cheaper than agent time. It is not for teams on other helpdesks, very small teams wanting a flat-fee chatbot, or buyers who need a fixed, predictable AI bill.

Key features

01

Autonomous resolution

Handles inquiries end to end across messaging, email, voice and social, escalating only when it cannot finish the job.

02

Knowledge grounding

Answers from your help center and other sources such as Google Drive files or PDFs.

03

Natural-language workflows

You describe a process in plain words and the agent asks clarifying questions and adapts mid-conversation; it can also act in connected systems.

04

Human handoff

Escalations route to the appropriate team with context so customers do not repeat themselves.

05

Multilingual

Supports up to 80 languages, detected from the customer's messages.

06

Quality review and learning

Zendesk says each interaction can be evaluated for policy and quality, and that gaps feed back into knowledge and workflows.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • AI agents come with every Suite and Support plan, so evaluating them does not require a separate contract; a 14-day trial is offered without a card.
  • Keeping AI, ticketing, routing and reporting in one system avoids syncing data with a third-party bot.
  • You are billed per automated resolution (no human escalation), which aligns cost with outcomes.
  • Wide coverage: messaging, email, voice and social, with up to 80 languages per Zendesk.

Cons

  • The included allowance (5 or 10 resolutions per agent per month) is small; a team with a busy queue will pay $1.50-$2.00 per resolution on top of seats.
  • Voice and messaging AI need Zendesk Voice and messaging respectively, and quality assurance needs its own add-on, so the real bill can exceed the headline seat price.
  • Zendesk's pricing page describes resolutions as tiered by complexity but we could not verify tier prices or exactly what counts as a resolution; ask before committing.
  • Enterprise pricing is not published, and add-ons like Copilot ($50/agent/month) stack on top.
  • Not usable unless you are on Zendesk (the standalone Forethought-based option is newly announced and unverified).

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free plan or trial?

No free plan, but Zendesk lists a 14-day free trial with no credit card, with Suite Professional features by default.

How much do Zendesk AI agents cost?

They are included in Suite and Support plans (from $19 per agent/month billed yearly). Automated resolutions beyond the included allowance are $1.50 each committed or $2.00 pay-as-you-go, per Zendesk's pricing page.

How many resolutions are included?

Zendesk lists 5 per agent/month on Support Team and Suite Team, and 10 on Suite Professional. Enterprise details were not published.

Do I need Zendesk to use it?

Generally yes. Zendesk has announced standalone agents that can run on other platforms, but we could not verify availability or pricing.

How does it compare with Gorgias?

Zendesk is a general helpdesk billed mostly per seat; Gorgias is ecommerce-focused and billed by ticket volume, so Gorgias fits Shopify-style stores better and Zendesk fits broader support operations.
