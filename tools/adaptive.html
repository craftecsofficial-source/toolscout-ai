Adaptive suits solo founders and small-business operators who want an AI agent to handle repetitive digital chores such as lead follow-up, research and light spreadsheet-based tools, without writing code. Skip it if you need transparent, fixed usage limits, deterministic trigger-action workflows, or strict compliance guarantees, since the free and paid allowances are not published as numbers and the product is young. It runs as a cloud 'computer' where agents keep working in the background, connect to tools like Gmail, Slack, Google Sheets, Notion, GitHub, Stripe and Square, and can reach other services that have an API. Sensitive steps can require your approval first, and there is an iOS app. The company, Adaptive Computer, Inc., raised a reported $7M seed round.

Best for

Non-technical solo founders, side-project operators and tiny teams who want a delegated AI assistant for outreach, research and light app-building. Not for teams needing predictable per-seat pricing, enterprise-grade guarantees on a tested track record, or rigid rule-based automations.

Key features

01

Autonomous task agents

Dedicated agents work in parallel on tasks like research, drafting outreach, following up with leads, and running scheduled jobs, reporting back rather than requiring step-by-step prompting.

02

Encoded Memory

Adaptive records how it solved a task the first time and converts that into a reusable routine, aiming to make repeated workflows faster and cheaper on subsequent runs.

03

Broad tool integrations

Native connections to Gmail, Slack, Google Sheets, GitHub, Stripe, Square, Notion, Discord, and 20+ other services let agents act directly inside the tools a small business already uses.

04

Browser and computer control

Agents can navigate web interfaces, click through forms, and take actions on sites without a dedicated API, extending automation beyond what pure API integrations cover.

05

Spreadsheet-to-app building

Users can turn a spreadsheet into a small operational app, and agents can read and modify connected GitHub repositories for lightweight custom software tasks.

06

Mobile-native execution

A native iOS app lets agents keep running in the background even when the phone is locked, so automations aren't tied to keeping a browser tab open.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Plain-English setup means a non-developer can delegate tasks without scripting.
  • Integration list is wide for a young product (Gmail, Slack, Sheets, Calendar, Notion, Discord, GitHub, Stripe, Square) and agents can reach other tools with an API.
  • Agents keep running in the background, including from the iOS app with the phone locked.
  • Human-in-the-loop approvals and scoped OAuth permissions are advertised for sensitive actions.
  • A free plan lets you test real agent runs before paying.

Cons

  • Allowances are given as 'substantial' or '5x' rather than numbers, and the pay-as-you-go rate is not stated, so monthly cost is unpredictable for heavy use.
  • Free plan cannot use pay-as-you-go and computer uptime eats into its small usage pool.
  • The pricing page says limits may change over time.
  • The product moved from a no-code app builder to an 'AI coworker', so third-party reviews may describe an older version.
  • No independently verifiable rating on G2/Capterra/TrustRadius for this product; a same-named construction-bookkeeping company is easy to confuse with it.
  • Security and performance claims on the site are vendor statements, not independently verified here.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Adaptive cost?

Free, Personal at $20/month and Pro at $100/month, all billed monthly per the pricing page. Enterprise is not priced publicly.

What usage do I get on each plan?

The page gives no numbers. Personal has a 'substantial' weekly allowance, Pro is 5x Personal, and Free has a small capped daily bonus.

Can I pay for extra usage?

Personal lists pay-as-you-go for extra needs; Free does not. The rate is not published.

Is it safe to let it send email or payments?

The vendor advertises approval steps for sensitive actions and scoped OAuth access. Start with the free plan and narrow permissions.

How does it compare with Zapier?

Zapier runs fixed trigger-action workflows. Adaptive uses instruction-driven agents that can browse and act, which is more flexible but less predictable.
