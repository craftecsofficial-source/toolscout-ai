Grammarly is a strong pick if you write daily in email, docs and chat and want mistakes and tone problems caught wherever you type. Skip it if you want a tool to generate long drafts from nothing, or if you are a creative writer who dislikes tools nudging your voice toward a neutral style.

It is an editing layer: browser extensions, Windows and Mac apps, Office and Google Docs add-ins and mobile keyboards underline problems and suggest fixes. The free plan handles spelling, grammar and a tone readout. Pro adds sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, plagiarism and AI-text detection and a bigger AI prompt allowance. Grammarly is now part of Superhuman, and the homepage says its writing support is delivered through Superhuman Go, though the Grammarly name and plans remain.

Best for

Good for students, professionals and teams who write across many apps and want in-line corrections and tone feedback. Not for people who mainly need long-form content generated from a prompt, writers who want full control of an idiosyncratic style, or large organizations that need many style guides and brand tones without going through Enterprise sales.

Key features

01

Real-time grammar and spelling

Flags grammar, punctuation and spelling errors as you type through browser extensions (Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge), Windows and Mac apps, and iOS and Android keyboards.

02

Tone view and adjustment

Free shows how your writing sounds; Pro adds tone adjustment and full-sentence rewrites.

03

Fluency and inclusive language

Pro adds fluent-English suggestions, inclusive-language checks and citation consistency.

04

Plagiarism and AI-text detection

Included on Pro and Enterprise. Dedicated plagiarism tools may be more thorough for academic or publishing use.

05

AI prompts

A generative assistant for drafting and rewriting. Allowance is 100 per month on Free, 2,000 per member per month on Pro and unlimited on Enterprise.

06

Team tools

Pro includes Snippets, Knowledge Share and team analytics, limited to one style guide, one brand tone and two user groups. Enterprise raises those to unlimited.

07

Enterprise security and admin

Enterprise adds SAML SSO, SCIM, BYOK encryption, data loss prevention, custom roles, confidential mode and an Audit Logs API.

08

Office and Google Docs support

Works inside Microsoft Office and Google Docs as well as in the browser.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Suggestions show up across nearly every writing surface, so you do not copy text into a separate chat window
  • The free plan is useful by itself: grammar, spelling and tone view at no cost
  • Pro bundles rewrites, tone adjustment, plagiarism and AI-text detection, which would otherwise be separate tools
  • 2,000 AI prompts per month on Pro is a generous allowance for an editing assistant
  • Enterprise tier covers SSO, SCIM, BYOK and DLP for regulated organizations

Cons

  • Pro is $30/month if billed monthly versus $12 on annual billing, so trying it month to month is expensive
  • Free excludes tone adjustment, full-sentence rewrites and plagiarism/AI detection
  • Pro team features are capped (one style guide, one brand tone, two user groups); lifting that means Enterprise and a sales call
  • Some writers find suggestions flatten their voice, especially in creative work
  • Plagiarism checking is a bundled extra; dedicated checkers may be more thorough
  • Parent-company move into Superhuman means product packaging and branding may keep shifting

Frequently asked questions

How much does Grammarly cost?

Free is $0. Pro is $12 per member per month on annual billing or $30 per month billed monthly, per the Grammarly homepage. Enterprise has no public price. Confirm on the official pricing page.

Is the free plan enough?

For catching spelling and grammar errors and seeing tone, yes, and it includes 100 AI prompts a month. You need Pro for full-sentence rewrites, tone adjustment, and plagiarism or AI-text detection.

How many AI prompts do I get?

100 per month on Free, 2,000 per member per month on Pro, and unlimited on Enterprise.

Is Grammarly now Superhuman?

Grammarly says it is now part of Superhuman, with its writing support delivered through Superhuman Go. The Grammarly plans above are what its own site lists today.

How does it compare with LanguageTool or Wordtune?

LanguageTool is stronger on many languages; Wordtune focuses on rewriting. Grammarly's edge is breadth of app coverage and the combined feature set on Pro.
