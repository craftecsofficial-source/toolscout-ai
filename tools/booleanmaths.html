BooleanMaths

Server-side attribution and ROAS analytics purpose-built for Shopify D2C brands, queryable by AI assistants via MCP.

Pro is $199/mo for up to 7,500 orders/month and adds WhatsApp retention campaigns and ad-blocker-resilient tracking. Business is $399/mo for up to 18,000 orders/month and adds Marketing Mix Modeling and priority SLA support. Enterprise is custom-priced (36,000+ orders/month) on the main site, though the Shopify listing shows a $799/mo Enterprise figure — the two sources don't fully agree, so treat exact numbers as directional until you check the live pricing page yourself. Annual billing is advertised at roughly 24-25% off, with no setup fees and cancel-anytime terms.

BooleanMaths solves a specific, well-understood pain point for Shopify sellers: ad platforms, GA4, and gut instinct rarely agree on which channel actually drove a sale, especially once third-party checkouts, cash-on-delivery orders, ad blockers, and cross-device browsing muddy the picture. Its answer is a server-side data layer — CAPI pushes to Meta and Google plus its own multi-touch attribution model — paired with AI layers on top (creative-fatigue scoring, audience segmentation, and an MCP endpoint that lets you literally ask Claude or ChatGPT 'which channel drove our margin last week' instead of building a Looker dashboard). That MCP piece is genuinely differentiated for a tool at this price point; most attribution vendors haven't built an AI-query layer yet.

Whether it's worth paying for comes down to two things: order volume and how badly your current tracking is broken. At $99-399/mo scaling with order count, the math only works if attribution gaps are actually costing you enough ad-spend efficiency to notice — a store doing a few hundred orders a month on a modest ad budget is unlikely to see enough signal-quality improvement to justify the line item, especially stacked on top of Shopify's existing app fees. A brand spending five or six figures a month on Meta/Google, where a few points of better optimization signal move real dollars, is a much more obvious buy. It's also worth treating the vendor's own accuracy claims (tracking-visibility uplifts versus GA4 and competitors like Triple Whale) as marketing numbers rather than audited benchmarks until you've run your own before/after comparison during the trial.

In the Data Analysis category on this directory, BooleanMaths sits closest to two very different tools worth ruling out first. Accoil is also an AI-driven analytics product, but it tracks product usage and account health/churn risk for B2B SaaS companies — a completely different buyer with a completely different data source (in-app events, not ad pixels and checkouts). If you're evaluating BooleanMaths because you searched 'AI analytics tool' and landed here by mistake while actually trying to reduce SaaS churn, Accoil is what you want instead; the two aren't substitutes despite sharing a category. HubSpot AI Tools is a more direct strategic fork: HubSpot's Breeze AI features sit inside a much broader, more expensive CRM-and-marketing suite, where attribution is one feature among dozens. If you already run (or plan to run) your marketing, sales and service stack on HubSpot, its AI attribution features may be 'good enough' bundled in, and adding a second specialist tool is redundant spend. But if you specifically need deep, Shopify-native tracking fixes — server-side CAPI, checkout-loss recovery, creative-fatigue detection — without buying an entire CRM platform, BooleanMaths as a focused bolt-on is the more surgical, cheaper choice. The decision is really 'specialist point solution' versus 'features of an all-in-one platform,' not which tool is objectively better.

Best for

Best suited to established Shopify D2C brands with meaningful (roughly four-to-six-figure monthly) ad spend on Meta and Google who need better attribution and server-side tracking than GA4 or pixel-only setups provide. Skip it if you're not on Shopify, if your order volume is too low for the tiered pricing to pencil out, or if you already have this covered inside a broader platform like HubSpot.

Key features

01

Multi-touch attribution

Models how each channel and touchpoint — first click through final purchase — contributed to a sale, rather than giving 100% credit to the last click.

02

Server-side CAPI for Meta & Google

Sends conversion events directly from BooleanMaths's servers to ad platforms, reducing the data loss caused by browser-side pixel blocking and iOS/ad-blocker restrictions.

03

Checkout, COD & cross-browser recovery

Reconstructs customer journeys that would otherwise go untracked because of third-party checkout pages, cash-on-delivery orders, or a shopper switching devices/browsers mid-journey.

04

BooleanVision creative analytics

Uses AI to analyze ad creative hooks and messaging combinations, flagging which creative angles are working and which are fatiguing with the audience.

05

Real-time ROAS and P&L dashboards

Rolls attribution and spend data into live return-on-ad-spend and profit-and-loss views at the channel and campaign level.

06

Post-purchase surveys

Collects zero-party 'how did you hear about us' data directly from buyers, used to cross-check and calibrate the attribution model.

07

MCP integration for AI assistants

Exposes BooleanMaths data through the Model Context Protocol, so tools like Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini can be asked natural-language questions about attribution and performance instead of requiring a dashboard.

08

Marketing Mix Modeling (Business tier+)

Adds top-down, spend-based modeling to complement the bottom-up, event-level attribution for a more complete read on channel effectiveness.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • It's a narrow, well-scoped tool rather than a bolted-on analytics module inside a bigger suite, which usually means the Shopify-specific edge cases (COD orders, third-party checkouts, RTO) get real engineering attention instead of being an afterthought.
  • Server-side CAPI is one of the few tracking investments with a defensible ROI story: better event match quality genuinely helps Meta and Google's optimization algorithms find better customers, independent of any dashboard value.
  • The MCP integration is ahead of most competitors in this space — being able to query attribution data conversationally through Claude/ChatGPT instead of learning a new BI tool lowers the operational overhead of actually using the data day to day.
  • Tiered, order-volume-based pricing is more honest than flat SaaS pricing for a Shopify tool, since a 500-order store and an 18,000-order store impose very different infrastructure and support loads.
  • The handful of visible Shopify App Store reviews are positive and specific (mentioning Event Match Quality improvements and being 'the most affordable way' to unify attribution), which is a better signal than marketing copy alone, even if the sample is small.

Cons

  • Being Shopify-only is a hard limitation, not a stylistic choice — any D2C brand on WooCommerce, BigCommerce, a headless stack, or planning a platform migration should look elsewhere or wait.
  • The discrepancy between the company's own pricing page (14-day trial, Enterprise 'custom') and its Shopify App Store listing (7-day trial, Enterprise ~$799/mo) is a real due-diligence flag — get current terms in writing before you commit rather than trusting either page at face value.
  • Claimed tracking-visibility gains ('+82% vs GA4,' '+5.6% vs Triple Whale') are vendor-reported and not verified by any third party in what's publicly available, so they should be treated as a starting hypothesis to test in your own trial, not a guarantee.
  • At 10 visible reviews, there isn't yet enough independent volume to know how the product performs under real support load, at higher order volumes, or across less common Shopify setups — early adopters are still somewhat trusting the vendor's own claims.
  • The order-volume pricing ladder means a fast-growing brand will get pushed into higher tiers relatively quickly, so the effective cost trajectory should be modeled against your growth plan, not just today's order count.

Frequently asked questions

Is BooleanMaths worth it for a small Shopify store?

Probably not yet — at $99+/mo, it mainly pays for itself once you're spending enough on Meta/Google ads that a few points of better attribution and optimization signal translate into real dollars saved; very low-order or low-ad-spend stores likely won't see enough upside to justify it.

Does BooleanMaths work with platforms other than Shopify?

No. It's built specifically for Shopify's checkout, order, and app ecosystem, so it isn't a fit for WooCommerce, BigCommerce, headless, or custom-built storefronts.

How does BooleanMaths differ from Triple Whale or similar attribution tools?

BooleanMaths markets itself on server-side CAPI depth and an MCP layer for AI-assistant querying; it directly claims a tracking-visibility edge over Triple Whale on its own site, but that figure is a vendor claim and worth testing yourself rather than taking at face value.

What's the difference between BooleanMaths and HubSpot's AI attribution features?

HubSpot bundles attribution and marketing AI as part of a much larger CRM/marketing suite (see HubSpot AI Tools), which suits brands already standardized on HubSpot; BooleanMaths is a cheaper, Shopify-native specialist for teams that just need the tracking/attribution problem solved without buying a whole platform.

How long is the BooleanMaths free trial?

The company's own pricing page states 14 days, but its Shopify App Store listing shows 7 days — the two sources don't match, so confirm the current trial length before you start one.

Are BooleanMaths's attribution-accuracy claims independently verified?

No independent, third-party audit of its stated tracking-visibility or accuracy uplifts was found; the figures on its site are self-reported and should be validated against your own data during a trial.
