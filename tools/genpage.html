GenPage's core idea is straightforward: instead of routing every ad click, cold email reply, or account visit to the same landing page, it uses AI to generate a matched variant per audience segment (a keyword, an account, a LinkedIn outreach list) and then tests those variants against each other automatically. It leans on bulk data import (CSV, Clay, Apollo, HeyReach) to make this practical at outbound scale rather than as a one-off design exercise, which is the main way it differs from a classic page builder or even most personalization tools.

The closest comparable product in this category is Mutiny, and the two make genuinely different bets. Mutiny is built as a personalization layer for a company's existing website, is priced per seat with a sales-gated Enterprise tier that commonly runs into five figures a year, and tends to require marketing ops or dev involvement to wire up account-level rules. GenPage instead sells flat, credit-metered plans with unlimited seats, lets a marketer self-serve from the free tier through the $249/mo Scale plan without ever talking to sales, and leans on AI generation plus bulk CSV/CRM imports to produce many page variants quickly rather than a small number of hand-tuned ones. In practice that makes Mutiny a better fit for an enterprise team personalizing a handful of high-value account experiences on a live corporate site, and GenPage a better fit for a leaner team that needs to spin up dozens or hundreds of campaign-specific pages fast.

On the numbers: current live pricing (verified directly on genpage.ai, September 2026) is meaningfully higher than what circulated during its AppSumo lifetime-deal era — Pro is now $79/mo billed yearly for 500 credits, and Scale is $249/mo for 2,500 credits, with heatmaps, session replays, and the HTML editor gated to that top self-serve tier. That's a fair price for a team that will actually consume the credit volume, but a team generating a handful of pages a month will find the free and Pro tiers cap out quickly once you count experimentation and A/B variants against the credit budget. The Ads Module (Google/LinkedIn campaign sync) being a separate paid add-on on top of Scale is worth budgeting for separately if ad-driven personalization is the main use case.

Our honest verdict: GenPage is worth it for outbound- and ads-heavy B2B teams that want AI to do the heavy lifting on page variants and are comfortable managing a credit budget; it's less compelling for a company that mainly wants to personalize its core marketing site for a small set of named accounts, where Mutiny's more purpose-built (if pricier) enterprise tooling, or a lighter-weight copy tool like Persado for message testing, may fit better. Independent third-party review volume is still thin (a handful of G2 reviews, a stronger but smaller-sample AppSumo history), so weigh a trial on the free plan more heavily than star ratings.

Best for

Best suited to small and mid-size B2B marketing, growth, and RevOps teams running paid ads, cold outbound, or ABM campaigns who need many personalized landing pages without a developer or an enterprise contract; teams wanting deep, hand-crafted personalization for a small set of strategic accounts on their core website are usually better served by something like Mutiny.

Key features

01

AI page generation per audience

GenPage generates a distinct landing page variant for a given keyword, ad campaign, account, or outreach list by learning the brand's existing site and content, rather than starting from a blank template each time.

02

Bulk import for outbound and ABM

Lists of leads or accounts can be pulled in from CSV, Clay, Apollo, HeyReach, Google Sheets, Zapier, or the API, letting a campaign generate many personalized pages from an existing prospect list instead of manually building each one.

03

Autopilot testing

Page variants are put into automated A/B (described by GenPage as 'A-Z') tests so underperforming versions are identified and can be swapped out without the team manually managing experiment configuration.

04

On-site analytics and alerting

Built-in conversion tracking, heatmaps, and session replays (Scale plan and above) are paired with real-time Slack and Gmail alerts so a team can see engagement without wiring up a separate analytics stack.

05

Custom domains and brand matching

Paid plans let pages run on the company's own domain and pull design cues from the existing site or an imported HTML template, so generated pages don't look visibly bolted-on.

06

Ads and CRM integrations

A dedicated (paid add-on) Ads Module syncs with Google and LinkedIn Ads for auto-publishing and ad intelligence, alongside GTM/CRM connections and webhook/SDK access on higher tiers.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The free-to-Pro-to-Scale path is entirely self-serve, so a team can validate whether AI-generated pages actually move conversion before committing budget, which is a real advantage over sales-gated personalization platforms.
  • Charging by credits rather than by seat means adding more marketers or SDRs to the workspace doesn't multiply the bill the way a per-seat tool like Mutiny's Business plan does.
  • Native support for bulk lead/account imports from tools like Clay, Apollo, and HeyReach makes GenPage genuinely useful for outbound and ABM motions, not just paid-search landing pages.
  • Bundling heatmaps, session replays, and alerting into the product removes a real integration step that teams would otherwise have to stitch together with a separate CRO tool.

Cons

  • Because usage is metered in credits, the true monthly cost is variable and can climb quickly for a team running heavy experimentation or generating many campaign variants, which makes the headline plan price a floor rather than a ceiling.
  • Some of the most operationally useful features — session replays, heatmaps, custom scripts — are withheld until the $249/mo Scale tier, so smaller teams evaluating on Pro don't get the full optimization picture.
  • The Ads Module needed for direct Google/LinkedIn Ads syncing and auto-publishing is priced and sold separately from the core plans, adding a second line item for teams whose main use case is ad-driven personalization.
  • Third-party validation is still limited: G2 shows only a handful of reviews and AppSumo's review base, while positive, reflects an earlier and cheaper version of the product rather than current Scale/Ultimate pricing.

Frequently asked questions

How much does GenPage cost?

As of our latest check on genpage.ai's own pricing page, GenPage has a free plan (100 credits/month), Pro at $79/month billed yearly (500 credits/month), Scale at $249/month billed yearly (2,500 credits/month), and a custom-priced Ultimate tier that requires a sales demo. A separate Ads Module add-on starts around $399+/month.

Is there a free plan or free trial?

Yes. GenPage offers an ongoing free plan with 100 credits per month and one user seat, with no credit card required to start, rather than a time-limited trial.

How is GenPage different from Mutiny?

Mutiny is a per-seat, sales-driven personalization platform aimed at enterprise teams personalizing an existing website for a limited set of key accounts. GenPage is credit-metered, self-serve, and offers unlimited seats on every paid plan, and leans on AI generation plus bulk data imports to produce many campaign-specific pages rather than a smaller number of hand-built ones.

What counts as a 'credit' in GenPage?

Credits are GenPage's usage unit for AI-driven actions such as generating a new page variant; plans include a monthly credit allowance (from 100 on the free plan up to thousands on paid tiers), and additional credits can be purchased as top-ups when a workspace runs out.

Can GenPage pages run on my own domain?

Custom domain support is available starting on the Pro plan, along with removal of the default 'Made with GenPage' branding; the free plan hosts pages on a GenPage-branded subdomain.

Does GenPage support Google Ads and LinkedIn campaigns?

Yes, but direct ad-platform syncing, auto-publishing, and ad intelligence live in a separate paid Ads Module add-on (roughly $399+/month) rather than being included in the base Scale or Pro plans.

Is GenPage still available as an AppSumo lifetime deal?

GenPage has previously run a lifetime-deal listing on AppSumo with tiers from $69 to $899 one-time, carrying a strong review average there; availability of lifetime deals changes over time, so check AppSumo directly to confirm whether it's currently active before assuming those terms still apply.
