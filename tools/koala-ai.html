Koala AI is a good fit if you run content sites or SEO for clients and want one tool to decide what to publish and draft it in bulk. Skip it if you write occasionally, want a distinctive personal voice, or only need a cheap general chatbot. It started as an article writer (KoalaWriter) and now centres on an SEO agent that uses your Search Console data to suggest topics, with KoalaWriter drafting the pieces.

Around that sit Brand DNA (your business details and voice), KoalaLinks for internal links and schema, KoalaImages, KoalaChat, KoalaMagnets embeddable GPTs, and an MCP server. Drafts can be published to WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, Ghost or via webhooks. Generated articles still need a human edit and fact check.

Best for

Good for SEO practitioners, affiliate and niche-site publishers, and agencies producing many articles a month. Not for casual writers who need a few posts a year, people who want a tightly controlled personal voice, or anyone expecting publish-without-review content. The full agent experience needs the $99 Boost plan, so budget-limited users may find it costly.

Key features

01

SEO agent

A chat assistant with keyword, backlink, SERP and competitor data that reads Search Console and suggests what to publish. Full access is on Boost and above.

02

KoalaWriter

Drafts articles, service pages and bulk batches, with a choice of underlying models that count words at different rates.

03

Brand DNA

Stores your business details and voice so content and recommendations stay grounded; brand counts and agent messages vary by plan.

04

KoalaLinks and KoalaMagnets

KoalaLinks adds internal links and schema markup; KoalaMagnets creates embeddable custom GPTs for leads and links. Both start at Professional.

05

Publishing and API

One-click publishing to WordPress, Shopify, Webflow and Ghost, webhooks, API access and an MCP server connecting 62 SEO tools.

06

KoalaImages and KoalaChat

AI image generation and a general assistant with real-time web data, included with subscriptions.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Connects content planning to your own Search Console data, so topics come from real performance rather than guesses.
  • Publishing to WordPress, Shopify, Webflow and Ghost removes copy-paste for most site setups.
  • Every paid plan includes the MCP server and API, useful for automation-minded teams.
  • Entry cost is low: a free trial with no card, then Starter at $25/mo.
  • A 15-day refund window exists for light usage (under 15,000 words and 100 credits).

Cons

  • Word counts depend on the model: the cheapest model counts 1x, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5 count 2x, Claude Opus 5.5 counts 3x, so premium quality burns allowance fast.
  • The full agent and Content Calendar Autopilot need Boost at $99/mo; lower tiers cap Brand DNA agent messages.
  • Monthly credits expire at month end; only annual credits last a year.
  • Annual prices are not shown, only the claim of 20% savings, so you cannot easily compare before checkout.
  • AI drafts at volume still need editing and fact checking; this is a drafting tool, not a guarantee of rankings.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Koala AI cost?

Per its pricing page today: Starter $25/mo, Professional $49, Boost $99, Growth $179, Elite $350, Advanced $500, Scale I $750, Scale II $1,250 and Scale III $2,000 per month. Annual billing reportedly saves 20% but those prices are not listed.

Is there a free plan?

There is a free trial rather than a permanent plan: 5,000 words and 25 credits with no credit card. You can also analyze your site without an account to see three suggested articles.

Why do my words run out faster on some models?

Word usage is weighted by model. The base model counts 1x, GPT-6.1 Sol and Claude Sonnet 5 count 2x, and Claude Opus 5.5 counts 3x.

Is there a refund policy?

Yes. New plans can be refunded within 15 days if you have used under 15,000 words and 100 credits. Contact Koala to request it.

How does it compare with Writesonic or Jasper?

Koala is more focused on SEO publishing, using your Search Console data and bulk article output. Jasper is aimed at marketing teams and brand voice, while Writesonic leans toward AI search visibility.
