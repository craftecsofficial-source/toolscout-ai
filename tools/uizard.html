Uizard is a good pick for non-designers such as founders, product managers and marketers who want a clickable mockup quickly, and a poor pick for professional UI designers who need fine control or a full design-system workflow. It generates editable multi-screen prototypes from text prompts, and converts screenshots or hand-drawn wireframes into digital designs you can then adjust in its editor. Its website still shows no shutdown notice and sells current plans, but its homepage footer shows a 2024 copyright and third-party sources report that Miro acquired the company, so check roadmap and support expectations before committing a team to it.

Best for

Who it is for: founders, product managers, marketers and small teams who need a quick visual prototype to show investors, customers or developers. Who it is not for: professional UI designers who need pixel-level control and design-system tooling, and anyone who wants monthly-only billing without checking the price first.

Key features

01

Prototype generation

Creates multi-screen, editable prototypes from a text prompt describing the app, site or desktop product.

02

Autodesigner 2.0

Changes selected components based on a written description of the edit you want. Free accounts use Autodesigner 1.5; Pro and above use 2.0.

03

Screenshot and Wireframe Scanners

Turn a screenshot of an existing interface, or a photo of a hand-drawn wireframe, into an editable mockup.

04

Themes and templates

Generates visual themes for a project and offers a template library (10 templates on Free, the full library on Pro).

05

Developer handoff

Pro and above can hand off designs in React and CSS.

06

Collaboration

Unlimited creators per organization and unlimited free viewers and commenters on all plans, per the pricing page.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Gets a non-designer from a blank page to a presentable, clickable prototype in minutes
  • Text, screenshot and sketch inputs suit different starting points
  • Pro at $12/mo (annual) is inexpensive for 500 AI generations a month
  • Viewers and commenters are free, so stakeholder review costs nothing extra

Cons

  • Free tier allows only 3 AI generations a month and 2 projects, which is barely enough to judge the tool
  • Only annual-billing prices are published; the monthly rate, if any, could not be verified
  • AI output generally needs hand editing before it looks finished or on-brand
  • The site carries a 2024 copyright and reported Miro ownership; I could not verify its current roadmap
  • Not built for large design systems or complex handoff workflows

Frequently asked questions

Is Uizard free?

There is a free plan with 3 AI generations a month, 2 projects, up to 5 screens per project and 1x exports. Anything heavier needs Pro or Business.

How much does Uizard cost?

Pro is listed at $12 per month and Business at $39 per month, both billed annually. The page does not show monthly-billing prices, and Enterprise is custom.

Can it turn a screenshot or sketch into a design?

Yes. The Screenshot Scanner converts screenshots into editable mockups and the Wireframe Scanner converts hand-drawn sketches.

Can developers get code from it?

Pro and above list developer handoff in React and CSS. Expect starting-point output, not production code.

Is Uizard still actively maintained?

Its site sells plans and promotes Autodesigner 2.0, with no shutdown notice. Third-party sources report Miro acquired it; I could not verify its future plans.
