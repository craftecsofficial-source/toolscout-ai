Scholarly is a strong pick for students and professionals who already have the source material (lecture PDFs, recordings, notes) and want cited study aids built from it, and it is worth paying for only if you will use the video lectures or weekly creation allowance. Skip it if you want a free ongoing study tool, since the free plan is a one-time trial, or if you just need basic flashcards, which cheaper or free apps cover. You upload a PDF, recording, website or note, and Scholarly produces flashcards with spaced repetition, quizzes and practice exams, narrated video lectures in several teaching styles, podcasts, slides, mind maps and cited Deep Research write-ups. Output stays tied to your own uploads, which keeps answers checkable. Paid plans run on a weekly allowance of AI creation credits plus separate caps on pages, upload size and voice time.

Best for

Good for college, law, medical and nursing students and for professionals who turn long documents or recordings into study material, summaries or slides. Not for anyone who wants a permanently free tier (free is lifetime-capped at 1 creation and 3 chat messages), for casual users who only need simple flashcards, or for people who would rather not manage weekly credit limits.

Key features

01

Deep Research

Answers user questions by reading and citing real sources, producing a written report in minutes that can be pushed further with follow-up questions.

02

AI Flashcards with Spaced Repetition

Instantly generates editable, active-recall flashcard decks from any uploaded file and schedules reviews using a spaced-repetition system.

03

AI Video Lectures

Turns source material into narrated videos in six formats (Standard, Illustrated, Short, Manim Math Animation, Kids, Freestyle), each with captions, transcript, and chat.

04

Source-Grounded AI Chat

Provides step-by-step help with math problems, essay outlines, or code debugging, guiding users to a solution rather than just giving the final answer.

05

AI Podcasts

Converts uploaded source material into a conversational, podcast-style audio format for passive review and learning.

06

Quizzes & Practice Exams

Generates unlimited practice questions and full practice exams from source material on paid plans, capped at limited lifetime use on the free plan.

07

AI Slides, Infographics & Mind Maps

Produces professionally formatted presentations, visual infographics, and mind maps directly from uploaded sources without manual design work.

08

AI Meeting Notes

Processes audio/video recordings into structured meeting notes as one of the platform's AI creation types.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Answers and study material are built from your own uploaded sources and cited back to them, which makes errors easier to catch than with a general chatbot.
  • One upload can become flashcards, exams, a narrated lecture, a podcast or slides, so you rarely need a second tool for another format.
  • Video lectures offer several teaching styles (including hand-drawn Illustrated and Manim-style math animation) with captions and a transcript, which is unusual among study apps.
  • Premium allows large inputs: 1,000-page PDFs, 300MB files, up to 10 sources per creation, plus Anki export.
  • Free plan needs no price commitment and lets you test one full creation before paying.

Cons

  • The free tier is a lifetime allowance, not a monthly one: 1 creation, 3 chat messages, 1 upload. Treat it as a trial.
  • Premium is $15/mo only if you pay yearly; month to month it is $30, double the headline price.
  • Creation credits reset weekly (15 on Premium, 80 on Laureate), and heavy outputs like long videos consume them, so big projects can stall mid-week.
  • Laureate at $99/mo is monthly only and costs far more than most study tools; its main extras are higher caps and frontier-model access.
  • Teams plans have no shared credit pool, so one member cannot borrow another's unused credits.
  • We found no independent review-site ratings (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) to confirm satisfaction; user counts are the company's own claim.

Frequently asked questions

Is Scholarly really free?

There is a free plan with no price, but its limits are lifetime totals: 1 AI creation, 1 upload, 3 chat messages, 5 practice questions and 1 exam. After that you need Premium.

What does Premium cost?

$15 per month billed yearly ($180/yr) or $30 per month billed monthly, as of 2026-10-27. It includes 15 AI creation credits per week.

Is Laureate worth $99 a month?

Only if you regularly hit Premium's weekly credit and page caps or need larger files (up to 1GB) and higher premium-model usage. Most students will be fine on Premium.

Can a team or class share one account?

Teams plans are self-serve for 1 to 29 seats at $28-35 (Premium) or $88-110 (Laureate) per seat per month. Each member keeps their own credit allowance. Larger school rollouts go through the sales contact.

How does it differ from a plain flashcard app?

It generates the material from your uploads (flashcards, exams, lectures, podcasts, slides) rather than just storing cards, with spaced-repetition scheduling on top. If you only need to review cards you write yourself, a simpler app is cheaper.
