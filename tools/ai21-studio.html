AI21 Studio suits developers and enterprises that want an inexpensive token-priced model API, or a vendor to custom post-train models, and are fine with a smaller ecosystem. Skip it if you need frontier-level reasoning or coding, a large community and plugin ecosystem, or certainty about a long-term public developer platform, because AI21's homepage now leads with services. AI21 Labs sells API access to its Jamba models, priced per million tokens (Mini and Large). Its homepage as of October 2026 describes a 'lab-as-a-service' model where forward-deployed researchers post-train specialized models for a customer, plus an Intelligent Gateway product, and does not mention Jamba or Maestro, although the pricing page still lists Jamba. We could not confirm from official pages whether Maestro and the earlier Playground tooling remain current.

Best for

Good for enterprises wanting custom-trained models in their own VPC or on-premise, and developers wanting low-cost Jamba tokens. Not for solo hobbyists needing a long free tier, teams that need top-tier reasoning or coding models, or anyone who requires published enterprise pricing.

Key features

01

Jamba models via API

Jamba Mini and Jamba Large are listed on the pricing page with per-million-token input and output prices, plus an SDK.

02

Post-training service

The homepage describes a four-stage platform (Evaluate, Train, Serve, Improve) where AI21 researchers post-train specialized models for a task.

03

Intelligent Gateway

Listed as a product on the homepage; the page content we could read gave no further detail.

04

Flexible deployment

Models can be managed by AI21 or run in the customer's environment, including a VPC or on-premise.

05

Custom plan

Adds volume discounts, premium API rate limits, private cloud hosting, priority support, a dedicated account manager and AI consultancy.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Token prices are published and low for Jamba Mini ($0.2 input, $0.4 output per 1M tokens), making experiments cheap.
  • Unlimited seats on pay-as-you-go means no per-user cost as a team grows.
  • Free trial requires no credit card.
  • Customers can run models in their own VPC or on-premise, useful for data-sensitive firms.
  • Custom post-training offers a route to a task-specific model rather than prompting a general one.

Cons

  • The homepage emphasis has shifted to post-training services; Jamba and Maestro are not mentioned there, so product direction is unclear.
  • The 7-day, $10 trial is short for serious evaluation.
  • Enterprise and Custom plans have no public prices.
  • We did not benchmark Jamba; earlier claims about 256K context and long-context speed are from AI21 and could not be re-verified from the pages fetched today.
  • Smaller developer community than the biggest model providers, so fewer third-party tutorials and integrations.

Frequently asked questions

How much does the AI21 API cost?

As checked on 2026-10-21: Jamba Mini is $0.2 per 1M input tokens and $0.4 per 1M output; Jamba Large is $2 input and $8 output. A Custom plan is available through sales.

Is there a free trial?

Yes, $10 in credits valid for 7 days, with no credit card required.

Is Maestro still available?

We could not confirm. The current AI21 homepage does not mention Maestro or Jamba. Ask AI21 or check the docs before relying on it.

Does AI21 offer private deployment?

The homepage says models can run in the customer's VPC or on-premise, and the Custom plan lists private cloud hosting.

Who should look elsewhere?

Teams needing frontier reasoning or coding performance, a large ecosystem, or fully public enterprise pricing.
