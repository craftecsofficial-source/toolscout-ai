VoiceGenie suits sales, support and collections teams that run enough call volume to justify an AI phone agent and already use tools like HubSpot or Twilio. Skip it if you make only a few calls a month or want a developer-first voice API, because the cheapest paid plan is $50 for 250 minutes (about $0.20 per minute) and minutes do not roll over. It lets you build agents that make or take calls, qualify leads, book meetings, send SMS, update records and transfer to a human, with post-call reports and campaign analytics. The homepage claims 50+ languages (older descriptions said 100+, so confirm your language in a trial). Quality claims such as accent and noise handling come from the vendor, so test on your own calls.

Best for

Sales, support and collections teams with steady call volume, in areas such as real estate, healthcare, finance and retail, who want an AI agent plugged into their CRM and telephony. Not for very low-volume businesses, for buyers who need a price per minute that falls sharply with scale, for anyone who wants rollover of unused minutes, or for those who can only pay by anything other than credit card.

Key features

01

AI voice agents for calls

Agents handle outbound and inbound calls, including interruptions, clarifying questions and confirming details, per the vendor.

02

Live actions during calls

Agents can book meetings, send SMS, update records, transfer calls and trigger workflows.

03

Voice and language choice

Choose voices and languages, design scripts and start from templates; the homepage states 50+ languages.

04

Human handoff

Calls can be transferred from the AI assistant to human associates; the Elite plan supports up to 16 associates, other plans up to 10.

05

Integrations

Listed integrations include HubSpot, Twilio, Plivo, Cal, GoHighLevel, Zapier, Pabbly, ElevenLabs and webhooks.

06

Analytics

Post-call reports, campaign insights and performance analytics.

07

Campaign use cases

Templates and flows for lead qualification, AI receptionist, abandoned-cart recovery, payment reminders, surveys and NPS calls.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Agents can take actions mid-call (book meetings, send SMS, update records, transfer), not just talk.
  • Integrations with HubSpot, Twilio, Plivo, Cal, GoHighLevel, Zapier and webhooks fit existing stacks.
  • Human handoff to live associates is built in on all plans.
  • Pricing page states no setup fees or hidden charges, and plans can be upgraded, downgraded or cancelled.
  • A trial lets you test the voice and call flow before committing, per the FAQ.

Cons

  • Per-minute cost is high at the entry level: $50 for 250 minutes works out to about $0.20 per minute.
  • Unused minutes expire at the end of each billing interval.
  • Only credit cards are accepted and there is no quarterly billing.
  • Yearly prices and the price of extra-minute packs are not published, so true cost at scale needs a sales conversation.
  • Marketing numbers (50+ languages on the homepage versus 100+ in older descriptions, uptime and accent handling) are vendor claims, and the pricing page's 'Trial' paid plan sits oddly beside a separate 7-day, 30-minute free trial.

Frequently asked questions

How much does VoiceGenie cost?

Monthly plans are $50 (250 minutes), $200 (1,000), $500 (2,500) and $1,000 (5,000). Enterprise is custom. Yearly prices and extra-minute pack prices are not shown on the pricing page.

Is there a free trial?

The FAQ describes a 7-day trial with 30 voice minutes; unused trial minutes expire at the end. This is separate from the paid $50 'Trial' plan.

Do unused minutes roll over?

No. Unused minutes expire at the end of each billing interval.

What payment methods are accepted?

Credit cards only, for subscriptions and other purchases.

What is the difference between an assistant and an agent?

The assistant is the AI voice bot; an agent is a human associate the assistant can transfer a call to.
