Zapier is a no-code automation platform that connects over 9,000 web apps, letting users build 'Zaps' — automated workflows triggered by an event in one app that perform actions in others — without writing code. Originally built around simple app-to-app automation, Zapier has expanded into a broader 'AI orchestration' platform that also includes Tables (structured data storage), Forms (custom intake forms), Canvas (visual workflow planning) and Zapier MCP/SDK for connecting AI agents to its app ecosystem.

Its core differentiator is breadth of integrations rather than depth of any single automation feature — with 9,000+ supported apps, Zapier connects tools that often have no native integration with each other. That breadth comes with a task-based pricing model that scales cost directly with automation volume, which is the single most common source of user frustration once workflows grow beyond simple, low-volume use.

Best for

Zapier is best suited for small businesses, freelancers and non-technical teams that need to connect a handful of everyday tools (CRM, email, forms, spreadsheets) without hiring a developer, and whose automation volume stays moderate. It becomes a weaker fit as task volume grows into the tens of thousands per month, where its per-task pricing gets expensive fast — technically-inclined teams with high-volume needs are frequently pointed by their own users toward alternatives like Make or n8n for better cost control at scale.

Key features

01

Zap workflows

Multi-step automations connecting a trigger app to one or more action apps, with support for conditional logic (Paths), filters and data formatting without code.

02

9,000+ app integrations

Direct integrations spanning CRMs, spreadsheets, messaging tools and e-commerce platforms, far exceeding most automation competitors' app libraries.

03

Tables

Built-in structured data storage that Zap workflows can read from and write to directly, reducing the need for a separate spreadsheet or database.

04

Forms

Custom-branded forms that can trigger Zap workflows on submission, letting users capture structured input without a third-party form tool.

05

Zapier MCP and SDK

Lets AI assistants and coding agents securely call actions across Zapier's app ecosystem via Model Context Protocol or direct SDK integration.

06

Zapier Copilot and AI fields

An AI assistant embedded in the Zap-building interface that helps troubleshoot and construct workflows, plus AI-powered data fields.

07

Canvas

A visual workflow-planning tool for mapping out automation logic with AI assistance before or alongside building the actual Zap.

08

Agents and Chatbots

Separate but related products for building AI-powered assistants and customer-facing chatbots that can trigger Zapier automations, sold on their own pricing tiers.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Largest app integration library of any mainstream automation tool (9,000+ apps), connecting services that often have no native integration path
  • Genuinely easy for non-technical users to learn — most reviewers describe building a first working Zap within minutes
  • Free tier and 14-day Professional trial make it low-risk to evaluate before committing to a paid plan
  • Zapier Copilot and AI fields meaningfully reduce the trial-and-error of building more complex multi-step workflows
  • Tables and Forms reduce the need for separate third-party tools for basic data storage and intake
  • Reliable for straightforward, lower-volume automations — most negative feedback centers on cost and complexity at scale, not core reliability

Cons

  • Task-based pricing scales quickly with volume — users running high-frequency workflows report bills reaching hundreds or even thousands of dollars a month
  • Debugging failed or broken Zaps is frequently described as difficult, with unclear error messages that don't identify which step in a chain failed
  • Many premium apps and advanced features (multi-step Zaps, webhooks) require paid plans, so the free tier is genuinely limited
  • CSV/data-import tools have documented limits that some users report causing problems with larger data imports
  • Complex multi-step logic and conditional Paths have a real learning curve, especially for non-technical users
  • Customer support quality is inconsistent across reviews — several users report slow resolution on billing and downgrade issues

Frequently asked questions

Is Zapier free to use?

Yes, Zapier's Free plan includes 100 tasks per month and single/two-step Zap workflows at no cost, though multi-step Zaps and premium apps require a paid Professional plan or higher.

How does Zapier's task-based pricing work?

You pay based on a selected monthly task tier (e.g. 750, 2,000, 10,000 tasks); each successful automated action counts as one task, and exceeding your tier either triggers pay-per-task overage billing or pauses your workflows.

What's the difference between the Team and Professional plans?

Team adds support for up to 25 users with shared Zap workflows, shared app connections and SAML SSO for collaborative account management, while Professional is built around a single user's automations.

Can Zapier get expensive for high-volume automation?

Yes — because pricing scales with task volume, teams running tens of thousands of monthly tasks report costs climbing into the hundreds or low thousands of dollars per month; this is the most common complaint in user reviews.

Does Zapier support AI agent workflows?

Yes, via Zapier MCP (Model Context Protocol) and the Zapier SDK, which let AI assistants and coding agents call actions across Zapier's 9,000+ app integrations.

What are Zapier Tables and Forms?

Tables is built-in structured data storage that Zap workflows can read from and write to, and Forms lets you build custom intake forms that trigger automations on submission — both are included as part of a standard Zapier plan.
