Zapier is a good fit for non-developers and small teams who want to wire together the apps they already use, and it is hard to beat on app coverage. Anyone running high-volume automations, or who needs tight cost control, should look closely at the per-task billing before committing.

You build a Zap: an event in one app triggers one or more actions in others, with filters, formatting and branching available without code. Zapier lists 9,000+ app integrations. Around that core it now offers Tables for storing data, Forms for intake, Canvas for mapping workflows, Agents and Chatbots for AI assistants, and an MCP server and SDK so AI chat or coding tools can use your connected apps. Tasks are only counted when an action completes successfully; triggers, polling and built-ins like Filter, Formatter, Paths, Tables and Forms don't count.

Best for

For: freelancers, small businesses and non-technical operations or marketing teams who need dependable app-to-app automation without a developer. Not for: teams running tens of thousands of tasks a month on a tight budget, or developers who want self-hosting and code-level control; those users tend to look at Make or n8n instead.

Key features

01

Zaps

Trigger-and-action workflows across apps, with filters, formatter steps and Paths for conditional branching. Multi-step Zaps need a paid plan.

02

9,000+ integrations

The widest app catalogue in the category, which often decides the choice for niche tools.

03

Tables and Forms

Built-in data storage and intake forms that Zaps can read, write and trigger from. They don't consume tasks.

04

Zapier MCP and SDK

Expose Zapier actions to AI chat clients and coding agents so they can act in your connected apps.

05

Canvas

A visual space for mapping out processes with AI help before building them.

06

Agents

AI assistants with live data sources and web browsing. Billed in 'activities' on separate Free (400/mo), Pro and Enterprise plans.

07

Chatbots

Customer-facing AI chatbots on their own plan ladder: Free, Pro, Advanced and Custom.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Largest integration catalogue of the mainstream no-code tools, so obscure apps are usually covered
  • Beginner-friendly: a first working Zap takes minutes
  • Only successful actions count as tasks; polling, triggers and built-ins like Filter, Formatter, Tables and Forms are free
  • Free plan plus a 14-day Professional trial without a card
  • MCP and SDK give AI tools a ready-made route into thousands of apps

Cons

  • Cost scales with task volume, and overage is billed at a higher per-task rate, so busy workflows can get expensive
  • The free plan only allows two-step Zaps and 100 tasks a month; multi-step logic, webhooks and premium apps need Professional
  • Agents and Chatbots are separate products with their own bills, easy to overlook when budgeting
  • Team and Enterprise trials are only available by contacting sales
  • Debugging long Zaps is a common complaint (unclear which step failed)

Frequently asked questions

Does Zapier have a free plan?

Yes. Free gives 100 tasks a month, two-step Zaps, and unlimited Zaps, Tables and Forms. Multi-step Zaps, webhooks and premium apps need Professional, which has a 14-day trial with no card.

What counts as a task?

Each action that completes successfully. Triggers and polling don't count, nor do built-in tools such as Filter, Formatter, Paths, Tables and Forms.

What happens if I exceed my task tier?

Pay-per-task billing applies at a higher per-task rate than your plan's. Check the rate in your account before running large jobs.

How much is Professional?

It starts at $19.99/month for the lowest task tier and rises with the tier, up to 2M tasks a month. The pricing page advertises a 33% saving for yearly payment.

Zapier or Make or n8n?

Zapier wins on app coverage and ease of use. Cheaper-at-scale or self-hosted options matter more once volume is high; Make and n8n are not in this directory, so compare their pricing directly.
