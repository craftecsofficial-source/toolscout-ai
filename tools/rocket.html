Rocket markets itself under the phrase "Vibe Solutioning," which sounds like empty jargon but actually describes something specific: the product is three tools stapled together under one login. "Solve" is a market-research assistant that takes a stated problem or opportunity and returns competitive analysis, go-to-market notes, and a rough PRD. "Build" is the actual AI app generator — prompt-to-code for web apps, mobile apps, landing pages, and internal tools, with Figma import and one-click deploy. "Intelligence" is a standing competitor-monitoring feed that watches pricing pages, hiring signals, and launches and rolls them into a daily briefing. None of this is revolutionary individually; what's unusual is packaging research, building, and competitive tracking as one subscription instead of three separate tools.

Whether that bundling is genuinely useful depends on what you're trying to do. If you're validating an idea from scratch, having Solve produce a PRD that Build can immediately act on is a real workflow advantage over stitching together a research doc, a prompt, and a separate competitor-tracking subscription. But if you already know what you're building and just want a fast, clean app builder, Rocket's extra surface area is overhead you're paying for and won't use. For that narrower job, Chat2Build is simpler and cheaper: it's a conversational app builder with no research or intelligence layer, and it's the better fit if all you want is to describe an app in chat and get it built. On the other end, teams building internal AI agents and multi-step workflows for an organization are better served by Stack AI, which leans enterprise with more mature workflow-orchestration and integration tooling than Rocket's more consumer-facing app-generation focus.

On pricing: the credit system (20 free, then 100/250/1,500 credits per month on paid tiers) is workable but not transparent — you won't know your real monthly cost until you've burned through a few builds and research runs, since both Solve and Intelligence queries consume credits alongside Build generations. The claim of 1.5 million users across 180 countries appears only in Rocket's own marketing with no third-party corroboration (no visible G2/Capterra review base at the time of this review), so it should be read as an unverified vendor number rather than independent proof of traction. The honest verdict: Rocket is a reasonable pick for someone who wants to know whether an idea is worth building before writing prompts, but it's not the tool to reach for if you just want the fastest, cheapest path from prompt to shipped app.

Best for

Best suited to solo founders, indie hackers, and small product teams validating a new idea who want research, building, and competitor-watching without juggling three subscriptions. Less suited to developers or teams who already have a validated spec and just want a fast, no-frills app builder, and not built for large enterprise workflow-automation needs.

Key features

01

Solve (market research)

Takes a stated problem or market opportunity and returns structured research: competitive landscape, product direction, go-to-market notes, and a draft PRD you can hand to the Build step.

02

Build (AI app generation)

Generates web apps, mobile apps, landing pages, SaaS products, and internal tools/dashboards from natural-language prompts, with Figma import and staging/production deploy environments.

03

Intelligence (competitor monitoring)

Continuously tracks named competitors' pricing changes, product launches, hiring activity, and website/messaging changes, summarized into a daily briefing.

04

Template library

25,000+ pre-built templates spanning common app categories, meant to shortcut common builds rather than starting from a blank prompt.

05

One-click deployment

Deploys generated apps directly to hosting with separate staging and production environments, without a separate DevOps step.

06

Team and security controls

SSO/SAML, role-based access control, and audit logging are available on paid plans, alongside SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certifications and GDPR/CCPA compliance claims.

07

Credit-based usage

Every plan (including Free) runs on non-expiring credits shared across Solve, Build, and Intelligence usage, with additional credits purchasable beyond the monthly allotment.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The Solve-to-Build handoff means you get a research-backed rationale before writing any prompts, which is genuinely different from builders that only respond to whatever you type in first
  • Competitor Intelligence briefings are a feature almost no other no-code app builder offers as a native, ongoing capability rather than a one-off report
  • Non-expiring credits and a usable free tier lower the risk of committing to a paid plan before you've tested whether the output quality fits your project
  • Enterprise-grade security posture (SOC 2, ISO 27001, SSO/SAML, RBAC, audit logs) is available even on lower tiers, which is uncommon for tools this consumer-facing

Cons

  • Credit consumption spans three different feature areas, so it's genuinely difficult to predict what a month of active use will cost until you've used it for a billing cycle
  • Bundling research and competitive intelligence with an app builder can feel like unrelated tools stapled together if you only ever use the Build feature — you're paying for capability you don't touch
  • As with most AI app generators, the code Rocket produces is a strong starting point but typically needs manual review, refactoring, or a developer's pass before production use beyond a prototype
  • The oft-repeated 1.5 million user figure is a vendor claim without an independently verifiable source, so it shouldn't be treated as proof of market validation on its own
  • Teams needing deep workflow orchestration or agent-building for internal enterprise systems will find Rocket's Build feature narrower than dedicated workflow platforms

Frequently asked questions

What does "Vibe Solutioning" actually mean?

It's Rocket's marketing term for combining market research (Solve), AI app generation (Build), and competitor tracking (Intelligence) into one product, rather than a technical distinction from other AI builders.

Is Rocket free to use?

Yes, there's a free tier with 20 one-time credits covering limited use of Build, Solve, and Intelligence. Paid plans start at $25/month for 100 monthly credits.

How is Rocket different from Chat2Build?

Chat2Build is a focused conversational app builder with no research or competitor-monitoring layer. Rocket adds those extra features but at a higher price and with more surface area to learn, so Chat2Build is the simpler choice if you just want to build an app via chat.

Is Rocket better than Stack AI for building AI agents?

No — Stack AI is built specifically for enterprise AI agent and workflow orchestration with deeper integration tooling. Rocket's Build feature is oriented toward generating full apps and landing pages rather than internal agent workflows.

Do Rocket's credits expire?

No, credits carry over indefinitely across Free and paid plans, and additional credits can be purchased separately if you run out mid-cycle.

Is the competitor-monitoring feature worth paying for on its own?

If competitive tracking is your main need, a dedicated competitive-intelligence tool will likely go deeper. Intelligence is best viewed as a bonus layered onto the app-building subscription rather than a standalone product.
