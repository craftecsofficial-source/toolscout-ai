Rocket is a good fit for founders who want to test whether an idea is worth building before generating it, and a poor fit for anyone who just wants the cheapest, simplest prompt-to-app builder. It is three tools under one login: Solve researches a market problem and returns evidence and a recommendation, Build generates web and mobile apps, landing pages and internal tools from prompts or from earlier research, and Intelligence tracks competitors' public pricing, messaging and launches and explains what changed.

The bundling is the point. If you are starting from an idea, passing Solve's output into Build saves stitching together a research doc, a prompt and a separate competitor-tracking tool. If you already know what you're building, the research and monitoring layers are surface area you pay for and won't use.

All three features draw on one credit pool. The Free plan gives 20 one-time credits; Pro, Rocket and Booster add 100, 250 and 1,500 credits a month on top of a platform fee, and extra credits can be bought. Because both research runs and builds spend credits, expect to need a few weeks of use before you know your real monthly cost. Rocket's own marketing makes large user-count claims; we could not verify any such figure independently, and we found no third-party review source today, so treat adoption claims as unproven.

Best for

Solo founders, indie hackers and small product teams validating a new idea who want research, building and competitor-watching in one place. Not for developers or teams who already have a validated spec and want a lean builder, and not for enterprises needing deep agent or workflow orchestration (SSO and data localization exist only via sales).

Key features

01

Solve (market research)

Takes a market problem or business decision and returns researched evidence and a recommendation. Free and Pro plans list essential research; Rocket and Booster list consultant-grade outputs.

02

Build (AI app generation)

Generates production-oriented websites, landing pages, web apps and mobile apps from natural-language prompts, or from Solve's output.

03

Intelligence (competitor monitoring)

Watches competitors' public activity such as pricing, messaging and launches and explains what the changes mean. The pricing page lists 'personalized business intelligence' on all plans, but volume limits per plan weren't stated.

04

Credit system

One credit pool covers all features. Paid plans include monthly credits that roll over while subscribed, and you can buy more at any time.

05

Teams and enterprise

Unlimited team members on every plan. SSO, data localization, premium support and onboarding are offered through sales.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Solve feeding straight into Build gives you a research-backed starting point instead of whatever you type into a blank prompt
  • Ongoing competitor tracking is a native feature, which most app builders don't offer at all
  • No per-seat fees means a whole team can use one plan without cost scaling by headcount
  • A card-free Free plan lets you check output quality before paying

Cons

  • One credit pool across research, builds and monitoring means you can't easily forecast a month's spend until you've used it
  • If you only use Build, you pay a platform fee that also covers research and intelligence you may never touch
  • Generated apps are a starting point; plan for review and developer cleanup before real production use
  • The pricing page is slightly inconsistent about whether Free credits expire, and which plans include competitive intelligence is unclear, so confirm before buying
  • We could not verify yearly prices or any independent user-count claims

Frequently asked questions

Is Rocket free to use?

Yes. The Free plan costs $0, needs no credit card and includes 20 one-time credits. Paid plans start at a $25/month platform fee with 100 monthly credits.

Do Rocket credits expire?

The pricing page says paid-plan credits roll over while you stay subscribed, and also describes credits as non-expiring. Free credits are described as one-time. Check the current terms if this matters to you.

What's the difference between Pro and Rocket plans?

Rocket ($50/mo) gives 250 monthly credits and consultant-grade research outputs; Pro ($25/mo) gives 100 credits and essential research.

Is the competitor monitoring worth paying for on its own?

Probably not. It's best treated as a bonus on top of app building; a dedicated competitive-intelligence tool will likely go deeper.

How much does yearly billing save?

The pricing page advertises 20% off with yearly billing but did not show the totals when we checked, so confirm the exact amounts at checkout.
