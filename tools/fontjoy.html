Fontjoy tackles one of the most common design bottlenecks: choosing fonts that look good together. Rather than relying on manual trial-and-error or generic best-practice lists, it uses a neural network trained to understand the visual characteristics of fonts (weight, style, shape) and generates pairings that balance contrast and harmony.

The interface is deliberately minimal: three text panels typically represent a heading, subheading, and body font, and clicking 'Generate' shuffles in a new AI-suggested combination drawn from the Google Fonts library. Users can lock any single font in place while continuing to shuffle the others, letting them iterate around a font they already like. A slider also lets users control how similar or contrasting the paired fonts should be.

Because it's free, fast, and requires no design background, Fontjoy has become a popular quick-reference tool for web designers, presentation builders, and brand designers looking for a fast starting point for typography decisions.

Best for

Fontjoy is well suited to web designers, developers, and marketers who need a quick, good-looking font pairing without deep typographic training. It's especially useful for people building websites, presentations, or simple brand assets who want a fast starting point rather than a definitive final answer. Professional type designers or brands needing licensed custom fonts will need additional tools. It works best as a brainstorming aid early in the design process.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Removes the guesswork from typography for people without formal design training
  • The lock feature makes it easy to iterate around a font you're already committed to
  • Being built entirely on Google Fonts means pairings are free and instantly usable in real projects
  • No cost or account barrier means it can be used casually as often as needed

Cons

  • Restricted to the Google Fonts catalog, so it can't suggest premium or custom brand fonts
  • No way to save, favorite, or export a history of past pairings for later reference
  • Only handles font pairing, not broader type scale, spacing, or layout decisions
  • Occasionally surfaces pairings that feel generic or overused across many web projects

Frequently asked questions

Is Fontjoy free?

Yes, Fontjoy is completely free to use with no account or payment required.

Does Fontjoy support custom or paid fonts?

No, it is limited to fonts available in the Google Fonts library.

Can I save my favorite font pairings?

Fontjoy does not currently offer a way to save or export a history of pairings within the tool.

How does Fontjoy generate pairings?

It uses a deep learning model trained on font characteristics to suggest combinations that balance visual contrast and harmony.

Can I keep one font while changing the other?

Yes, the lock feature lets you fix one font in place while shuffling the rest.

Is Fontjoy good for print design?

It's primarily oriented toward web typography via Google Fonts, though the pairing logic can still inform print choices.
