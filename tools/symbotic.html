Symbotic is a publicly traded (NASDAQ: SYM) robotics and AI automation company headquartered in Wilmington, Massachusetts, that builds warehouse automation systems for large retailers and distributors. Founded in 2007 by Rick Cohen — owner of one of the world's largest wholesale grocery distributors, who needed to automate his own warehouses — Symbotic combines fleets of autonomous mobile robots (branded SymBot and BP Bot) with AI-driven software that orchestrates depalletizing, high-density storage and retrieval, and case-level palletization. Rather than selling a piece of software or a single robot, Symbotic delivers a fully integrated, pre-engineered system, meaning customers don't need to hire a separate systems integrator to combine robotics, software, and racking into a working warehouse.

The company has scaled significantly since going public in 2022: it reports over 22,200 active bots, more than 2.6 billion packages moved through its systems, 100+ operating locations worldwide, and over 1,050 patents issued or pending. Symbotic's largest customer by far is Walmart, which generated roughly 85% of the company's fiscal 2025 revenue and holds a contract to automate all of its U.S. regional distribution centers by 2034; other customers include Target and Albertsons. Symbotic also offers a Warehouse-as-a-Service model for customers who prefer an operating-expense structure over large upfront capital investment. Because it sells complete physical-and-software systems rather than SaaS subscriptions, Symbotic sits at the far enterprise end of the "Productivity & Automation" category — its AI software specifically handles fleet orchestration, task sequencing, and inventory optimization across thousands of robots operating simultaneously.

Best for

Symbotic is built for large retailers, grocers, and distributors — think national chains the size of Walmart, Target, or Albertsons — that need to automate entire distribution-center operations end to end. It is not a fit for small or mid-size businesses, teams needing a lightweight software tool, or anyone looking for self-serve pricing rather than a multi-year capital deployment.

Key features

01

SymBot Fleet

Autonomous robots that travel a structured storage grid at high speed to retrieve and store inventory with dense, automated racking.

02

BP Bot

A robotic arm-based bot that performs case picking and palletization, building mixed pallets to match store or order requirements.

03

AI Orchestration Software

Central AI software plans and sequences the movement of thousands of robots simultaneously, optimizing throughput, storage density, and order fulfillment in real time.

04

Automated Depalletizing

Robotic systems automatically unload inbound pallets of mixed case sizes without manual labor.

05

Warehouse-as-a-Service

An alternative delivery model letting customers pay for automation as an ongoing service rather than a large upfront capital purchase.

06

Pre-Engineered Integration

Symbotic delivers robots, software, and racking as one integrated system, removing the need for a separate third-party systems integrator.

07

SymMicro

A smaller-footprint automation solution aimed at facilities that don't require full-scale distribution-center automation.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Delivers a complete, pre-integrated system spanning robots, AI orchestration software, and racking, removing the need to coordinate a separate systems integrator.
  • Operates at proven industrial scale, with 22,200+ active bots and over 2.6 billion packages moved through its systems to date.
  • Anchored by a long-term flagship contract with Walmart to automate all U.S. regional distribution centers by 2034, demonstrating durable enterprise trust.
  • Backed by substantial R&D investment (over $1 billion to date) and a deep patent portfolio (1,050+ issued or pending), suggesting sustained technical differentiation.
  • Offers a Warehouse-as-a-Service option, giving large customers a path to automation without a massive upfront capital outlay.

Cons

  • Revenue is heavily concentrated in a single customer — Walmart accounted for roughly 85% of fiscal 2025 revenue — creating dependency risk for the business.
  • The system requires major capital investment and multi-year physical deployment timelines, putting it out of reach for small and mid-size operations.
  • As a public company (NASDAQ: SYM), pricing and contract terms are negotiated individually per deployment with no published self-serve pricing.
  • Implementation is a long-cycle infrastructure project rather than a software rollout, so time-to-value is measured in months to years, not days.

Frequently asked questions

What does Symbotic actually sell?

Symbotic sells a fully integrated warehouse automation system combining autonomous robots (SymBot, BP Bot), AI orchestration software, and racking, deployed as one pre-engineered solution.

Who are Symbotic's main customers?

Symbotic's largest customer is Walmart, which represented about 85% of fiscal 2025 revenue; other customers include Target and Albertsons.

Is Symbotic a software product or a robotics company?

Both — Symbotic combines physical autonomous robots with AI-driven software that plans and sequences robot activity across the warehouse in real time.

How much does a Symbotic deployment cost?

Symbotic does not publish self-serve pricing; deployments are custom-quoted, multi-year enterprise contracts, available as a capital purchase or via its Warehouse-as-a-Service model.

Is Symbotic a public company?

Yes, Symbotic Inc. trades on Nasdaq under the ticker SYM and went public in 2022.

When was Symbotic founded?

Symbotic was founded in 2007 by Rick Cohen and is headquartered in Wilmington, Massachusetts.
