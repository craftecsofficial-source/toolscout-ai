ThinkDiffusion is great for people who already want to use ComfyUI, Automatic1111, or Kohya but lack the hardware, and it is a poor fit if you want a simple prompt-box image generator or already own a capable GPU. It rents you a private cloud machine preloaded with these open-source tools, so you launch one from the browser, pay by the hour, and install whatever models and extensions you like.

Best for

Good for AI artists, LoRA trainers, agencies, and classes that need flexible, unrestricted tooling. Not for casual users who just want to type a prompt and get an image, people who dislike managing models and workflows themselves, anyone with a strong local GPU who runs many hours a day (local is cheaper), or users without reliable internet.

Key features

01

Dedicated machines

Each machine serves one user with its own GPU. Tiers are Quick (16GB VRAM), Turbo (24GB), and Ultra (48GB).

02

Choice of apps

Automatic1111, ComfyUI, Fooocus, Forge (with Flux support), and Kohya for training. The homepage also mentions video apps like Wan and Hunyuan.

03

Model and extension freedom

Add models by URL (such as Civitai) or drag and drop, and install extensions and custom nodes.

04

Training

Dreambooth, Textual Inversion, and LoRA training are included in all plans per the pricing page.

05

Storage and workspaces

Hobby gets a 10GB drive with 48-hour persistence; paid plans get 200GB with persistence for the life of the plan. Teams plans add separate workspaces.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Gives access to ComfyUI, A1111, Forge, and Kohya without buying a GPU.
  • Rates are published per hour by tier, so you can estimate cost before a session.
  • No content-curated front end: any model, extension, or node you choose can be installed.
  • The Hobby plan has no subscription, and it comes with 15 free minutes to test.
  • Auto-stop timers help avoid paying for an idle machine.

Cons

  • You pay by the hour even on a subscription, so daily heavy use gets expensive quickly.
  • Hobby storage is only 48 hours and files are accessible only while a machine runs.
  • You must learn the tools yourself; this is an environment, not a guided product.
  • The pricing page does not state whether yearly plans are charged upfront.
  • We did not verify independent review ratings today, so none are shown.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to run?

On Hobby you pay $0.99/hr (Quick 16GB), $1.75/hr (Turbo 24GB), or $2.50/hr (Ultra 48GB). TD-PRO and TD-TEAMS get 20% off these rates.

Do I need a subscription?

No. Hobby is pay-as-you-go with 15 free minutes on sign-up, but storage lasts only 48 hours.

Can I train LoRAs?

Yes. The pricing page lists Dreambooth, Textual Inversion, and LoRA training in all plans, using Kohya.

Can I use my own models?

Yes. Upload by URL (e.g., Civitai) or drag and drop, and install extensions and custom nodes.

Does it keep my files?

Paid plans keep persistent storage for the life of the plan (up to 200GB). Hobby keeps files for 48 hours.
