NeuralWriter is a good pick if you need a free, no-account tool to reword or simplify text and can live with a per-run size cap. Skip it if you want grammar checking, team features, support guarantees or a vendor with a visible company track record. It rewrites whole sentences rather than swapping single words, lets you choose from 8 writing styles and 13 language standards such as plain language or CEFR levels, and offers Light and Hard strengths. A diff view highlights every change, names, numbers and technical terms are kept, and Markdown, HTML, links and citations are preserved. You can lock up to 20 words or phrases. The site says texts are deleted within 24 hours and are not published or sold, which is a claim we could not verify independently. The site also links a free AI content detector and a paraphrasing API.

Best for

Good for students, bloggers, non-native English writers and anyone who needs to simplify text to a reading level without paying or signing up. Not for people who need grammar and tone checking, long documents in one pass (10,000 characters per run), a guaranteed service level, or anyone pasting confidential text who needs a verified privacy review.

Key features

01

Light and Hard strength

Light makes conservative edits; Hard rewrites more deeply. On a computer, Hard needs the free Chrome extension.

02

8 styles and 13 language standards

21 modes in total, including plain language (ISO 24495-1), Easy-to-Read, CEFR A2-B1, ASD-STE100 and UX writing. Some standards are language-specific, and a few work only on English.

03

Diff view

Highlights every change so you can check the rewrite against the original.

04

Preservation and locking

Keeps names, titles, brands, numbers and technical terms, preserves Markdown, HTML, links, tables and citations, and lets you lock up to 20 words or phrases.

05

30 languages

The tool supports 30 languages.

06

Related tools

The site links a free AI content detector and a Paraphrase API (API pricing not seen).

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Costs nothing and needs no account, so you can try it in seconds.
  • The diff view makes it easy to see what changed instead of trusting the output blindly.
  • Names, numbers, links, tables and citations are preserved, and you can lock specific terms, which suits academic and technical text.
  • Reading-level and plain-language standards go beyond a simple synonym swapper.
  • Covers 30 languages.

Cons

  • Each run is limited to about 1,500 words, so long documents must be split.
  • Hard mode on desktop depends on installing the Chrome extension.
  • Does not check grammar or style the way a full writing assistant does.
  • Little company information and no independent reviews that we could verify; the privacy and deletion claims are the site's own.
  • Rewriting tools can alter meaning; always read the output before using it, and check your school or employer's rules on AI-assisted rewriting.

Frequently asked questions

Is NeuralWriter really free?

Yes. The site describes it as free with no sign-up and no daily limit. We found no paid plans listed.

What is the length limit?

Each run accepts up to 10,000 characters, roughly 1,500 words. Longer text has to be split.

Do I need the Chrome extension?

Light mode works on the website. According to the site's FAQ, Hard strength on a computer needs the free Chrome extension.

What happens to my text?

The site says texts are deleted automatically within 24 hours and are never published or sold. We could not independently verify this, so avoid pasting confidential material.

How does it compare with Wordtune or Rewording.io?

It is free with no account. Wordtune is a broader paid rewriting assistant; check each tool's current limits before choosing.
