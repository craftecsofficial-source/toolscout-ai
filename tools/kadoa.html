Kadoa is an AI-native web scraping and data extraction platform built to remove the two most painful parts of traditional web scraping: writing custom parsers for every site, and constantly fixing scrapers when target websites change their HTML structure. Instead of relying on brittle, hand-coded selectors, Kadoa uses AI models to understand the semantic structure of a page or document and extract the relevant fields automatically.

A key differentiator is its "self-healing" approach: when a website changes its layout, Kadoa's models re-detect the relevant data fields rather than requiring a developer to rewrite scraping logic. Beyond live websites, the platform also handles unstructured documents like PDFs, enabling extraction pipelines that combine both web and document data sources into one workflow.

Kadoa targets both technical and non-technical users, offering a no-code workflow builder for defining what data to extract and how often, alongside APIs and integrations for developers who want to pipe extracted data directly into their own systems, databases, or analytics tools. It's commonly used for use cases like competitive price monitoring, lead/company data enrichment, real estate listings aggregation, and general market intelligence gathering.

Best for

Kadoa suits data teams, growth marketers, and engineers who regularly need structured data pulled from websites or documents (competitor pricing, listings, public records, company data) but don't want to build and maintain custom scraping infrastructure. It's a good fit for startups and mid-size companies running market intelligence, lead enrichment, or price-monitoring workflows. Larger enterprises with high-volume, mission-critical extraction needs are also served through custom enterprise plans. It's less necessary for one-off, small-scale data pulls that don't justify a dedicated extraction tool.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Self-healing extraction significantly cuts down the ongoing engineering time typically spent fixing broken scrapers
  • Unified handling of both websites and unstructured documents (PDFs) means fewer tools needed for mixed data-source pipelines
  • Free tier and playground make it easy to validate the tool on a real use case before committing financially
  • No-code builder opens up web scraping to non-engineering teams like growth, sales ops, or market research
  • API-first delivery makes it straightforward to pipe extracted data into existing data warehouses or apps

Cons

  • Full pricing transparency is limited past the entry tiers, requiring a sales conversation for larger-scale needs
  • As with any scraping tool, target websites with aggressive anti-bot protections or paywalls can limit reliability
  • Users should be mindful of the legal and terms-of-service considerations of scraping certain sites, which is a shared risk across the category, not unique to Kadoa
  • Being a newer entrant, its library of pre-built connectors/integrations is smaller than some longer-established ETL players
  • Extraction accuracy on highly dynamic or JavaScript-heavy sites may still require some manual review or tuning

Frequently asked questions

Does Kadoa require coding knowledge?

No, Kadoa offers a no-code workflow builder for defining extraction targets, though it also provides an API for developers who want programmatic control.

What happens when a website Kadoa scrapes changes its layout?

Kadoa's AI models are designed to re-detect the relevant data fields automatically, a feature the company calls self-healing, reducing the need for manual scraper fixes.

Can Kadoa extract data from PDFs, not just websites?

Yes, Kadoa supports extraction from unstructured documents like PDFs in addition to live web pages.

Is there a free way to try Kadoa?

Yes, Kadoa offers a free first workflow and an AI web scraper playground for testing before committing to a paid plan.

How is extracted data delivered?

Data can be delivered via REST API, webhooks, or direct integrations into downstream databases and tools.

Is web scraping with Kadoa legal?

Legality depends on the target website's terms of service and applicable data/privacy laws; users are responsible for ensuring their specific use case complies with relevant regulations.
