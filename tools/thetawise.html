ThetaWise positions itself as a specialist alternative to general-purpose AI chatbots for STEM coursework, rather than a broad K-12 curriculum platform. Where Khan Academy is free and spans nearly every subject and grade level, and Photomath focuses narrowly on scanning and solving math problems, ThetaWise sits in between: a subscription product aimed at high school and college students working through calculus, physics, chemistry, statistics, biology, and economics, with tools built around actually finishing an assignment rather than just browsing lessons.

The product's core pitch is accuracy on harder, multi-step STEM problems where the company claims general models like ChatGPT are more prone to errors — a claim it supports mainly with its own testimonials and marketing copy rather than published, independent benchmarks. Its differentiator on the pedagogy side is the tutor mode, which offers hints and follow-up questions instead of an immediate final answer, addressing a common complaint about 'AI homework helpers' encouraging copy-paste use. Whether that meaningfully improves retention versus just being another solver depends heavily on whether a student actually chooses tutor mode over the direct-answer option, which the platform also offers.

Key features

01

Math & Science Solver

Accepts photographed, uploaded, or typed problems and returns a step-by-step solution, with support for math notation, tables, and document uploads.

02

AI Tutor Mode

A selectable response style that offers hints and guiding questions rather than an immediate answer, intended to walk students through reasoning instead of just giving a result.

03

AI Video Lessons

Generates short animated video explanations with narration on a requested topic, functioning as an on-demand mini-lecture.

04

Practice Quizzes

Auto-generates practice problems with instant feedback, letting students test themselves on a topic after solving or reviewing it.

05

Flashcards

Creates study flashcards automatically from uploaded course material or topics covered in chat.

06

Desmos Graphing & Whiteboard

Built-in graphing calculator and a drawing pad let students visualize functions or sketch out work without leaving the app.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Targets upper-level STEM coursework (calculus, physics, chemistry, statistics) rather than stopping at basic arithmetic, which is where several free scanner apps top out.
  • The tutor-mode option is a real design choice toward guided learning, not just a marketing label, since users can toggle between hint-based and direct-answer responses.
  • Bundles several study formats — solver, video lessons, flashcards, quizzes — so students can move from getting an answer to reviewing and self-testing without switching apps.
  • Built-in Desmos graphing and a whiteboard cut down on needing separate tools for visualizing functions or working problems by hand.
  • Multiple billing cadences (weekly, monthly, annual) give students a low-commitment way to try Pro during exam crunch periods.

Cons

  • The free tier is capped tightly enough that regular use for a full course load likely requires paying for Pro, unlike fully free competitors such as Khan Academy.
  • Accuracy and 'better than ChatGPT' claims are sourced from the company's own site and testimonials; there is no independent benchmark or peer-reviewed evaluation to verify them.
  • No reviews are currently listed on G2, and the app has only a handful of ratings on mobile app stores, so there isn't yet a meaningful body of independent user feedback to check claims against.
  • Subject coverage, while STEM-deep, is narrower than broad platforms like Khan Academy or Chegg, which also cover humanities, test prep, and non-STEM subjects.

Frequently asked questions

How much does ThetaWise cost?

There's a free tier with limited daily usage. Pro unlocks unlimited-style usage and advanced features starting at $9.99/week, or cheaper per-month rates of $19.99/month or $119.99/year if billed annually.

What subjects and grade levels does ThetaWise cover?

It's built for STEM coursework at the high school and college level, including calculus, physics, chemistry, statistics, biology, and economics — it's not a general K-12 curriculum tool.

How accurate is the math solver?

ThetaWise markets itself as more accurate than general chatbots like ChatGPT on complex STEM problems, but this is based on the company's own claims and testimonials rather than a published independent benchmark, so treat it as a marketing claim to verify yourself on your specific problem set.

What's the biggest limitation?

The free plan is limited enough that serious use likely means paying for Pro, and there isn't yet meaningful independent review data (G2 shows no reviews) to confirm the platform's accuracy and quality claims at scale.

How does it compare to Photomath, Khan Academy, or Chegg?

Photomath is narrower and mostly free, focused on scanning basic-to-intermediate math; Khan Academy is free and covers far more subjects and grade levels but with less AI-driven personalization; Chegg leans on textbook solutions and human expert Q&A. ThetaWise's niche is a paid, STEM-specific AI tutor with more built-in study formats (video lessons, flashcards, quizzes) in one app.

How do I get started?

You can sign up for the free plan directly at thetawise.ai with no credit card required, and upgrade to a Pro trial if you need higher usage limits or advanced features like PDF uploads and the advanced solver.
