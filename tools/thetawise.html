ThetaWise is a good fit for high school and college students who want an AI that handles calculus, physics, chemistry, statistics, biology and economics problems and can coach rather than just answer; skip it if you only need occasional help, since free-tier limits are unpublished and serious use means a paid plan, or if you need humanities or broad K-12 coverage. It takes typed, photographed or uploaded problems and returns step-by-step solutions, with a selectable 'Tutor me' mode that gives hints and questions instead of the final answer. Around that sit generated narrated video lessons, quizzes with graded feedback, flashcards, Desmos graphs and a drawing pad. Its claims of beating general chatbots on hard STEM problems come from the company itself; we found no independent benchmark.

Best for

STEM-focused high schoolers and undergraduates who will use a dedicated solver and study suite regularly and can pay for Pro. It is not for people who only need a few free answers a month, learners wanting non-STEM subjects or a full curriculum (Khan Academy is free and broader), or anyone who needs a guarantee of correct answers without checking the work.

Key features

01

Math & Science Solver

Accepts photographed, uploaded, or typed problems and returns a step-by-step solution, with support for math notation, tables, and document uploads.

02

AI Tutor Mode

A selectable response style that offers hints and guiding questions rather than an immediate answer, intended to walk students through reasoning instead of just giving a result.

03

AI Video Lessons

Generates short animated video explanations with narration on a requested topic, functioning as an on-demand mini-lecture.

04

Practice Quizzes

Auto-generates practice problems with instant feedback, letting students test themselves on a topic after solving or reviewing it.

05

Flashcards

Creates study flashcards automatically from uploaded course material or topics covered in chat.

06

Desmos Graphing & Whiteboard

Built-in graphing calculator and a drawing pad let students visualize functions or sketch out work without leaving the app.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Covers upper-level STEM (calculus, physics, chemistry, statistics, biology, economics) rather than stopping at basic algebra like many scan-and-solve apps.
  • Tutor mode is a real option: you choose 'Tutor me', 'Answer Directly' or a custom style, so you can pick guidance over copy-paste.
  • One app covers solving, narrated video lessons, quizzes with graded feedback and flashcards, so you can go from stuck to self-testing without switching tools.
  • Desmos graphing and a drawing pad are built into chats, which saves tabbing out for function plots or hand-worked steps.
  • Weekly, monthly and annual billing lets you pay for Pro only during exam crunch; the annual price works out to roughly $10 a month.

Cons

  • Free-tier limits are not published on the site, so you cannot tell in advance how far free use will go; serious use realistically needs Pro.
  • The cheapest Pro option is $9.99/week, which adds up to over $500 a year if left running; the monthly or annual plan is far cheaper.
  • Accuracy claims against general chatbots are the company's own; we found no independent benchmark, so check answers on your own problem types.
  • Video generation is metered by weekly credits (40 on Pro), which heavy video users may exhaust.
  • Narrower than Khan Academy or Chegg: no humanities or broad test-prep coverage.

Frequently asked questions

How much does ThetaWise cost?

Signing up is free and a 1-week trial is advertised. Pro is $9.99/week, $19.99/month or $119.99/year; Max is $59.99/month. Prices were checked on the live pricing page on 2026-10-31 and can change.

What does the free plan include?

The site says it is free to sign up and use, but it does not publish specific message or feature limits, so test it on your own coursework before paying.

What is the difference between Pro and Max?

Max adds higher limits: 120 weekly video credits instead of 40 and roughly 4x Pro's weekly message usage, at $59.99/month. Feature types are otherwise the same.

Will it just give me the answers?

It can: the response style can be set to 'Answer Directly'. 'Tutor me' instead gives hints and guiding questions. Which you use is your choice.

How accurate is it?

The company claims strong accuracy on hard STEM problems, but we found no independent benchmark. Verify results against your textbook or instructor.
