Podcast Transcript AI is a sensible cheap tool if you mostly want readable text from other people's public podcast episodes; skip it if you produce your own show and need to edit audio, or want meeting transcription. You pick an episode or upload a file, it returns a transcript with a summary, and you can copy, search and share it. The free tier works without an account but is rationed.

Free users get 3 transcripts a day, episodes up to 90 minutes, TXT export only, 3 AI chat messages per episode, and a random queue of 30 to 120 minutes. Pro removes those limits and costs $19.90 a year at launch pricing, adding speaker labels, SRT/VTT/PDF export, show notes, chapters, quote extraction, note-app sync and RSS automation. The Pro page does not state a monthly price or an accuracy figure, and episodes that are paywalled or protected may not be fetchable, so test one episode before paying.

Best for

Students, researchers, writers and heavy podcast listeners who want searchable text or summaries of public episodes at very low cost. Not for podcast producers who need to edit audio from the transcript, teams transcribing meetings or calls, or people who need a documented accuracy guarantee.

Key features

01

Link-based transcription

Paste a Spotify, Apple Podcasts or RSS link and the service fetches the episode and transcribes it. You can also upload your own audio. No account is required for basic use.

02

Summaries and AI chat

Each transcribed episode can come with a short summary. Free users get a small number of chat messages (3) to ask questions about a transcript; Pro lifts that limit.

03

Searchable transcript library

Transcripts are kept in a library you can search, and you can copy and share them. Public transcripts are cached so repeat requests for the same episode load faster.

04

Speaker labels and exports

Pro includes speaker diarization and exports to TXT, SRT, VTT and PDF. The free tier is limited to TXT.

05

Show notes, chapters and quotes

Pro generates show notes, chapters, quotable pull-quotes and a citation feature, useful for turning an episode into written content.

06

Automation and sync

Pro advertises RSS feed automation, bulk upload of several episodes at once, and syncing transcripts to Notion, Obsidian and Evernote. An API is also mentioned on the site.

07

Privacy and priority handling

Pro offers private transcriptions, priority queue processing and manual repair of failed jobs. The FAQ says transcripts are not sold or used to train third-party AI models.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Free tier needs no account, so one episode can be tried in a minute.
  • Choosing episodes from Spotify or Apple Podcasts avoids downloading and re-uploading audio.
  • Pro at $19.90 per year is far cheaper than typical transcription subscriptions, and early subscribers are said to keep the launch rate.
  • Pro includes things researchers and creators use: speaker labels, SRT/VTT, show notes, chapters and note-app sync.
  • Pro advertises manual rescue of failed episodes and priority repair of wrong transcripts, with failed episodes handled within 24 hours.

Cons

  • Free tier is restrictive: 3 per day, 90-minute cap, TXT only, and a random queue of up to two hours.
  • No transcript editor, and no accuracy figure is stated on the Pro page, so check output on your own audio, especially with accents or overlapping speech.
  • Speaker labels, subtitle exports and show notes are Pro-only.
  • Monthly price and bulk-upload limit (5-10 episodes) are unclear; there is no dedicated pricing page.
  • Pro is billed through Whop, with crypto via NOWPayments as an alternative, which some buyers may not want.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an account?

Not for basic free transcription. Pro is purchased through Whop.

What are the free tier limits?

3 transcriptions per day, episodes up to 90 minutes, TXT export only, 3 AI chat messages per episode, and a queue of 30 to 120 minutes.

What does Pro cost?

$19.90 per year (about $1.66 a month), described as launch pricing. A monthly price is not listed on the Pro page.

What sources does it support?

Spotify and Apple Podcasts episodes, plus uploaded audio; the earlier listing also mentioned RSS links, and Pro lists RSS feed automation.

What if a transcript fails or is wrong?

Pro members can request manual rescue of failed episodes (handled within 24 hours) or flag bad sections for a human re-run. Free users get no recovery.
