Pickaxe is a good pick for consultants, agencies and small businesses who want to turn an AI agent into a branded product they can embed or sell, and a poor pick for engineers needing complex multi-step logic or anyone wanting a free plan to experiment on. It is a no-code builder where you configure agents, attach knowledge documents, connect tools such as Zapier, HubSpot, Slack or Google Sheets, and publish them through embeds, email, messaging apps or a white-labelled portal. Its distinctive part is the commercial layer: built-in billing, access control, usage tracking and a revenue-share model, where you keep 90 to 98 percent depending on plan. The homepage did not name which language models power agents, though the pricing page mentions access to the latest AI models.

Best for

Best for solo founders, coaches, consultants and small agencies who want to sell or deploy AI agents for clients under their own brand. Not for developers who need full control over orchestration, teams wanting a free tier to prototype, or very high-traffic public bots where usage credits could add up.

Key features

01

No-code agent builder

Configure agents and tools through prompts and settings rather than code, for uses like lead qualification, support, intake and content generation.

02

Knowledge base

Upload documents per workspace so agents answer from your own material; limits are 50 documents per workspace on Gold and unlimited on Pro.

03

Integrations and actions

Connect to tools such as Zapier, HubSpot, Attio, Google Sheets, Slack, Airtable, ClickUp and Todoist. Gold allows 3 actions per agent.

04

Branded portals and monetization

Sell agents through white-labelled portals with custom domain, billing and access control; Pickaxe takes a share of revenue that shrinks on higher plans.

05

Multi-channel deployment

Embeds and email on all paid plans; Telegram and Discord on Gold (one each), with Slack, WhatsApp and webhooks on Pro and above.

06

Analytics and collaboration

Customer activity analytics, plus collaborators per workspace (1 on Gold, 4 on Pro, 5+ on Business).

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Lets non-developers ship a working agent, quiz or assistant quickly, then publish it to a site, email or chat channels.
  • Billing, access control and white-labelling are built in, which removes a lot of glue work if you resell agents.
  • Pro tier removes most caps: unlimited workspaces, documents and action connections.
  • Integrations with common marketing and ops tools cover typical lead and support workflows.

Cons

  • The cheapest plan is $37/month and its limits (3 workspaces, 3 actions per agent, 1 collaborator) bite quickly for agencies.
  • Slack, WhatsApp, webhooks and Embed SSO require Pro at $147/month.
  • Pickaxe keeps 2 to 10 percent of revenue you earn, depending on tier.
  • Heavy usage is metered through credits, so costs for busy agents are hard to predict; actual per-message consumption was not verified.
  • Business is $597/month with a six-month minimum, a large commitment for small teams.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need coding experience to use Pickaxe?

No. Agents are built through a visual, prompt-based setup, and integrations are configured in the builder.

Is there a free plan?

None was listed on the pricing page we checked on 2026-11-03, which showed Gold, Pro and Business. Confirm on the site whether a trial exists.

Can I sell access to the agents I build?

Yes. Branded portals include billing and access control, and Pickaxe retains a share of revenue (you keep 90% on Gold, 92% on Pro, up to 98% on Business).

Which AI models does it use?

The pricing page says plans include access to the latest AI models, but the homepage names none, so check the app for the current list.

How does annual billing work?

Annual billing saves 20%: Gold is $29/month and Pro $116/month. Business shows only a monthly price with a six-month minimum.
