Gong pioneered the 'revenue intelligence' category by recording and transcribing sales calls, emails, and meetings, then applying AI to extract patterns across thousands of conversations. Instead of relying on reps to self-report what happened in a deal, managers get an objective record plus AI-driven analysis of what was discussed, what risks are present, and how the deal compares to historical patterns that led to wins or losses.

Beyond individual deal visibility, Gong is heavily used for coaching: managers can review call scorecards, talk-time ratios, and competitor mentions to identify what top performers do differently and share that with the wider team. Its forecasting tools layer AI-driven deal-risk signals on top of traditional CRM pipeline data to give leadership a more grounded view of what's likely to close.

Gong integrates tightly with CRMs (especially Salesforce and HubSpot), calendars, dialers, and email, making it a hub that most other sales tools feed into or read from. This depth of integration and analysis is a major reason it has become a default choice for larger sales organizations, though it comes at a price point that puts it out of reach for many small teams.

Best for

Gong is best suited to mid-market and enterprise B2B sales organizations with dedicated sales management layers who want to standardize coaching, deal review, and forecasting using AI-analyzed conversation data. It's a strong fit for RevOps and sales leadership teams already invested in Salesforce or HubSpot who want deeper visibility into what's actually happening on calls. It's less cost-effective for very small teams or those without a formal coaching and forecasting process to plug the insights into.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Transcription and conversation analysis quality is a category benchmark, with strong accuracy across accents and industry jargon
  • Coaching tools (scorecards, trackers, talk-time analysis) give managers concrete, data-backed ways to develop reps
  • Forecasting features add a layer of conversation-based signal on top of standard CRM pipeline data, improving forecast accuracy for many teams
  • Mature integration ecosystem means it fits into most existing sales tech stacks without major disruption

Cons

  • Enterprise-oriented pricing is a common barrier for startups and small sales teams evaluating the platform
  • The volume of data and insights can be overwhelming without dedicated admin time to configure dashboards and alerts
  • Recording calls raises compliance and consent considerations that vary by region and require careful rollout
  • Some smaller or highly technical sales teams find the coaching-oriented feature set less relevant to their motion

Frequently asked questions

What does Gong actually record?

Gong records and transcribes sales calls, video meetings, and can also analyze email threads to build a full picture of each deal's conversation history.

How much does Gong cost?

Gong uses custom, quote-based pricing tied to seat count and deployment scope; there's no public self-serve pricing.

Does Gong integrate with Salesforce?

Yes, Gong has deep, widely used integrations with Salesforce and HubSpot, syncing call insights and activity automatically.

Can Gong help with sales forecasting?

Yes, Gong layers AI-driven deal-risk analysis from conversations on top of CRM pipeline data to improve forecast accuracy.

Is Gong only for large enterprises?

While it's most popular with mid-market and enterprise teams, smaller teams do use it, though pricing can be a limiting factor.

Are there compliance concerns with recording calls?

Yes, call recording consent laws vary by region, so companies need to configure notifications and consent workflows appropriately.
