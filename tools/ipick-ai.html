iPick.ai is a research dashboard, not a document tool or a spreadsheet chatbot. Its homepage describes searching and screening over 6,000 stocks and ETFs, live tracking of major indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow Jones, Russell 2000), a Day Movers list, sector-level Top Industry Performers, and an Investment Track feature for lining up similar companies. Above the free tier, its Agentic AI (Pro) mode runs an autonomous process that buckets tickers into categories it calls Power Picks, Hidden Gems, Value Picks, and Bear Picks, alongside AI-written synthetic news summaries and a ticker ranking system. Per its team page, the company was founded by Dr. Yunhong Zhou and Huajun Zeng, both with backgrounds at Microsoft, Alibaba, Baidu, eBay, and Yahoo, so this reads like a search-and-ranking engineering team pointed at equity markets rather than a finance-first shop with an analyst bench behind it.

What actually separates it from the two nearest tools already in this category is worth spelling out rather than hand-waving. Hebbia is a document-reasoning platform: it reads and reasons over huge sets of filings, contracts, and PDFs for institutional due diligence, and it is priced and sold for hedge funds, banks, and private equity, not for someone who wants to glance at day movers before coffee. It has no interest in giving you a ticker page or a market index widget, because that is not the job it is built for. Julius AI sits on the opposite side: it is a general-purpose data-analyst chatbot that turns whatever spreadsheet or CSV you upload into charts and insights, with zero built-in financial data of its own, ask it about a stock and it only knows what you feed it. iPick.ai's actual differentiator is that the market data is already wired in on day one: indices, movers, sector performance, and individual ticker pages are there the moment you open the site, no uploading a file and no enterprise sales call required.

The honest caveat applies to the category, not just this product. Synthetic news summaries and algorithmically labeled Power Picks or Hidden Gems are pattern-matching and ranking output, not licensed investment advice, and the site does not publish the methodology behind how a stock lands in one bucket versus another. Market data on a consumer-facing tool like this can lag the feeds professionals pay for, and any AI system can simply be wrong about a company's fundamentals or miss context a human analyst would catch. Treat it as a starting point for idea generation and screening, and do your own follow-up before acting on anything it surfaces.

Best for

Choose iPick.ai over Hebbia and Julius AI if you are a retail or prosumer investor who wants one dashboard for indices, day movers, sector performance, and ticker-level research without paying institutional prices or having to upload your own data first. Skip it if you are an institutional analyst or a due-diligence team that needs to reason across thousands of pages of filings and contracts, that is squarely Hebbia's job and iPick.ai is not built for document depth. Skip it too if what you actually want is a general chatbot for messy spreadsheets that have nothing to do with public markets, Julius AI fits that better and does not tie you to financial-market framing you do not need. And treat the Agentic AI picks as a screening aid, not a signal to trade on, given the lack of published methodology and the usual limits of AI-generated market analysis.

Key features

01

AI Search

Search and query across more than 6,000 stocks and ETFs to research a company or find names matching criteria, rather than manually digging through separate data providers.

02

Market Indices and Day Movers

Live tracking of major indices, S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow Jones, Russell 2000, the VIX volatility index, and BTC/USD, plus a Day Movers view surfacing the stocks with the biggest price swings that session.

03

Top Industry Performers

A sector view that filters the best and worst performing companies by industry and by timeframe, useful for spotting rotation or momentum without building your own screen.

04

Investment Track

A side-by-side comparison tool for lining up similar companies within the same industry, meant to help with relative valuation and peer comparison.

05

Agentic AI (Pro) categorized picks

An autonomous AI process available on the Pro tier that sorts tickers into labeled buckets, Power Picks, Hidden Gems, Value Picks, and Bear Picks, the site does not publish the exact scoring logic behind these labels.

06

Synthetic news summaries and Ticker Ranker

AI-generated summaries of news tied to a given ticker, paired with a ranking system that scores and orders tickers, intended to compress research time rather than replace reading primary sources.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Built-in market data (indices, movers, sector performance, ticker pages) means there is no setup step before you can start researching, unlike tools that need you to upload your own dataset
  • Founding team's background is in search and ranking engineering at large tech companies, which shows in the product's screening and ranking-first design rather than a document-review interface
  • Free tier is a real way to try AI search and market browsing before committing to a paid plan
  • Purpose-built for individual investors rather than trying to be a general-purpose analytics tool stretched to cover finance as an afterthought

Cons

  • No public pricing page anywhere on the site, you have to create an account and log in just to learn what Premium or Agentic AI (Pro) cost, which is a friction point compared to tools that post pricing openly
  • The methodology behind Power Picks, Hidden Gems, Value Picks, and Bear Picks is not explained on the site, so it is hard to judge how much to trust a given label without treating it as a black box
  • As a consumer-facing product, its market data and analysis should be assumed to lag the real-time, professional-grade feeds that institutional tools and brokerages use
  • No sign of broker integration, paper trading, or portfolio execution features on the site, it is a research and screening layer, not a place to act on the research

Frequently asked questions

What data does iPick.ai actually cover?

Per its site, iPick.ai covers more than 6,000 stocks and ETFs, major indices such as the S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow Jones, and Russell 2000, the VIX volatility index, and at least one crypto pair, BTC/USD.

Is iPick.ai free to use?

There is a free tier, but Premium and Agentic AI (Pro) pricing is not published publicly, you only see the actual cost after signing up and logging in.

What are Power Picks, Hidden Gems, Value Picks, and Bear Picks?

These are AI-generated categories from the Agentic AI (Pro) tier that sort stocks into buckets. The site does not publish the scoring methodology behind how a stock lands in each category.

Is iPick.ai a substitute for a financial advisor?

No. Its AI rankings and synthetic news summaries are research and screening aids, not licensed investment advice, and like any AI-generated analysis they can be wrong or based on lagging data.

How is iPick.ai different from Hebbia?

Hebbia is a document-reasoning platform built for institutional due diligence over large sets of filings and contracts, priced for hedge funds, banks, and private equity. iPick.ai is a retail-facing app with market data, indices, movers, ticker pages, built in from the start, with no document upload required.

How is iPick.ai different from Julius AI?

Julius AI is a general-purpose data-analyst chatbot for spreadsheets and CSVs you provide yourself, with no built-in financial data. iPick.ai comes with tickers, indices, and market data already wired in, so you are researching stocks immediately rather than uploading your own dataset first.
