AudioStack (built by Aflorithmic Labs Ltd.) is described on its own site as "the agentic audio production platform for media and advertising," turning a creative brief or raw text into broadcast-ready audio automatically and at scale. Rather than being a single voice generator, it's an orchestration layer that sits on top of 17+ third-party voice and music model providers (ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Google, Microsoft Azure, Amazon Polly, Cartesia, Resemble, WellSaid Labs, and others), giving customers access to 2,600+ voices and over 1,000 sonic assets through one platform. It's aimed squarely at enterprise use cases: radio and streaming ad production, dynamic/personalized ad variants at programmatic scale, embedded audio generation inside other software products, and long-form content like audiobooks and serialized audio fiction.

AudioStack offers its technology two ways: as a self-serve console (app.audiostack.ai) where a team member types a brief and gets broadcast-ready audio without engineering involvement, or as an embeddable API/engine that other platforms integrate directly (a single POST request returns finished, white-labelled audio). Its named products include InstantCreative (self-serve brief-to-audio), DynamicCreative (one asset generating thousands of variants tied to a VAST ad tag), CreativeEngine (the embeddable API layer), and StoryEngine (long-form text-to-audio for audiobooks). The company's customer base — Bloomberg, Omnicom Media Group, Publicis Groupe, Dentsu, Prisa, Global, NewsCorp, Porsche, and PocketFM among its published logos — signals it is built for large agencies, publishers, and adtech platforms rather than individual creators, and it holds SOC2, GDPR, and Content Authenticity Initiative (CAI) credentials reflecting its enterprise/media-industry focus.

Best for

AudioStack is built for agencies, adtech platforms, broadcasters, and publishers that need to produce audio ads, dynamic ad variants, or long-form audio content programmatically at scale — its case studies (hyperlocal ad campaigns, serialized audio fiction, radio sales) reflect enterprise and media-industry buyers. It's a poor fit for solo creators, podcasters, or small teams looking for simple self-serve pricing, since AudioStack requires an enterprise sales conversation and is architected around API/embedded integration rather than a lightweight consumer app.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • AudioStack's client roster (Bloomberg, Omnicom, Publicis, Dentsu, Prisa, NewsCorp, Porsche, PocketFM) indicates it is proven in production at large media and advertising organizations, not just a demo-stage product.
  • A published case study reports 24,000+ hyperlocal ads generated in 3 days, and the platform claims up to 90% faster production than traditional audio workflows, which is a concrete efficiency signal for agencies.
  • Because it orchestrates 17+ voice/music providers rather than building its own single model, customers get provider flexibility and redundancy instead of lock-in to one voice vendor.
  • A G2 reviewer specifically praised how a simple API call replaced manual podcast-style production from blog content, calling it "much much better than browser's simple TTS."
  • Offering both a self-serve console and a fully embeddable API means it can serve both non-technical marketing teams and engineering-led product integrations with the same underlying engine.

Cons

  • Pricing is entirely opaque on the public site — there's no rate card, so prospective customers must go through a sales/demo process to learn cost, which slows evaluation for smaller teams.
  • A G2 review notes the free plan's output carries a watermark, making it hard to properly evaluate real audio quality before committing budget.
  • Another G2 reviewer flagged that SDK support is limited to JavaScript and Python, which could be a blocker for teams standardized on other languages.
  • The credits-based billing model was described by a reviewer as difficult to map to a simple "per audio" cost basis, complicating budget planning for high-volume users.
  • One reviewer noted certain native-language audio packs weren't "up to the mark," suggesting quality can vary by language/voice provider within the aggregated catalog.

What reviewers say

Frequently asked questions

Does AudioStack publish public pricing?

No, pricing is not published on the site; prospective customers need to book a demo and go through a sales process, with billing reportedly based on a credits system.

Who is AudioStack built for?

It's built for agencies, adtech platforms, radio and streaming publishers, and brands that need programmatic, scalable audio production, not individual creators.

How many voices does AudioStack offer?

It provides access to 2,600+ voices and more than 1,000 sonic assets aggregated across 17 voice/music providers, including ElevenLabs, OpenAI, Google, and Amazon Polly.

Can AudioStack be embedded into another product?

Yes, its CreativeEngine product is an API that other platforms can call directly to generate white-labelled audio inside their own product without users leaving the flow.

What company operates AudioStack?

AudioStack is operated by Aflorithmic Labs Ltd., as noted in the site's copyright footer.

Is there a free way to try AudioStack?

A demo/trial is available, though a G2 reviewer noted that free-tier output includes a watermark, limiting full evaluation before purchase.
