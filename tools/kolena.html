Kolena is an AI automation platform, founded by CEO Mohamed Elgendy and CTO Andrew Shi (both with prior AI/infrastructure experience at companies including Amazon, Rakuten, and Palantir), that focuses on turning document-heavy business processes into automated operations. Rather than positioning itself as a general chatbot or copilot, Kolena runs entire document workflows end-to-end: it pulls input files from a company's Google Drive, SharePoint, or inbox, applies a team's specific extraction and analysis logic, generates output in a custom template, and delivers results to a defined destination — processing hundreds or thousands of documents in parallel in minutes rather than weeks. The platform serves four core verticals: real estate (lease abstraction, deal underwriting, mortgage due diligence), financial services (investment memos, PE fund modeling, risk assessment), insurance (claims processing, loss run analysis, policy review), and banking (loan underwriting, compliance testing, complaints resolution).

Kolena's key differentiator is transparency: every AI output is designed to include reasoning and citations back to source documents, addressing the 'black box' concern common in enterprise AI adoption, and the platform maintains a full audit trail for compliance-sensitive industries. It also functions as living process documentation — when a company updates a policy, its Kolena agents realign automatically, and staff can query institutional knowledge in plain language (e.g., 'what's our renewal notice period?'). The company reports customer outcomes such as a 96% reduction in UCC filing review labor at Milestone Bank and roughly $100,000 in efficiency gains across 58 leases for Paragon Loan Advisors. Kolena is SOC 2, HIPAA, and PCI compliant, offers a library of hundreds of expert-verified prebuilt agents, and continuously benchmarks new LLMs so customers can upgrade to the most accurate, cost-efficient model with one click rather than re-testing accuracy themselves.

Best for

Kolena is a strong fit for real estate firms, insurers, banks, and financial-services teams that process high volumes of documents — leases, claims, loan packages, compliance files — and need an auditable, citation-backed AI system rather than a generic chatbot. It is a weak fit for general productivity users, small teams without document-heavy workflows, or anyone outside its core verticals looking for self-serve, transparently priced software.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Kolena automates complete document workflows end to end (ingest to export) rather than just answering questions about a single uploaded file, which differentiates it from generic AI chat tools.
  • Customer testimonials cite substantial measurable results, including a 96% reduction in UCC filing review labor and turnaround dropping from five days to a few hours at Milestone Bank.
  • Every output includes citations and reasoning, which several customers (e.g., a real-estate due-diligence lead) credit with getting their risk or compliance committees comfortable approving AI-driven workflows.
  • The prebuilt agent library (hundreds of verified agents across real estate, insurance, banking, and lending) lowers the barrier to launching automation without in-house AI expertise.
  • Founders bring relevant enterprise AI/infrastructure backgrounds from Amazon, Rakuten, and Palantir, and the company maintains SOC 2, HIPAA, and PCI compliance for regulated industries.

Cons

  • Kolena does not publish pricing tiers or per-seat costs on its site; prospective customers must request a demo or trial to get a quote, which slows evaluation for smaller teams.
  • The platform is narrowly focused on real estate, insurance, banking, and financial-services document workflows, so it's not a fit for general productivity or cross-industry automation needs.
  • No independent third-party review data (G2, Capterra, Trustpilot) was publicly available at the time of research, making it harder to verify satisfaction at scale beyond the vendor's own case studies.
  • As an agent-based automation platform tied to specific document types and templates, initial setup for a new use case likely requires configuration time before an organization sees full end-to-end automation.
