Coveo (TSX: CVO) is a Canadian enterprise software company that builds an AI-driven relevance platform, marketed as the Coveo Relevance Cloud. At its core is a machine-learning engine that indexes content from hundreds of connectors (CRMs, CMSs, ticketing systems, file stores, product catalogs) and ranks results based on relevance signals learned from user behavior, rather than static rules.

The platform is sold across three main product lines: Coveo for Service (case deflection, agent assist, self-service knowledge search), Coveo for Commerce (personalized product search, recommendations, merchandising), and Coveo for Workplace (unified internal search across enterprise tools). In recent years Coveo has layered generative AI on top of this relevance layer with Coveo Relevance Generative Answering, which produces grounded, cited answers from enterprise content rather than relying on an LLM's raw training data alone.

Coveo primarily targets mid-market and large enterprises with complex, multi-source content environments, and it is typically deployed by IT, digital experience, or customer support leadership teams rather than purchased as a lightweight self-serve tool.

Best for

Coveo is built for mid-market and large enterprises that manage large, fragmented content ecosystems and need AI-driven relevance across support, ecommerce, or internal knowledge search. It suits organizations with dedicated digital experience, IT, or customer support platform teams who can manage connector setup and ongoing relevance tuning. It is a strong fit for companies already running complex CRM, CMS, or ecommerce stacks who want one relevance layer across them. It is generally not the right choice for small businesses or teams wanting a quick, low-cost search widget.

Key features

01

Unified Relevance Engine

Indexes and ranks content from hundreds of connectors (Salesforce, ServiceNow, SharePoint, product catalogs, and more) using machine learning rather than static keyword rules.

02

Generative Answering

Produces AI-generated, cited answers grounded in a company's own indexed content, reducing hallucination risk versus a standalone LLM.

03

Case Deflection & Agent Assist

Surfaces relevant knowledge articles and suggested responses to customers and support agents to reduce ticket volume and resolution time.

04

Commerce Search & Recommendations

Delivers personalized product search results, autocomplete, and merchandising recommendations tuned to individual shopper behavior.

05

Workplace Search

Provides a single search layer across internal wikis, file shares, and business applications so employees can find information without switching tools.

06

Relevance Analytics & ML Tuning

Continuously learns from click and conversion data to automatically refine ranking models over time, with dashboards to monitor search performance.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • The connector library covers most major enterprise systems out of the box, reducing custom integration work for large IT environments.
  • Relevance ranking is powered by machine learning trained on real usage patterns, so results improve over time rather than requiring manual rule maintenance.
  • Generative Answering is grounded in indexed enterprise content with citations, which helps address hallucination concerns common with generic LLM chatbots.
  • Supports multiple business functions (support, commerce, workplace) on one underlying platform, which can reduce vendor sprawl for large organizations.
  • Established, publicly traded vendor with a long track record in enterprise search, which reduces vendor-viability risk for large buyers.

Cons

  • Pricing is entirely custom and quote-based, making it hard for prospective buyers to budget or compare costs without a sales conversation.
  • Initial setup, connector configuration, and relevance tuning typically require a dedicated implementation project rather than a quick self-serve rollout.
  • The platform's depth and configurability can be more complexity than smaller organizations or simple use cases actually need.
  • As with most enterprise platforms, meaningful ROI depends on ongoing tuning and content governance, not just initial deployment.
  • Smaller teams evaluating lightweight site-search or FAQ bots may find simpler, cheaper point solutions sufficient instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is Coveo used for?

Coveo is used to power AI-driven search, recommendations, and generative answering across customer support, ecommerce, and internal workplace applications by indexing and ranking content from many connected systems.

How much does Coveo cost?

Coveo does not publish fixed pricing; costs are quoted individually based on the product line, content volume, traffic, and number of connectors needed.

Does Coveo offer generative AI features?

Yes, Coveo Relevance Generative Answering produces AI answers grounded in a company's own indexed content, with citations back to source documents.

Is Coveo suitable for small businesses?

Coveo is primarily built for mid-market and enterprise use cases with complex content environments; it is generally not cost-effective or necessary for very small teams.

What systems can Coveo connect to?

Coveo offers connectors for hundreds of systems including Salesforce, ServiceNow, SharePoint, Adobe Experience Manager, and various ecommerce and file-storage platforms.

Does Coveo replace a help desk or CMS?

No, Coveo layers on top of existing systems like help desks, CMSs, and product catalogs to unify search and relevance rather than replacing them.
