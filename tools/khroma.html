Khroma is a great fit for solo designers and developers who want a stream of color pairings tuned to their own taste, and a poor fit for teams that need shared libraries, brand governance or file integrations. It trains a small neural network in your browser on colors you pick, then generates combinations it expects you to like.

You can view results as typography pairs, gradients, palettes or on a custom image, filter by hue, tint and value, and search by hex or RGB. Each pair shows color names, hex, RGB, CSS code and a WCAG accessibility rating, and you can save favorites to a collection.

Best for

Designers, front-end developers and hobbyists who want fast, personalized color inspiration, particularly for readable text and background pairs. Not for teams needing shared palettes, brand-wide color governance, or direct plugin export into design software; not for people who want palettes derived from a mood or brand keyword.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Personal training makes the feed noticeably more relevant than random generators.
  • WCAG rating next to every pair helps you pick readable text and background colors quickly.
  • Several view modes (typography, gradient, image) let you judge a combination in context.
  • Provides ready-to-paste hex, RGB and CSS values.

Cons

  • No evidence of team collaboration or shared libraries on the site.
  • Output is combinations of a few colors, not a full brand palette with tints and roles.
  • Export routes beyond copying values are not documented, so verify before relying on it in a pipeline.
  • Pricing and account requirements could not be confirmed today; check the site before assuming it is free.

Frequently asked questions

Is Khroma free?

The homepage shows no prices or paid plans, and it has historically been free, but we could not verify pricing today.

How does it personalize results?

You select colors you like during training and a neural network in your browser learns your preferences to generate and filter combinations.

Can I check accessibility?

Yes, each color pair shows a WCAG accessibility rating along with hex, RGB and CSS code.

Can I export palettes to Figma or Adobe?

No direct integration is documented; you can copy the hex, RGB or CSS values into any tool.
