Adobe LLM Optimizer launched in October 2025 as Adobe's answer to the shift from clicks to AI-generated answers, and by mid-2026 Adobe began rebranding it to Adobe Brand Visibility, folding in market intelligence from Semrush (which Adobe acquired) alongside its own optimization engine. It did not start as a standalone SEO company's bolt-on feature the way Conductor or BrightEdge's AI tools did; instead it was built from day one inside Adobe Experience Cloud, drawing on Adobe's existing SEO heritage through the Semrush acquisition plus Adobe's own content, CDN, and analytics infrastructure (Experience Manager, Adobe Analytics, Customer Journey Analytics).

Its core differentiator is closed-loop deployment: rather than just reporting that a brand is missing from AI answers, LLM Optimizer can push fixes (structured data, missing content, crawler access issues) live at the CDN edge in minutes, with instant rollback, and then trace whether that specific change moved AI-referred traffic and revenue inside Adobe Analytics. Competitors like Conductor and BrightEdge focus more heavily on insight and reporting that a human then has to implement elsewhere; Adobe's pitch is that insight and implementation live in the same system, which appeals to enterprises already running their web presence on Adobe infrastructure but is far less compelling for brands on other CMS/CDN stacks.

Best for

Adobe LLM Optimizer is the right fit for large enterprises already running Experience Manager, Adobe Analytics, or Customer Journey Analytics, and who want AI-search visibility management to plug directly into that existing content and measurement stack without adding a new reporting silo. Smaller teams, self-serve buyers, or any brand not already invested in the Adobe ecosystem should look elsewhere: there's no published pricing, no free trial, a five- or six-figure minimum commitment, and much of the platform's differentiated value (CDN-edge auto-optimize) simply doesn't apply outside Adobe's infrastructure.

Key features

01

Auto-identify, auto-suggest, auto-optimize

A three-stage workflow that first detects visibility gaps and crawler issues, then proposes fixes, then implements approved changes automatically.

02

CDN-edge deployment

Serves AI-optimized versions of pages directly to AI crawlers at the CDN layer, bypassing the normal CMS publish cycle, with one-click rollback.

03

Cross-engine share-of-voice tracking

Monitors brand presence and citation share across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, and Microsoft Copilot using a database of nearly 300 million AI search prompts.

04

Agentic crawler analytics

Reads CDN logs to show exactly when and how AI crawlers and agents access a site's content, distinct from simply reporting on rankings.

05

Content gap and topic cluster mapping

Identifies where a brand's SEO authority should be earning AI citations but isn't, and prioritizes content briefs by projected topic-cluster impact.

06

Off-site authority generation

Surfaces opportunities on Wikipedia, YouTube, Reddit, and earned media to build the third-party citation authority that AI engines pull from.

07

Business Impact / revenue attribution

Connects AI-driven visibility and traffic changes to Adobe Analytics and Customer Journey Analytics so teams can prove GEO work moved real business outcomes.

08

Cross-team workspace

Unifies SEO, PR, social, and affiliate teams around shared AI-visibility priorities, progress tracking, and automated issue routing.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Uniquely combines AI-visibility insight with one-click implementation at the CDN edge, cutting the usual gap between finding a problem and fixing it.
  • Revenue attribution runs natively through Adobe Analytics and Customer Journey Analytics, so GEO impact doesn't live in a reporting silo separate from the rest of marketing performance.
  • Draws on a very large prompt and clickstream dataset (reported near 300 million prompts) for market-trend and share-of-voice benchmarking.
  • Handles technical GEO issues like blocked crawlers, missing structured data, and 404s without requiring engineering tickets.
  • Strong fit for organizations that already run content and campaigns through Adobe Experience Cloud, since it shares data and workflows with tools they already use.
  • Adobe's scale and support resources reduce the risk of an unsupported point solution.

Cons

  • Pricing is entirely opaque on the public site; third-party estimates put annual cost around $115,000, with per-prompt rates reportedly quoted near €60/prompt/year, but nothing is confirmed by Adobe.
  • The 1,000-prompt annual minimum and 200-prompt expansion increments mean costs can escalate quickly for brands tracking broad topic coverage.
  • As a late entrant (launched October 2025) it lacks the multi-year AEO track record that dedicated startups in this space have built.
  • Its most powerful capability, CDN-edge auto-deployment, delivers the most value to brands already on Adobe Experience Manager; value is diminished on non-Adobe CMS/CDN stacks.
  • Public review data is essentially nonexistent (0 reviews on G2 and Capterra as of August 2026), so prospective buyers can't yet lean on peer validation the way they can with Conductor or BrightEdge.
  • Overkill and cost-prohibitive for small or mid-sized teams; this is squarely an enterprise, sales-assisted purchase with a long procurement cycle.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Adobe LLM Optimizer cost?

Adobe does not publish pricing. It sells via annual contract on a prompt-based model with a minimum of 1,000 tracked prompts per year, expandable in 200-prompt increments. Third-party estimates put typical annual spend around $115,000, but these are unofficial figures — contact Adobe sales for a real quote.

How is this different from a dedicated AEO/GEO tool?

Unlike point solutions that only report on AI visibility, Adobe LLM Optimizer can implement fixes directly, deploying AI-optimized content at the CDN edge without engineering involvement, and it ties results back to Adobe Analytics for revenue attribution. The tradeoff is that its deepest capabilities are most powerful for brands already running Adobe Experience Manager.

Which AI engines does it track?

It tracks brand presence and share-of-voice across ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Mode, and Microsoft Copilot, alongside traditional Google AI Overviews.

Is this a good fit for smaller teams?

No. It's built and priced for enterprises, with a five-figure-plus minimum commitment, an annual contract, and a sales-led buying process. Smaller teams are better served by self-serve AEO tools with transparent monthly pricing.

Do I need Adobe Experience Manager to use it?

No, but the CDN-edge auto-optimize capability, which is the product's biggest differentiator, delivers its full value primarily to brands running on Adobe's content and CDN infrastructure.

Is Adobe LLM Optimizer being renamed?

Yes. Adobe has been transitioning the product to the name Adobe Brand Visibility through 2026, combining the original LLM Optimizer engine with Semrush market intelligence following Adobe's acquisition of Semrush.
