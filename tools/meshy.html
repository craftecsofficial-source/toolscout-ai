Meshy is a good fit for indie game developers, 3D-printing hobbyists and designers who want quick draft or prototype 3D assets without learning a modeling package; it is a poor fit for engineers who need dimensionally exact, cleanup-free hard-surface or CAD-grade geometry. It generates 3D models from a text prompt or a photo or sketch, then lets you texture, rig and animate them in the same browser tool.

Beyond generation, it applies PBR texture sets (albedo, normal, metallic, roughness), auto-rigs humanoid and quadruped characters, and offers an animation library (the homepage cites 600+ motion presets). For printing, it advertises watertight meshes, repair, auto-splitting of large models and export to slicers such as Bambu Studio and OrcaSlicer. Developers get an API, CLI and MCP server, and there are plugins for Blender, Unity, Unreal and Godot. Meshy is operated by Meshy LLC; the site mentions no acquisition. Traction figures on the homepage (12M+ users, 100M+ models) are the company's own claims and were not independently verified.

Best for

Good for indie game developers, hobbyist 3D printers, toy and product designers, and teams prototyping props or characters quickly. Not for mechanical or CAD engineers who need precise dimensions, nor for studios that need production-ready topology with no retopology step, nor for anyone who cannot accept AI-generated assets in their pipeline. Free-plan users must credit Meshy under CC BY 4.0.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Generation, texturing, rigging, animation and print prep live in one tool, so a rough prop or character can go from prompt to rigged model without switching software.
  • The free plan needs no card and allows real testing, including commercial use with attribution.
  • Broad export formats (FBX, OBJ, GLB, USDZ, STL) and engine and slicer plugins reduce import friction.
  • API, CLI and MCP server make it scriptable for automated asset pipelines.
  • Paid plans give private, customer-owned assets, which matters for commercial projects.

Cons

  • Hard-surface, mechanical and precise geometry typically needs manual retopology or cleanup, so it is not a CAD substitute.
  • Credits are consumed per generation and per texture or rig step, so iterating on shape and look burns allowance quickly.
  • The free plan is low priority, capped at 10 assets/month and lacks features such as remesh and multi-view input.
  • Free output requires attribution under CC BY 4.0 and is not private.
  • The pricing page lists the same per-month price for monthly and yearly billing despite an FAQ mention of annual discounts, so the true annual saving could not be verified.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Meshy models commercially?

Yes. Free-plan output is CC BY 4.0, so commercial use needs attribution. Paid plans list assets as private and customer-owned.

What does the free plan include?

100 credits a month, 1 concurrent task, low queue priority, up to 10 assets a month, and no card required. Some features like remesh and multi-view image-to-3D are paid only.

Which formats can I export?

FBX, OBJ, GLB, USDZ and STL are listed on the homepage.

Is there an API?

Yes, on paid plans, along with a CLI and MCP server. Check the pricing page for which tiers include it.

Is it good for CAD or precise mechanical parts?

Not really. Expect to clean up or rebuild hard-surface and dimension-critical models in a dedicated tool.
