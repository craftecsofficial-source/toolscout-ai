ContentOptima suits editors and SEOs who want a cheap, repeatable checklist for one page at a time before publishing. Skip it if you need to prove to a client or boss that AI engines are citing your brand, because it measures page structure and does not monitor any AI engine. You paste a URL or copy, it runs a fixed set of pass/fail checks (the vendor says 34, mixing HTML inspection with AI yes/no questions) across structure, relevance, depth, trust and citability, then gives a 0-100 score and exact fix text. Plans differ only in how many analyses you get per month.

Best for

Good for content editors, technical SEOs, freelancers and small agencies who know which pages to fix and want a structured audit first. It is NOT for anyone who needs cross-engine citation tracking or ROI reporting (look at tracking platforms such as AthenaHQ or SurgeGraph), anyone who wants content written or published for them, or teams auditing hundreds of pages a month, since even the top plan caps at 200 analyses.

Key features

01

Pass/fail scoring engine

The vendor says 34 checks: 19 inspect the page HTML and 15 are AI yes/no judgments, rolled into a 0-100 score across five categories. Note the per-category counts shown on the homepage add up to 32.

02

Repeatable scores

Because most checks are rule-based, the vendor says unchanged content returns the same score, which makes before/after comparisons meaningful.

03

Fix suggestions

Each failed check comes with copy-paste text and where to put it.

04

Score history

Past results for each URL are kept on all plans, including Free.

05

Page-type awareness

Checks that do not apply to a page type are left out rather than counted as failures.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Most checks are rule-based, so a score is auditable and a before/after comparison after an edit is trustworthy, unlike opaque AI visibility scores.
  • The Free plan is a permanent 3 analyses a month with no card, enough to test accuracy on real pages before paying.
  • Category scores show which area (structure, depth, trust and so on) to fix first, shortening the path from score to edit.
  • Paid plans differ only by quota, so there is no feature gating to trip over; $29-79/month suits a solo consultant or small team.
  • Viewing an already-run report costs nothing, and checks that do not fit the page type are skipped instead of failing it.

Cons

  • No monitoring of whether ChatGPT, Perplexity or AI Overviews actually cite you, which is the metric many buyers care about most.
  • Each analysis is one URL, and re-running a URL after edits counts as a new analysis, so iterating on a page eats quota quickly.
  • Quotas do not roll over, so a site migration or big client backlog can exhaust even the 200/month Agency plan.
  • About 15 of the checks rely on an AI model, so page content is processed by a third-party AI provider; check this against confidentiality terms for unpublished client copy (I could not verify the vendor's data policy today).
  • The vendor itself says a good score does not predict citation, and the homepage's 34-check claim does not match its own per-category counts (32).

Frequently asked questions

Does a high score mean AI engines will cite my page?

No. The vendor says on-page score is not the main driver of citation; treat it as a quality floor.

What counts as one analysis?

One URL analysed. Re-analysing after changes counts again; viewing a report you already ran is free. Unused analyses do not roll over.

Do paid plans unlock more checks?

No. Per the pricing page, all plans include every check and suggestions; paid plans only add analyses and support level.

Can I cancel or downgrade any time?

Yes, from Plan & Billing. You keep your paid limit until the period ends, then move to Free. Payments are handled by Paddle as merchant of record.

Is it a replacement for an AI visibility tracker?

No. It grades pages at a point in time; it does not monitor brand mentions across AI engines.
