Kaiba positions itself not as a typical AI coding tool but as a "software capability platform" aimed at high-trust organizations undertaking legacy modernization and agentic transformation. Rather than selling a subscription product a developer signs up for directly, Kaiba operates through forward-deployed experts who work embedded with a client's own teams, using Kaiba's platform — comprising Cortex (a governed knowledge graph of the organization's code, documentation, and domain knowledge), Cloud (managed infrastructure for running agents), Campus (a workspace for building custom agents and skills), Core (the shared agent harness of models, policies, and patterns), and an in-development Console for decision auditing — to modernize legacy systems while building capability that the client retains, rather than becoming dependent on Kaiba as a vendor.

The company frames its approach around what it calls the Agentic Team Operating Model (ATOM): humans and AI agents functioning as one governed team, with four adjustable modes of oversight ranging from a human reviewing agent output at a system level down to a human doing the work directly with agent assistance. Delivery follows a six-stage process — scan, standardise, modernise, transform, sustain, evolve — designed so a client can pause after any stage and keep the value produced. Every agent action and decision is recorded to create an audit trail, a design choice aimed at regulated and compliance-sensitive organizations. Kaiba is a small, newer company with no public funding disclosures found and no listings yet on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot, so independent validation of client outcomes is limited to what the company publishes itself.

Best for

Kaiba fits large, compliance-conscious organizations with significant legacy technology estates who want a governed, audit-friendly approach to modernization delivered through embedded experts plus agentic tooling. It is a poor fit for individual developers, startups, or teams looking for an off-the-shelf coding assistant with transparent self-serve pricing.

Key features

01

Kaiba Cortex

Ingests an organization's codebases, knowledge bases, and people into a governed knowledge graph that serves as shared context for agents and experts.

02

Kaiba Cloud

Managed infrastructure that runs agent containers, communication, workflow engines, and session management behind the scenes.

03

Kaiba Campus

A workspace for building and customizing agents and skills specific to a client's domain, on top of Kaiba's core capabilities.

04

Kaiba Core

The shared agent harness — the models, policies, and behavioral patterns that power every agent deployed on the platform.

05

Kaiba Console (in development)

A governance layer for tracking decisions and records, intended to give end-to-end traceability of agent and human actions.

06

Four modes of oversight (HOTEL/HOTL/HITL/HATL)

Configurable human-oversight levels ranging from systemic evaluation review down to humans doing the work directly with agent assist.

07

Six-stage delivery methodology

Scan, standardise, modernise, transform, sustain, evolve — a staged engagement model where each phase is designed to deliver standalone value.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Places strong emphasis on governance and auditability — every agent decision is recorded — which is attractive to regulated, high-trust organizations that need traceability.
  • Combines embedded human expert judgement with agent execution instead of pursuing full automation, reducing the risk of ungoverned AI changes to critical legacy systems.
  • The staged six-phase methodology (scan through evolve) is designed so a client can stop after any phase and retain the value delivered, lowering commitment risk.
  • Aims to build capability the client owns going forward (via Cortex, Campus, and Core) rather than creating long-term vendor lock-in.
  • The four adjustable oversight modes give organizations flexibility in how much autonomy they grant agents based on risk tolerance.

Cons

  • No public pricing is available anywhere on the site; prospective buyers must go through a sales/demo process to learn cost, which slows evaluation.
  • As a very new company, Kaiba has no listed reviews on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot, so there is no independent third-party validation of outcomes or client satisfaction.
  • The platform is positioned as a high-touch, consulting-plus-software engagement rather than a self-serve developer tool, making it inaccessible to smaller teams or individual developers.
  • Several core components (notably Kaiba Console) are explicitly listed as still "in development," indicating parts of the platform are not yet fully built out.

Frequently asked questions

What does Kaiba actually do?

Kaiba delivers agentic transformation and legacy modernization services, combining embedded human experts with AI agents running on its own platform (Cortex, Cloud, Campus, Core) to modernize an organization's legacy systems.

How much does Kaiba cost?

Kaiba does not publish pricing. Engagements are scoped individually through a demo and consultation call.

Is Kaiba a self-serve software product?

No. Kaiba operates as a high-touch platform-plus-expert engagement model rather than a subscription tool a developer signs up for directly.

What is the Agentic Team Operating Model (ATOM)?

ATOM is Kaiba's methodology for combining humans and AI agents into one governed team, with four adjustable levels of human oversight depending on risk and trust.

Does Kaiba have independent reviews?

As of this writing, Kaiba has no listed presence on G2, Capterra, or Trustpilot, so there is no independent third-party review data available.

What industries is Kaiba built for?

Kaiba targets high-trust, often regulated organizations with large legacy technology estates that require governed, auditable modernization rather than fast, unsupervised automation.
