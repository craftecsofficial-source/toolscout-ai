Language I/O is great for mid-sized to large support organizations that need multilingual chat, tickets and email translated inside Salesforce, Zendesk, Oracle or ServiceNow. It is a poor fit for small teams on a tight budget, since published pricing starts at about $10,000 a year, and for anyone needing general document or marketing translation.

Rather than being a single translation engine, it routes each message to whichever machine translation model it judges best for that language pair, and keeps a glossary of your brand and product terms. It covers chat, email and tickets, chatbots, voice and knowledge-base articles in 150+ languages, and states a zero-data-retention policy for the models it uses.

Best for

Support and CX teams, especially in finance, healthcare, travel, gaming or government, that already use a supported CRM or helpdesk and have enough multilingual volume to justify an annual contract. It is NOT for small businesses, individuals, or teams wanting one-off document, legal or creative translation, and it is not a self-serve tool.

Key features

01

Smart Model Selection

Automatically routes each translation request to whichever underlying AI engine performs best for that language pair and context, with failover if one provider is unavailable.

02

Channel coverage

Translates live chat, support tickets, email, in-app messaging, knowledge base articles and browser content through one platform rather than separate point tools.

03

Self-improving glossary

Builds and refines a client-specific terminology database over time so brand names, product terms and industry jargon are translated consistently instead of literally.

04

Toxicity Shield

Flags or filters harmful or abusive language in translated conversations before it reaches agents or customers.

05

Native CRM/helpdesk integrations

Embeds directly into Salesforce Service Cloud, Zendesk, Oracle Service Cloud, ServiceNow and Intercom, plus a Chrome extension and developer API for custom builds.

06

Zero data retention

Customer conversation data is not stored or used for model training by the translation providers Language I/O routes through, which the company positions as important for regulated industries.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Native integrations with Salesforce, Zendesk, Oracle Service Cloud, ServiceNow and Intercom, plus a Chrome extension and developer API
  • Multi-engine routing and a self-improving glossary address inconsistent terminology in support conversations
  • States ISO 27001:2022 certification and zero data retention, relevant for regulated buyers
  • Covers chat, email, tickets, voice and knowledge-base content in one platform
  • Vendor says standard integration takes under a day

Cons

  • Annual commitments starting around $10,000 put it out of reach for small teams
  • Several tiers and all per-word rates are not public, so you cannot fully budget without a sales call
  • Built for support workflows; not suited to general document or marketing translation
  • Quality is bounded by the third-party engines it routes to, and vendor claims of up to 60% savings come with no stated baseline
  • Earlier material cited SOC 2 and HIPAA; the site checked stated ISO 27001 and HIPAA/GDPR safeguards, so confirm which certificates you need

Frequently asked questions

How much does Language I/O cost?

The pricing page shows starting points of $10,000/year (Pilot), $25,000/year (Economy) and $50,000/year (Business), plus usage. Premium, First Class and Enterprise are contact-sales. These are described as starting points, not quotes.

Which platforms does it integrate with?

Salesforce, Zendesk, Oracle Service Cloud, ServiceNow and Intercom, plus a Chrome extension and a machine translation API.

What languages and channels are covered?

More than 150 languages across voice, live chat, email and tickets, chatbots and knowledge-base articles. Quality varies by language pair.

How does it handle data privacy?

The vendor states a zero-data-retention policy, works only with translation models that follow it, and holds ISO 27001:2022 certification, with GDPR and HIPAA safeguards. Request current certificates during procurement.

Can I try it before committing?

The site says a demo is required to get started. A pilot tier exists, but trial terms were not published.
