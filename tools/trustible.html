Trustible is a good choice for compliance, legal and risk teams in regulated enterprises that need a system of record and audit trail for the AI they build and buy. Skip it if you are a small team, have no one to run governance reviews, or just want a lightweight checklist.

It keeps one inventory of AI use cases, models and vendors, runs intake and risk and impact assessments, maps controls to the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001, and monitors systems after launch. Its site lists customers such as Leidos, Mass General Brigham, Google and Databricks and cites a Gartner Peer Insights rating and Gartner Magic Quadrant mention; we did not verify those third-party claims.

Best for

AI governance leads, compliance officers and legal or risk teams at mid-size to large regulated organizations (financial services, healthcare, insurance, technology, government contractors) who must show regulators or auditors how AI is approved and monitored. It is not for individuals, startups without formal governance duties, or teams wanting a self-serve tool they can try today.

Key features

01

AI Intake & Triage

Provides a structured way to log new AI use cases, models, agents, and vendor tools as they're proposed, with automated routing based on assessed risk level.

02

Risk Scoring

Evaluates each AI use case or system against risk criteria and directs higher-risk items to expert human reviewers rather than treating every request the same way.

03

Continuous Monitoring

Tracks AI systems after they go live, watching for performance issues or external risk signals and surfacing governance alerts over time.

04

Regulatory Compliance Mapping

Aligns an organization's governance controls to named frameworks such as the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, and ISO 42001, reducing manual cross-referencing.

05

Centralized AI Inventory

Maintains a single source of truth listing every AI model, agent, and vendor tool in use across the organization.

06

Agentic Workflows with Human Oversight

Uses automation to speed up routine governance tasks while keeping human reviewers in the loop for judgment calls that require context.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • One workspace covers inventory, intake, risk assessment, compliance mapping and monitoring, replacing scattered spreadsheets
  • Named framework coverage (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001) reduces manual control mapping
  • Tracks third-party AI vendors as well as in-house models and agents
  • Risk-based routing sends higher-risk items to human reviewers instead of treating every request alike

Cons

  • No public pricing or self-serve trial; you must book a demo to learn cost
  • Likely overbuilt for small teams or organizations with light AI oversight needs
  • It supports a governance process but does not replace the staff needed to review and act on alerts
  • Depth versus larger GRC suites is hard to judge from public material
  • Customer logos and analyst mentions come from the vendor's own site

Frequently asked questions

How much does Trustible cost?

Not published. The site offers only a demo request, so pricing is quote-based. Ask what drives cost, such as number of AI systems, users or frameworks.

Which regulations and frameworks does it map to?

The site names the EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001, plus state, global and industry-specific frameworks. Ask for the current list for your jurisdiction.

Does it monitor AI after deployment?

Yes, it describes continuous monitoring of live systems. Ask what signals are monitored and how alerts reach your team.

Does it build or host AI models?

No. It is a governance layer over AI you already use or are evaluating.

Do we need a dedicated governance team?

Realistically yes; someone must own intake reviews and respond to risk routing. Without that, the tool adds little.
