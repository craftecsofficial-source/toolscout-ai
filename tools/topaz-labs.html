Topaz Labs is a Dallas, Texas-based software company that built its reputation on desktop AI plugins for photographers, most notably Gigapixel AI (image upscaling), DeNoise AI, and Sharpen AI, later consolidated into two flagship apps: Topaz Photo AI and Topaz Video AI. The company says it serves over a million customers and lists enterprise users such as Apple, Netflix, and NASA on its G2 profile. In late 2025, Topaz discontinued its long-standing perpetual license model entirely, moving its whole product line to subscriptions under a new 'Studio' umbrella that bundles the desktop apps with a newer set of cloud-based tools.

The current lineup splits into two groups: general-purpose enhancement apps (Photo AI, Video AI, Gigapixel AI) that fix blur, noise, low resolution, and compression artifacts in ordinary photos and footage, and newer cloud apps -- Astra, Bloom, and Mosaic -- aimed specifically at polishing AI-generated images and video, a growing need as more visual content originates from diffusion and generative video models rather than cameras. Astra upscales AI-generated video to 4K with scene-by-scene controls and batch rendering, while Bloom focuses on restoring believable material detail (skin pores, cloth weave, hair strands) that generative models often smooth over. Photo AI and Video AI use subject/background-aware analysis to apply different corrections to different regions of an image automatically, which is Topaz's core differentiator versus manual sharpening and denoising filters in general editors. The tools work standalone or as plugins inside Lightroom, Premiere Pro, and DaVinci Resolve.

Best for

Best suited to photographers, videographers, and post-production teams who regularly need to rescue underexposed, noisy, blurry, or low-resolution source material, and increasingly to studios that need to polish AI-generated video or images before publishing. It's a weaker fit for casual users who only occasionally need a quick upscale, since a one-off online tool would be cheaper, or for buyers who want a one-time purchase rather than an ongoing subscription, since Topaz no longer sells perpetual licenses.

Key features

01

Photo AI

Automatically detects and fixes noise, blur, and low-resolution issues in a photo, analyzing subject and background separately.

02

Gigapixel AI

Upscales images up to roughly 100MP while attempting to preserve or recover fine detail rather than just interpolating pixels.

03

Video AI

Upscales video resolution and adds frame interpolation, slow motion, and deinterlacing for footage repair.

04

Astra

A cloud upscaler built specifically for AI-generated video, offering precise or creative 4K upscaling with scene controls and batch rendering.

05

Bloom

A creative upscaler for diffusion-generated images that restores plausible micro-detail in skin, cloth weave, and hair that generative models tend to smooth over.

06

Batch processing

Applies a chosen set of corrections across an entire folder or library of photos/videos at once instead of one file at a time.

07

NLE/DAM plugin integrations

Works as a plugin inside Lightroom, Premiere Pro, and DaVinci Resolve so enhancement fits into an existing editing pipeline.

08

Regularly updated AI models

Topaz ships periodic model updates (e.g. its 'Realism' update) adding new enhancement models and refinements across the product line.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Automatic subject/background-aware correction removes most of the manual masking work other upscalers require, according to G2 reviewers.
  • Noise reduction is repeatedly singled out in reviews as removing grain 'without losing detail,' a common failure point in competing tools.
  • Astra and Bloom address a real gap -- cleaning up artifacts specific to AI-generated video and images -- that general photo editors don't target.
  • Broad plugin support means the tools slot into existing Lightroom, Premiere, and Resolve pipelines instead of requiring a separate standalone workflow.
  • Enterprise-scale customers cited on G2 (Apple, Netflix, NASA) suggest the underlying models hold up under professional, high-volume use.

Cons

  • The move to subscription-only in late 2025 (ending perpetual licenses) raised long-term cost for users who previously paid once plus a year of updates.
  • Reviewers note the software can be slow or buggy on weaker hardware, and it generally needs a capable GPU for smooth local rendering.
  • AI results can look over-processed or unnaturally smoothed if default settings aren't manually adjusted per image.
  • Frequent interface and model updates mean the learning curve resets somewhat with each major release.
  • Cloud credit limits on the Studio Personal tier (300 monthly video credits) can be restrictive for video-heavy workflows, pushing users toward the pricier Pro tier.

Frequently asked questions

Does Topaz Labs still offer a one-time purchase license?

No. As of October 2025, Topaz moved its entire lineup to subscriptions and no longer sells perpetual licenses; existing perpetual-license holders keep their software.

What's the difference between Photo AI, Gigapixel AI, and Video AI?

Photo AI focuses on general repair (noise, blur, exposure), Gigapixel AI specializes in upscaling resolution, and Video AI applies similar fixes plus frame interpolation to video.

What are Astra and Bloom for?

They're cloud tools aimed specifically at cleaning up AI-generated video (Astra) and AI-generated images (Bloom), restoring realistic detail that diffusion and generative video models often smooth away.

Can I use Topaz as a plugin inside other software?

Yes, the desktop apps integrate as plugins with Lightroom, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, and other NLEs/DAMs.

Is there a free trial?

Topaz offers free trials of its apps with watermarked or resolution-limited exports so you can test quality before subscribing.

Does Topaz require a powerful computer?

Local rendering is GPU-intensive; Topaz recommends a dedicated GPU for smooth performance, especially in Video AI, and performance can lag on older or integrated-graphics machines.
