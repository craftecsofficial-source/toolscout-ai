DiffusionBee is a good fit for Mac users who want free, private image generation and are happy with Stable Diffusion-era quality; Windows or Linux users, and anyone who wants the newest top-tier models, should skip it. It wraps Stable Diffusion in a desktop app with text-to-image, image-to-image, generative fill, upscaling, control images and custom model training, all running on your own hardware. The official site says it needs macOS 13.1 or later, recommends Apple silicon, and quotes about 30 seconds per image on an 8GB M1 MacBook Air. The site shows no version number or release date and a 2024 copyright line, so how actively it is updated could not be verified.

Best for

Good for Mac hobbyists, privacy-minded users and people who want to experiment with local AI art at no cost. Not for Windows or Linux users, Intel Mac owners who need speed, professionals who need the latest commercial-grade models, or people who want a large plugin and community-model ecosystem like ComfyUI offers.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Everything runs on-device, so prompts and results are not uploaded.
  • No per-image cost or credits, so you can iterate freely.
  • Simple desktop app rather than a Python environment.
  • Covers inpainting, upscaling and training in one place.

Cons

  • Mac-only; the site lists no Windows or Linux version.
  • Intel Macs are described as much slower.
  • Based on Stable Diffusion, so output quality trails current cloud models.
  • No release date or changelog on the homepage, so update cadence is unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Is DiffusionBee really free?

Yes, the official site describes it as completely free and lists no paid plans.

What Mac do I need?

macOS 13.1 or later. Apple silicon is recommended; Intel Macs work but are much slower.

Does it need internet?

The site says it runs offline, keeping prompts and images on your device.

Can I use the images commercially?

The site points to the CreativeML Open RAIL-M license. Read that license for your use case.
