R2 Copilot is a privacy-focused AI assistant designed to sit inside the tools professionals already use — primarily as an add-in for Microsoft Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and OneNote, alongside a web chat, a Telegram bot, and a Chrome extension. Its central pitch is straightforward: most AI assistants collect and retain user prompts, sometimes using them for further model training, and R2 Copilot is built to avoid that entirely. Content typed into R2 is encrypted on the user's device and in transit, and is immediately deleted when the add-in is closed, so conversations don't persist beyond the session. The company markets itself as the "first-ever privacy-conscious AI assistant" and lists a broad set of compliance claims — GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, PCI, ISO 27001, CSA STAR, and FedRAMP — aimed at industries where confidentiality is a hard requirement.

R2 Copilot's stated user base skews toward professions with real confidentiality stakes: lawyers handling pleadings and client communications, healthcare workers protecting patient data, compliance and infosec officers, bankers and insurers processing customer financial information, and executives managing sensitive internal documents. Functionally, it behaves like a general-purpose GPT-based writing and analysis assistant — drafting emails, analyzing datasets, generating first drafts, translating text — but the differentiator is entirely the privacy/security posture rather than unique AI capability. The correct live domain is r2copilot.ai (the r2copilot.com domain does not resolve), and the product is also listed on Microsoft's own AppSource-style marketplace as a Teams/Office add-in and on Product Hunt, where it has accumulated 18 reviews averaging a perfect 5.0 rating, with reviewers most often praising its Outlook/Word integration and encryption model.

Best for

R2 Copilot is best suited for lawyers, healthcare staff, compliance/InfoSec officers, bankers, and executives who need everyday AI help inside Outlook, Word, and Excel but are barred (by policy or by preference) from sending sensitive data to consumer AI tools that may retain or train on it. It's a weaker fit for users who want the most advanced model capabilities available, prefer a widely benchmarked tool with large third-party review volume, or need clear, upfront self-serve pricing.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Product Hunt reviewers give R2 Copilot a perfect 5.0/5 across 18 reviews, with the platform's own AI-generated review summary noting the most common praise is that it 'feels safer to use than other AI assistants' due to privacy and secure prompt handling.
  • Deep, multi-app Microsoft 365 integration (Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OneNote) means users get AI help without leaving their existing document and email workflows, unlike browser-only assistants.
  • The privacy mechanism is concrete and specific rather than a vague marketing claim: on-device encryption plus deletion on app exit is a clearly stated, testable behavior.
  • Reviewers specifically highlight ease of setup and fit into daily work — one Product Hunt reviewer with a compliance/InfoSec background said dashboards and analytics 'were a pleasant surprise' and that the tool delivered on its privacy promises where others 'crumble.'
  • Broad compliance claims (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, PCI, ISO 27001, FedRAMP) target exactly the regulated-industry buyers who most need an alternative to consumer AI chat tools.

Cons

  • The correct domain is r2copilot.ai; r2copilot.com does not resolve at all, which creates real risk of users landing on the wrong site or a parked/expired domain when searching casually.
  • Multiple Product Hunt reviewers, even positive ones, describe the interface as 'basic' and note the feature set is 'still developing,' with one calling it a fair trade-off for privacy but not yet feature-complete.
  • No G2 or Capterra listing with a meaningful review count was found, so buyers can't cross-reference enterprise-software review platforms the way they could for larger competitors.
  • Pricing is not transparently laid out on the public marketing pages; third-party marketplace listings show a starting price near $29/month and "3 membership packages," but exact current tiers require direct sign-up to confirm.
  • As a smaller, security-focused vendor, it likely trails major AI assistants (ChatGPT, Copilot, Claude) in raw model capability and update cadence, since its core value proposition is data handling rather than frontier AI performance.

Frequently asked questions

What is R2 Copilot?

R2 Copilot is a privacy-focused AI assistant, delivered mainly as a Microsoft 365 add-in, that encrypts user content on-device and deletes it when the app is closed, so prompts are never retained or used for model training.

Is r2copilot.com the right website?

No — the live, working domain is r2copilot.ai. The .com version does not resolve, so users should navigate directly to r2copilot.ai to avoid confusion.

Which apps does R2 Copilot integrate with?

It offers add-ins for Outlook, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, and OneNote, plus a web chat, a Telegram bot, and a Chrome extension.

How does R2 Copilot protect privacy?

Content typed into R2 is encrypted on the user's device and in transit, and is permanently deleted as soon as the add-in or session is closed; the company states it never uses customer data to train wider AI models.

What compliance standards does R2 Copilot claim?

The company states adherence to GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2, PCI, ISO 27001, CSA STAR Level 1, and FedRAMP, targeting regulated industries like legal, healthcare, banking, and government.

How much does R2 Copilot cost?

Exact current pricing isn't fully published on the marketing site; third-party marketplace listings reference paid plans starting around $29/month across multiple membership tiers, with a free trial available.
