Impact Analytics is best suited to mid-size and large retailers with complex SKU and store networks that want planning and pricing decisions automated. Small retailers or anyone wanting self-serve software with published prices should skip it. It is a retail AI vendor selling modules for pricing and promotions (PriceSmart, MarkSmart, PromoSmart and others), merchandising (PlanSmart, AssortSmart, SizeSmart), supply chain (DemandSmart, InventorySmart, SpaceSmart) and data tooling, with an agent layer on top that the company says has 250+ agents live in production.

Best for

Retailers, grocers and consumer brands with large assortments and store counts and existing data infrastructure. Not for small shops, DTC startups, buyers who need transparent self-serve pricing, or companies outside retail looking for a general supply-chain suite.

Key features

01

AI agents

The site lists agents for SKU planning, planning, assortment, pricing, markdown, promotion, base pricing, inventory and insights. The company claims 250+ are live in production; we could not verify this or how autonomous they are.

02

CortexEye

The insights agent, meant to explain what is driving retail KPIs.

03

Pricing and promotions

PriceSmart, MarkSmart, BaseSmart, TradeSmart and PromoSmart cover regular pricing, markdowns, and promotions.

04

Merchandising

PlanSmart, ItemSmart, AssortSmart, SizeSmart, StoreSmart and VisualSmart support planning, assortment, sizing and stores.

05

Supply chain

DemandSmart, SpaceSmart and InventorySmart cover forecasting, space and inventory.

06

Consulting

Services include Agentic AI, Data Engineering, Retail Analytics, Pricing War Room and Sizing as a Service.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Modules are built for retail problems like markdowns, size curves and assortment.
  • Named customers include Under Armour, Levi's, Dollar General, Coach and Ralph Lauren (logos on the vendor site).
  • Combines software with consulting, which helps teams lacking analytics staff.
  • The agent approach targets acting on decisions, not only producing reports.

Cons

  • No public pricing or plans at all.
  • Performance and ROI claims come from the vendor; we found no independent review source today.
  • Unclear from public pages how much is truly autonomous versus rules-driven automation.
  • Integration with POS/ERP systems, security certifications and timelines are not documented publicly.
  • The sheer number of module names makes it hard to know what you actually need.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Impact Analytics cost?

Pricing is not public — contact sales. We found no pricing page.

Which industries does it serve?

The site names retail (apparel, grocery, furniture, electronics), manufacturing, wholesale, consumer packaged goods and quick-service restaurants.

Is there a free trial?

Can I trust the 250+ agents claim?

It is the vendor's own figure. Ask for a pilot or reference customers to confirm what the agents do autonomously.
