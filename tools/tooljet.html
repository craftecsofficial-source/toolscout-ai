ToolJet is a strong choice for engineering and ops teams that want an open-source internal-tool builder they can self-host, and a weaker one for teams who need pixel-perfect design or a no-conversation, flat-price purchase at higher tiers. It combines a drag-and-drop UI builder, 100+ data connectors, a built-in PostgreSQL-backed database and AI generation that drafts screens and queries from a plain-English prompt. Developers can add JavaScript or Python logic. You can run it on ToolJet Cloud or on your own infrastructure, including Docker, Kubernetes and air-gapped setups on Enterprise. Workflow automation and the agent builder are listed as self-hosted only, and governance features such as SSO, audit logs and release management sit on the Team and Enterprise tiers.

Best for

Good for developers, IT and operations teams replacing spreadsheets and scripts with admin panels, approval flows and dashboards, especially where data must stay on your own infrastructure. Not for teams needing highly custom front-end design, consumer-facing apps, or very large end-user populations on cheap plans, since lower tiers cap end users at 50 to 100.

Key features

01

Visual app builder with AI

Drag-and-drop components, plus AI that turns a plain-English description into a draft app with components and queries.

02

100+ integrations

Connect to databases, REST and gRPC APIs and SaaS tools without custom backend code.

03

ToolJet Database

A no-code table editor built on PostgreSQL; the self-hosted Free plan includes basic usage.

04

Workflows and agent builder

Automate multi-step processes and build AI agents for tasks like triage; listed as available on self-hosted only.

05

ToolJet MCP

A plugin that lets coding agents build ToolJet apps under the connected developer's permissions.

06

Governance

SSO (SAML, OIDC, LDAP), role-based access, audit logs, Git sync and environments with rollback on paid tiers.

07

Flexible deployment

Use ToolJet Cloud, or self-host with Docker or Kubernetes; Enterprise adds air-gapped and multi-instance setups.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Open source, with a free self-hosted plan and the option to run on your own servers or air-gapped, which helps with compliance.
  • The 100+ connectors reduce the work of wiring up existing databases and SaaS tools.
  • AI generation gives a quick first draft, and you can drop into JavaScript or Python when the visual layer runs out.
  • Cloud pricing is public and tiered from $0 to $199 per builder, so small teams can start cheaply.

Cons

  • The free plans cap you at 2 builders, 50 end users and 10 apps, so real rollouts need a paid tier quickly.
  • SSO, audit logs, Git sync and white-labelling are not in place until the $199 Team tier.
  • Per-builder pricing means cost grows with the number of people building, not just using apps.
  • Third-party reviews we did not re-verify today mention a learning curve and slowness on heavy pages; treat that as a point to test in a trial, not a confirmed fact.
  • The Team plan purchase widget showed an inconsistent $249 monthly price, and the trial terms are unclear (no defined free trial; contact the team for paid-feature trials).

Frequently asked questions

Is ToolJet free and open source?

Yes, it is open source and the self-hosted Free plan costs $0, limited to 2 builders, 50 end users and 10 apps.

Can I deploy it on my own infrastructure?

Yes. Self-hosting options include Docker and Kubernetes, and air-gapped deployment is listed under Enterprise.

How is it priced?

Per builder per month: Cloud Basic $23, Pro $79, Team $199, with Enterprise custom. Annual billing saves 20%. Extra AI credits cost $1 per 100.

Do I need to code?

Not to start: you can build visually or generate an app with AI. JavaScript and Python are available for custom logic.

Is there a free trial?

There is a Free plan for evaluation. The pricing page has no clearly defined trial, though Team shows a Start Trial button on Cloud and the FAQ says to contact them for paid-feature trials.
