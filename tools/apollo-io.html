Apollo.io positions itself as an end-to-end sales platform, pairing one of the larger self-service B2B contact and company databases on the market with a full sales engagement suite. Instead of buying a data provider and a separate sequencing tool, teams can search and filter prospects by role, industry, technographic, and intent signals, then immediately add them into multi-channel outbound sequences combining email, calls, and LinkedIn tasks.

Apollo has expanded over time to include AI-assisted features such as lead scoring, buying-intent signals, and AI-written email copy, along with a built-in dialer and email sending infrastructure with deliverability safeguards. A Chrome extension lets reps enrich and prospect directly from LinkedIn or company websites, feeding data straight back into Apollo and connected CRMs.

Its pricing model, which includes a usable free tier and relatively affordable paid plans compared to enterprise-only competitors, has made it especially popular with startups, SMBs, and growing sales teams that need broad functionality without enterprise-level budgets.

Best for

Apollo.io is best suited to SMB and mid-market B2B sales teams that want a single, affordable platform covering both prospecting data and outbound engagement. It works well for startups and growing sales orgs that can't justify enterprise-priced point tools for data and sequencing separately. Larger enterprises with complex compliance or data governance needs may still prefer combining specialized tools instead.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Bundles a sizeable contact database with a full engagement suite, cutting down on the number of separate tools a sales team needs to buy
  • Pricing is significantly more accessible than many enterprise-focused competitors, with a genuinely useful free tier
  • Frequent product updates have added AI-assisted features like intent scoring and AI email writing at no major price jump
  • Chrome extension makes it easy for reps to prospect and enrich data in the flow of their existing browsing habits

Cons

  • Like most large B2B databases, contact accuracy varies by region and job level, occasionally requiring manual verification
  • Higher-value features such as advanced intent data and larger export limits are gated behind pricier plans
  • New users can find the sheer number of features and settings overwhelming without guided onboarding
  • Deliverability and sending limits require careful configuration to avoid domain reputation issues at high outbound volume

What reviewers say

Frequently asked questions

Does Apollo.io have a free plan?

Yes, Apollo offers a free tier with limited contact credits and sequencing features, suitable for individuals or very small teams.

How large is Apollo's contact database?

Apollo maintains one of the larger self-service B2B databases, commonly cited in the hundreds of millions of contacts, though exact figures change over time.

Can Apollo replace a separate dialer or email tool?

Yes, Apollo includes a built-in dialer and email sending infrastructure, so many teams use it instead of separate calling or sequencing tools.

Does Apollo integrate with Salesforce and HubSpot?

Yes, Apollo offers native integrations with major CRMs including Salesforce and HubSpot for syncing contacts and activity.

Is Apollo good for small teams or only larger companies?

Apollo is popular with both; its low-cost tiers and free plan make it especially attractive to startups and small sales teams.

How accurate is Apollo's contact data?

Data quality is generally solid but can vary by region and seniority level, so some teams supplement it with verification tools for critical outreach.
