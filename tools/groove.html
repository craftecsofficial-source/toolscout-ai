Groove was originally built as a standalone sales engagement platform and is now the engagement layer of Clari's broader Revenue AI suite, following Clari's acquisition of the company. Its core promise is reducing the administrative burden on sales reps: instead of manually logging calls, emails, and meetings into a CRM, Groove captures that activity automatically and feeds it into pipeline and forecasting workflows.

Beyond activity capture, Groove layers on AI features such as conversation intelligence for calls, suggested next steps, and flow-based cadences that reps can trigger or that run automatically based on CRM triggers. Because it sits on top of Salesforce (its primary CRM integration), Groove is most commonly deployed in mid-market and enterprise environments where Salesforce is already the system of record.

Groove differentiates itself from pure engagement tools like Outreach or Salesloft by its emphasis on bidirectional CRM sync and its integration into Clari's revenue forecasting and deal inspection tools, positioning it less as a standalone cadence tool and more as part of an end-to-end revenue operations stack.

Best for

Groove is aimed at mid-market and enterprise B2B sales organizations, particularly those already running Salesforce as their CRM. RevOps and sales leadership teams that need tight activity capture, forecasting accuracy, and coaching tools in one platform are the core buyers. It's less suited to small sales teams or startups looking for a simple, self-serve cadence tool, since pricing and setup assume a larger, more structured sales operation. Companies already using or considering Clari for forecasting get the most value from adopting Groove alongside it.

Salesforce-Native Workflow

Deep, two-way Salesforce integration keeps CRM data current without reps switching between tools.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Automatic, bidirectional Salesforce sync significantly cuts down on rep admin time compared to manually logging activity
  • Being part of the Clari suite gives revenue leaders end-to-end visibility from rep activity through to forecast accuracy
  • Conversation intelligence features provide coaching and deal-risk signals without needing a separate call-recording tool
  • Flow-based cadence automation supports complex, multi-touch enterprise sales motions
  • Well suited to large sales orgs with dedicated RevOps teams who can configure and maintain the system

Cons

  • No published pricing makes it hard for smaller teams to evaluate cost before engaging sales
  • Primarily built around Salesforce, so orgs on HubSpot or other CRMs get a weaker integration experience
  • Initial setup and administration require more RevOps involvement than lighter-weight engagement tools
  • Full value is realized mainly when paired with other Clari modules, increasing total cost of ownership
  • Some users report the interface and reporting are less modern than newer sales engagement entrants

Frequently asked questions

Is Groove the same company as Groove.co (a helpdesk tool)?

No, Groove the sales engagement platform (part of Clari) is a different product from Groove HQ, a customer support helpdesk tool; they share a name but serve different markets.

Does Groove require Salesforce?

Groove's deepest integration and value proposition center on Salesforce, though it does offer some support for other CRMs; Salesforce shops get the fullest feature set.

Is there a free trial?

Groove does not publicly advertise a self-serve free trial; prospective customers typically go through a sales demo and custom quote process.

How is Groove priced?

Pricing is custom and quote-based, typically structured per seat on an annual contract, and varies depending on which Clari modules are bundled in.

What's the difference between Groove and Clari?

Groove handles sales engagement (cadences, activity capture, call intelligence) while Clari focuses on revenue forecasting and pipeline inspection; the two are integrated into one suite.

Can Groove replace a dialer or conferencing tool?

Groove includes calling and meeting features but is often used alongside dedicated dialers or conferencing tools depending on an org's existing telephony setup.
