Cosine is a good fit for regulated organisations, such as banks, defence and telecom firms, that want a coding agent running in a private or air-gapped setup, particularly for legacy or niche-language code; solo developers who just want a cheap editor copilot should skip it. The company dropped its earlier Genie agent and now sells the Lumen family of code models (Scout and Outpost available, Sovereign marked coming soon) through a CLI, a desktop app and a cloud surface for delegated tasks. Pricing is credit based, from $19 a month, with private deployments quoted by sales. Its customer logos and benchmarks are self-published, and we could not verify them independently.

Best for

Enterprises with strict data-residency or security needs, and teams maintaining legacy or niche-language codebases. Not for hobbyists or small teams who want a flat-rate, widely reviewed IDE assistant, or anyone who needs the Sovereign model today since it is marked coming soon.

Key features

01

Lumen model family

Three specialist coding models -- Scout (fast, low-cost, on-device), Outpost (high-quality production implementation), and Sovereign (frontier-scale reasoning, coming soon) -- each targeting a different layer of the dev workflow.

02

Cosine CLI

A terminal-native interface with a local-to-remote execution model, external tool access via MCP, and multi-agent orchestration for complex changes.

03

Cosine Cloud

Lets teams run multiple coding tasks in parallel and collaborate across engineers, PMs, and stakeholders on large changes in a shared project.

04

Flexible security posture

Deployable as fully managed public cloud, a managed single-tenant private environment, or fully air-gapped inside a customer's own perimeter.

05

Niche and legacy language support

Models are post-trained specifically for enterprise and niche languages such as COBOL, Fortran, Verilog, Rust, and complex SQL, beyond mainstream web-stack languages.

06

Published benchmarking

Cosine publishes its own Niche-Bench, Vibe-Bench, and Slop-Bench results comparing cost-per-successful-task against models like GPT-5.4, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Kimi K2.6.

07

Red Team application

A dedicated application built on Lumen models for AI-assisted cybersecurity and red-teaming workflows.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Deployment choice from managed cloud to fully air-gapped is rare among coding agents and suits strict compliance needs.
  • Focus on legacy and niche languages (COBOL, Fortran, Verilog, complex SQL) targets a gap left by mainstream copilots.
  • Three surfaces (CLI, Desktop, Cloud) cover local work and delegated parallel tasks.
  • Add-on credit price falls as you move up tiers ($6.50 to $4.50 per 1M).

Cons

  • The Genie to Lumen change means older reviews and benchmarks describe a different product.
  • Credits cover agent work, model calls and cloud execution, and the page says usage depends on task size, model and runtime, so monthly cost is hard to predict.
  • Big price steps between tiers leave little middle ground for small teams.
  • Lumen Sovereign, the frontier-scale model, and the Training app are marked coming soon.
  • Customer logos and benchmark figures are published by Cosine; we did not verify them and found no independent review data.

Frequently asked questions

What happened to Genie?

Cosine now presents the Lumen model family (Scout, Outpost, and Sovereign marked coming soon) as its product. Treat older Genie coverage as describing a different offering.

Can it run air-gapped?

Yes, the site lists a fully air-gapped option that runs inside your own perimeter, plus managed public cloud and single-tenant cloud. Private deployments are handled through sales, and the site mentions minimum hardware of 4 H100 GPUs.

How much does it cost?

Starter from $19/month with 4M credits, Team $199/month with 47M, Enterprise $999/month with 240M. Extra credits cost $6.50, $5.00 and $4.50 per 1M respectively. Whether these are per seat is not stated, so confirm before buying.

How does it compare with Claude Code or Cursor?

Those are general-purpose tools with large user bases. Cosine competes mainly on private deployment and niche-language tuning, not on being the cheapest or most widely used.
