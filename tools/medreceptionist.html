MedReceptionist is great for small outpatient practices (medical, dental, chiropractic, therapy, wellness) that miss calls and want AI to book into their existing scheduling system. It is a poor choice for hospitals or large groups needing proven, independently reviewed call handling, and for anyone who wants third-party evidence of reliability before buying, since none was found.

It answers inbound calls around the clock, books, reschedules and cancels appointments in the connected scheduling software, texts patients confirmations and missed-call follow-ups, collects intake forms and payments, and warm-transfers urgent calls to staff. It is made by AI Scan Solutions and sold alongside a website product called MedSiteAI.

Best for

Independent or small-group outpatient practices that lose bookings to voicemail or lack after-hours cover, and use an EHR or practice system on the vendor's list. It is NOT for practices wanting independently verified performance data, for complex multi-location groups that need published pricing, or for anyone expecting an AI to make clinical judgments.

Key features

01

24/7 call answering

Answers inbound calls around the clock, including nights, weekends, and peak hours, aiming to eliminate voicemail overflow.

02

EHR-native scheduling

Books, reschedules, and cancels appointments directly inside the practice's calendar system rather than a separate task list, respecting provider-specific templates and buffers.

03

Multi-language support

Handles calls in 145+ languages with claimed automatic mid-call language switching, aimed at practices serving diverse patient populations.

04

SMS follow-up and missed-call rescue

Sends confirmations, reminders, and intake links via text, and automatically texts back callers who couldn't be reached live.

05

Emergency triage and routing

Applies practice-defined rules to detect urgent symptoms and warm-transfer calls to on-call staff or provide guidance protocols.

06

Call analytics dashboard

Surfaces call volume, resolution rates, and booking outcomes so front-office staff can track what the AI is handling versus escalating.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Flat per-practice price rather than per-provider, with usage allowances (500 minutes, 10,000 texts) stated up front
  • Wide list of named EHR/PMS integrations across medical, dental, chiropractic and allied health
  • Signed HIPAA BAA with every practice, included in the standard plan
  • Month-to-month with a 14-day trial lets you test against real call volume
  • Handles more than booking: SMS follow-up, intake forms and text-to-pay

Cons

  • No independent G2 or Capterra reviews were verified, so accuracy and support quality rest on vendor claims
  • Site statistics appear to be sample or placeholder numbers, so do not treat them as results
  • Overage of $0.10/minute can add up for busy practices; check the 500-minute allowance against your call volume
  • SOC 2 Type II is stated for the hosting infrastructure, not clearly for MedReceptionist itself; ask for audit evidence
  • Edge cases like unclear symptoms, heavy accents or niche terminology still risk mishandled routing
  • Language count differs between earlier material (145+) and the site checked (135); confirm your languages

Frequently asked questions

How much does MedReceptionist cost?

$349/month per practice including 500 voice minutes and 10,000 texts, then $0.10/minute and $0.02/text. It is $249/month with a MedSiteAI website plan and free with Practice Complete ($599/month) or higher. Multi-location pricing is not public.

Is it HIPAA compliant?

The vendor says it signs a BAA with every practice, uses AES-256 at rest and TLS 1.3 in transit. Its SOC 2 Type II claim refers to hosting infrastructure. These are vendor claims; ask for documentation.

Which EHR systems does it work with?

Listed systems include Epic (FHIR), athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, Oracle Health, Open Dental, Dentrix, Eaglesoft, ChiroTouch, Jane, WebPT, Cliniko and SimplePractice, plus FHIR R4 and HL7 for others. Confirm your exact system and version before trialing.

Is there a contract or trial?

There is a 14-day free trial and no contract; you can cancel month to month.

What is the main risk before buying?

No independent third-party reviews were found, so use the trial to listen to real calls and check booking accuracy in your own calendar.
