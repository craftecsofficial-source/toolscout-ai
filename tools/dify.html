Dify is a good fit for technical teams that want to build RAG apps, agents and multi-step LLM workflows visually, and want the choice of self-hosting. It is a weaker fit for non-technical people who want a one-click chatbot, or for teams that need a monthly-billed, per-seat plan. Dify provides a workflow canvas, a knowledge pipeline for ingesting and indexing documents, agents, and a plugin marketplace for models and tools. Finished apps can be published as hosted experiences, APIs, embeds or MCP-compatible tools, with logs and usage data. It is offered as managed Dify Cloud, a self-hosted Community Edition, and an Enterprise edition for your own cloud.

Best for

Who it is for: developers and technical product teams building RAG assistants, agents or internal AI tools, especially those wanting to self-host. Who it is not for: non-technical users wanting the simplest possible chatbot, teams unwilling to manage infrastructure who also dislike annual commitments, and anyone needing a strict OSI-approved open-source license.

Key features

01

Workflow Studio

A visual builder showing retrieval, decision, tool-use and human-input steps, which you can test and debug.

02

Agents

Create agents by chatting with the platform or configuring them manually; embed them in sites or use them as nodes inside workflows.

03

Knowledge Pipeline

Ingests files, websites, online documents and drives, then extracts, cleans, chunks and indexes the content, with retrieval testing before you connect it to an agent.

04

Marketplace and plugins

Model providers, tools, data sources and MCP integrations that can be reused across apps.

05

Publish and monitor

Ship apps as hosted pages, APIs, embeds or MCP tools and watch logs, feedback, latency and usage.

06

Enterprise controls

The Enterprise edition lists SSO/SAML, role-based access, audit logs, a Kubernetes Helm chart, and SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 compliance (per Dify's site).

Pros and cons

Pros

  • You can run it yourself with the free Community Edition and keep data on your own infrastructure.
  • One product covers workflows, agents, knowledge ingestion and publishing, so you stitch together fewer parts.
  • Message credits let you trial hosted models, and you can switch to your own API key once they run out.
  • Apps can be exposed as APIs, embeds or MCP tools, which helps when wiring into existing systems.
  • The free Sandbox and Community Edition allow a real evaluation before paying.

Cons

  • The Cloud Professional plan allows only 3 team members; collaboration at scale means the $1,590/year Team plan.
  • The public pricing page shows annual rates only; we could not verify a monthly option.
  • Sandbox is small: 200 message credits, 5 apps and 50 documents.
  • The Community Edition is labeled source-available with a derivative license, so check its terms before commercial or multi-tenant use.
  • Self-hosting means you handle upgrades, scaling and monitoring; multiple workspaces and SSO need Enterprise.

Frequently asked questions

Is Dify free?

The Community Edition is free to self-host, and Dify Cloud has a free Sandbox with 200 message credits. Professional ($590/workspace/year) and Team ($1,590/workspace/year) are paid.

Is Dify truly open source?

Dify's site calls the Community Edition source-available under an Apache-2.0-derivative license. Read the license in the repository before commercial use.

Do I need to pay for model usage separately?

Cloud plans include message credits for trying models from providers such as OpenAI and Anthropic. After they run out you can use your own API key, which is billed by the provider.

Can I pay monthly?

The pricing page we checked lists only annual rates for Professional and Team. Monthly billing could not be verified.

Can I get SSO and audit logs?

Those are listed under Dify Enterprise, which is custom-priced through sales.
