Hebbia is a strong fit for finance and legal teams at institutions that review large volumes of filings, transcripts and deal documents; individuals, small firms and anyone wanting to buy online without talking to sales should skip it. It is an AI platform whose main products are Matrix, a grid for running the same research questions across many companies or documents, and Max, an AI analyst aimed at finance work. It plugs into data providers such as SEC filings, FactSet, S&P Capital IQ and PitchBook, and into internal stores like SharePoint, Box and Snowflake. Its site targets four groups: investing, banking, legal and corporate finance.

Best for

Hebbia is for investment, banking, legal and corporate finance teams at larger organizations that analyze many documents or companies and have budget for an enterprise contract. It is not for individual analysts, students, small firms, or anyone who wants transparent self-serve pricing or a free trial. It is also not a general-purpose chatbot for non-finance document work.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Focused on finance workflows, so the integrations (filings, FactSet, Capital IQ, PitchBook) match what deal teams actually use.
  • Matrix lets a team apply the same questions across many companies consistently instead of reading one by one.
  • Connects to internal document stores such as SharePoint and Box, so firm data can be included.
  • Publicly names major banks, law firms and investors as customers, which suggests institutional adoption.

Cons

  • No published price or plans; you cannot estimate cost without a sales call.
  • No free trial is listed; evaluation means a demo request.
  • Clearly built for institutions; a solo analyst or small boutique is unlikely to be served or priced in.
  • Which data integrations you get, and at what cost, is unclear from public pages, so confirm during the demo.
  • AI answers on high-stakes deal work still need human checking against source documents.
