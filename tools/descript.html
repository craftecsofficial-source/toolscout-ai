Descript is a strong pick for podcasters, YouTubers, course creators and small marketing teams who work mostly with spoken-word recordings and want to cut them like a text document. Skip it if you need frame-accurate color grading, heavy multicam or effects work, in which case a traditional editor such as Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve fits better.

The app transcribes your audio or video and links the text to the timeline, so removing a sentence removes that footage. On top of that sit AI tools: Underlord (an AI agent that carries out edit requests and cuts clips), Studio Sound for noise removal, eye-contact and lighting fixes, filler-word removal, voice cloning, generated B-roll, captions and translation. Paid plans differ mainly in media hours, AI credits, export resolution and team size.

Best for

Good for podcasters, YouTubers, course creators and marketing teams who edit mostly talking content and want AI cleanup in the same app. Not for editors who need advanced color, complex multicam or effects pipelines, nor for anyone who only needs standalone text-to-speech narration, where a dedicated voice tool is a closer fit.

Key features

01

Text-based editing

Edit video or audio through its transcript; deleting a word cuts the matching moment and moving a sentence moves the footage.

02

Underlord AI agent

Takes instructions to turn a raw recording into a tighter cut, create clips and highlights, and draft supporting text. It consumes AI credits.

03

Studio Sound

One-click background-noise removal and voice enhancement.

04

Refine tools

Eye-contact correction, lighting fixes, jump-cut smoothing and filler-word removal.

05

Generated media and voice

Fill gaps with AI-generated B-roll, voice, music and images; paid plans include custom voice clones.

06

Translation and dubbing

Translate captions and dub into other languages; the pricing page lists translation and dubbing in 30+ languages with proofreading on Business.

07

Share and automate

Captions, platform reformatting, and an API/MCP integration are listed on the homepage.

08

Business and security controls

Team-wide Brand Studio on Business; SOC 2 Type II, SSO/SCIM and audit logs on Enterprise.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Text-based editing is a genuine time-saver for interviews, podcasts and talking-head video
  • Studio Sound, filler-word removal and eye contact are built in, replacing several separate tools
  • The Free plan needs no credit card and includes text-based editing, so you can test the workflow
  • Annual billing lowers each paid tier by roughly a third
  • Enterprise tier lists SOC 2 Type II, SSO/SCIM and AI-training opt-out

Cons

  • Prices are per person, and Creator and Business seats are billed separately, so team costs add up
  • AI credits (400 to 1,500 a month) and media hours are capped; heavy Underlord use can exhaust credits
  • Free plan is small: 1 hour of media a month, 720p, watermarked, and only the first 5 stock-library results
  • Web-link export is capped at 1 hour on Free and Hobbyist, 3 hours on higher tiers
  • Better suited to spoken-word content than effects-heavy edits; we did not verify performance on long 4K projects

Frequently asked questions

Is Descript free?

Yes. The Free plan needs no credit card and gives 1 hour of media a month, 100 one-time AI credits, 5 GB storage and 720p exports with a watermark.

How much does Descript cost?

Per person per month billed yearly: Hobbyist $16, Creator $24, Business $50. Monthly billing is $24, $35 and $65. Enterprise is custom.

What are media hours and AI credits?

Media hours cap how much audio and video you can transcribe each month per editor (10, 30 or 40 on paid tiers). AI credits (400, 800 or 1,500 a month) are spent on AI features; Descript's pricing page does not spell out every feature's cost, so check in-app.

Can Descript replace Premiere Pro?

For talking-head videos, podcasts and quick clips it can. For complex effects, color work or multicam, a traditional editor remains the better choice.

Does Descript clone voices?

Custom voice clones are listed as a feature from the Hobbyist plan up. It is a supporting tool for patching dialogue, not a dedicated voice-generation product.
