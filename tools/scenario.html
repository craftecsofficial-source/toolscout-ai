Scenario suits game studios and solo developers who need a consistent art style across many generated assets and who can supply their own reference art; skip it if you only want occasional one-off images, since credit plans and the learning curve are overkill for that. It started as a game-asset tool built around training custom models on your art, and now presents itself as a broader production platform for image, video, 3D and audio.

It lists 650+ models from 40+ providers (names include Google, OpenAI, ByteDance, Runway, Meshy), node-based workflows, a REST API with SDKs, and an experimental Blender plugin. 3D features include image-to-3D and rigging. On the paid side, custom model training starts at the Pro plan, and credits do not roll over. The site states SOC 2 Type II and that under enterprise terms nothing trains a model without written consent. We could not verify training image requirements, since the pages we checked did not describe them.

Best for

Good for indie and mid-size game studios, concept artists and teams that want one style applied consistently, plus developers who want API access to many models. Not for casual users wanting a few images, teams without reference art to train on, or buyers who want fixed predictable pricing, since usage is credit based.

Key features

01

Custom model training

Train private models on your own art to reproduce a style, character or product look; listed on Pro and above.

02

Large model catalogue

650+ models from 40+ providers across image, video, 3D and audio.

03

Workflows and agents

Node-based workflows publishable as apps, plus a node-building agent and an in-app assistant.

04

3D pipeline

Turn an image into a textured 3D model, rig it, and take it into a game engine; experimental Blender plugin.

05

API and MCP

REST API with TypeScript and Python SDKs, and an MCP server for Claude, Codex and Cursor.

06

Enterprise controls

SSO/SAML, SOC 2, audit trails, IP detection add-on, spending limits and model allowlists.

Pros and cons

Pros

  • Custom models keep style consistent across a growing asset library.
  • One workspace covers images, video, 3D and audio from many providers.
  • Paid plans include a full commercial license (per the pricing FAQ).
  • API, SDKs and MCP let you wire it into a pipeline.
  • Enterprise tier has SSO, audit logs and SOC 2 Type II.

Cons

  • Custom training is not on Starter; you need Pro ($45/mo) or higher.
  • Credits do not roll over, and costs can rise with heavy batch or training use.
  • Free tier is for personal and evaluation use only, so not usable for shipped work.
  • Breadth of models and workflows means a learning curve.
  • Value depends on having good reference art to train on.

Frequently asked questions

Can I train a custom model on the free or Starter plan?

The pricing page lists custom model training from the Pro plan upward.

Can I use output commercially?

Paid plans include a full commercial license; free-plan output is personal and evaluation use only.

Do credits roll over?

No, per the pricing FAQ. Extra credits can be bought as add-ons.

Is there an API?

Yes, a REST API with TypeScript and Python SDKs, plus an MCP server.

How many images do I need to train a model?

Could not be verified; the pages we checked did not state a requirement. Check Scenario's docs.
